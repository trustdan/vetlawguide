## 1. Evidentiary & Interpretive Canons (`38 C.F.R. §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.6, 4.7, 4.21`)

### A. Longitudinal Historical Context (`§§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.6` & *Schafrath v. Derwinski*)

Sections `4.1`, `4.2`, and `4.6` form an indivisible evidentiary triad (verbatim text in [`38-cfr-4.1-4.31-general-policy-snapshots.md`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.1-4.31-general-policy-snapshots.md)):
* **`§ 4.1` (*Essentials of evaluative rating*):** Percentage evaluations measure *"average impairment in earning capacity"* in civil occupations (`38 U.S.C. § 1155`), requiring accurate, fully descriptive examinations emphasizing limitation of activity, with **each disability viewed in relation to its entire history**.
* **`§ 4.2` (*Interpretation of examination reports*):** Adjudicators must interpret individual C&P examination reports *"in the light of the whole recorded history, reconciling the various reports into a consistent picture so that the current rating may accurately reflect the elements of disability present."* If an exam report lacks sufficient clinical detail for evaluation, **it is the mandatory duty of the rating official to return the report as inadequate**.
* **`§ 4.6` (*Evaluation of evidence*):** Every element in any way affecting the degree of disability must be considered; ignoring favorable medical or lay history in the C-file to rely exclusively on an isolated, unrepresentative "good day" C&P snapshot violates `§§ 4.1, 4.2, and 4.6` (*Schafrath v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 589, 594–95 (1991)`).

### B. Reasonable Doubt on Evaluation Tiers (`§ 4.3` & *Bufkin v. Collins*)

Under `38 C.F.R. § 4.3` (implementing `38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)`), whenever a reasonable doubt arises regarding the **degree of disability** after careful consideration of all procurable and assembled data, such doubt **will be resolved in favor of the claimant**.
* **Appellate Standard of Review (*Bufkin v. Collins*, `604 U.S. 369 (2025)`):** At the AOJ and the Board of Veterans' Appeals, `§ 4.3` / `§ 5107(b)` operates as a potent substantive tie-breaker whenever competing medical exams or symptom accounts stand in approximate balance. At the CAVC, however, *Bufkin* holds that the Board's approximate-balance determination is reviewed deferentially for **clear error** (`38 U.S.C. § 7261(b)(1)`), making inadequacy-of-reasons-or-bases challenges under `§ 4.2` / `§ 4.6` / `§ 7104(d)(1)` far higher-percentage CAVC remand vehicles than pure equipoise reweighing.
* **Absolute CUE Bar (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(d)(3)`):** By explicit regulation, VA's failure to apply the `§ 4.3` / `§ 5107(b)` benefit-of-the-doubt rule **can never constitute CUE**.

### C. `38 C.F.R. § 4.7` (*Higher of Two Evaluations*): Disjunctive vs. Conjunctive vs. Successive/Cumulative Criteria

`38 C.F.R. § 4.7` states: *"Where there is a question as to which of two evaluations shall be applied, the higher evaluation will be assigned if the disability picture more nearly approximates the criteria required for that rating. Otherwise, the lower rating will be assigned."* Together with `38 C.F.R. § 4.21` (confirming it is not expected in atypical instances that all cases will show every specified finding, so long as rating coordinates with functional impairment), `§ 4.7` is the primary weapon against hyper-literal "checklist" denials—**except** when blocked by a **successive/cumulative conjunctive rating structure**:

| Diagnostic Code Structure | Leading Precedents | How `38 C.F.R. § 4.7` Operates | Classic VASRD Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Successive / Cumulative Conjunctive Criteria (`"and"` cascading upward)** | ***Camacho v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 360, 366–67 (2007)`**; ***Middleton v. Shinseki*, `727 F.3d 1172, 1177–78 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`** | **`§ 4.7` CANNOT Bypass Missing Prongs:** Where each ascending tier explicitly incorporates *all* requirements of the preceding lower tier **plus** an additional criterion joined by `"and"`, the veteran **must satisfy every cumulative prong**. Failing a required lower-tier element (e.g., `"regulation of activities"`) forecloses `§ 4.7` even if the veteran has the higher tier's extra symptom! | **`DC 7913` Diabetes Mellitus:** `20%` (`insulin AND restricted diet`) -> `40%` (`insulin, restricted diet, AND regulation of activities`) -> `60%` (`40% criteria + episodes of ketoacidosis/hypoglycemic reactions`). Under *Middleton*, ketoacidosis hospitalizations *without* regulation of activities cannot yield `40%` or `60%` via `§ 4.7`! |
| **2. Variable / Non-Successive Multi-Symptom Criteria** | ***Tatum v. Shinseki*, `23 Vet. App. 152, 156 (2009)`** | **`§ 4.7` FULLY APPLIES:** Even when a tier lists multiple symptoms joined by `"and"`, if the rating tiers are **not** structured as rigid upward-cascading cumulative steps, the Board **must apply `§ 4.7`** to decide whether the veteran's overall severity more nearly approximates the higher tier despite lacking one listed symptom! | **Pre-2017 `DC 7903` Hypothyroidism** (*Tatum*: `10%` vs. `30%` tiers listed distinct symptom clusters rather than incorporating lower tiers verbatim, requiring a `§ 4.7` approximation analysis); **Pre-May 2024 `DC 7346` Hiatal Hernia/GERD** (`30%` vs. `60%`). |
| **3. Exemplar / Holistic Occupational-Impairment Formulas (`"such symptoms as"` / `"or"`)** | ***Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki*, `713 F.3d 112, 116–18 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`**; ***Bankhead v. Shulkin*, `29 Vet. App. 10, 21–23 (2017)`** | **`§ 4.7` & Symptom-Driven Holistic Assessment:** Listed symptoms are **non-exhaustive examples** demonstrating the frequency, type, and severity of occupational/social impairment. Presence of a higher-tier hallmark symptom (e.g., passive suicidal ideation at `70%` under *Bankhead*—requiring no plan, intent, or hospitalization!) compels rigorous `§ 4.7` analysis of overall impairment. | **`§ 4.130` General Rating Formula for Mental Disorders (`DC 9400–9411`)** ([`mental-health-dc-9400-9411.md`](../vasrd/mental-health-dc-9400-9411.md)). |

## 2. Functional Impairment & The Ameliorative Medication Doctrine (`38 C.F.R. § 4.10`)

Under `38 C.F.R. § 4.10`, the basis of disability evaluations is *"the ability of the body as a whole, or of the psyche, or of a system or organ of the body to function under the ordinary conditions of life, including employment."*

### A. *Jones v. Shinseki* (`2012`) & *Ingram v. Collins* (`2025`): Prohibition on Uncodified Medication Discounting

For decades, VA examiners and Board members routinely depressed orthopedic, gastrointestinal, neurological, and dermatological evaluations by measuring range of motion or symptom frequency *while the veteran was heavily medicated* without accounting for baseline unmedicated severity:
1. ***Jones v. Shinseki*, `26 Vet. App. 56, 61–63 (2012)`:** Analyzing `DC 7319` Irritable Bowel Syndrome, the CAVC held that **unless a Diagnostic Code specifically contemplates the ameliorative effects of medication in its rating criteria, VA may NOT deny a higher disability evaluation by relying on relief provided by medication**—because doing so impermissibly adds uncodified criteria to the Rating Schedule.
2. ***Ingram v. Collins*, `38 Vet. App. 130 (2025)`** *(Federal Circuit appeal `No. 25-1972` voluntarily dismissed Mar. 30, 2026)*: Extending *Jones* squarely to **musculoskeletal range-of-motion and functional-loss evaluations (`38 C.F.R. §§ 4.10, 4.40, 4.45, 4.59, 4.71a`)**, the CAVC held that because goniometric joint/spine Diagnostic Codes do not mention medication, **C&P examiners and adjudicators MUST ascertain and discount the ameliorative/masking effects of pain or anti-inflammatory medications** so the veteran is compensated for baseline functional impairment ([`orthopedic-spine-joints.md`](../vasrd/orthopedic-spine-joints.md)).
   * **Contrast Explicit Medication DCs:** Where a Diagnostic Code *explicitly* builds medication into its text—such as `DC 6602` Asthma (intermittent vs. daily inhalational bronchodilators/corticosteroids), `DC 7101` Hypertension (`10%` continuous medication), `DC 7913` Diabetes (insulin/oral hypoglycemics), or `§ 4.124a` Epilepsy—the specific DC criteria govern.

### B. Quarantine Alert: The Rescinded 10-Day `§ 4.10` Interim Final Rule (`Feb. 17–27, 2026`)

> [!CAUTION]
> **Quarantined & Rescinded Agency Rulemaking (`91 FR 7118` -> Rescinded at `91 FR 9712`):**

> * Following immediate congressional and veterans-bar backlash, VA **formally rescinded the Interim Final Rule ten days later on February 27, 2026 (`91 FR 9712`)** and voluntarily dismissed its appeal in *Ingram* (`No. 25-1972`, Mar. 30, 2026) ([Authority Verification Log](../../docs/authority-verification-log.md)).

Conversely, **flag every RO, HLR, or Board decision issued between February 17, 2026 and February 27, 2026** (or relying on a DBQ completed under temporary Feb. 2026 M21-1 guidance) for immediate **Higher-Level Review, Supplemental Claim, or CAVC remand** under *Ingram*!

McDonough*, `596 U.S. 740 (2022)` (`open-claim-only-george-restricted`), whereas *Ingram* governs **100% of open, pending, timely-appealed, `§ 3.156(b)–(c)`, and new increased-rating claims**!

## 3. Change of Diagnosis (`§ 4.13`) & Avoidance of Pyramiding (`§ 4.14`)

### A. `38 C.F.R. § 4.13` (*Effect of Change of Diagnosis*) & *Read v. Shinseki*

When VA changes a previously assigned diagnosis, `§ 4.13` requires certifying whether the new diagnosis reflects **(1) progression of an earlier diagnosis**, **(2) development of a new and separate disease**, or **(3) correction of a prior diagnostic error** (where prior factual findings are reviewed as a whole).
* ***Read v. Shinseki*, `651 F.3d 1296, 1300–02 (Fed. Cir. 2011)`:** When VA reclassifies the Diagnostic Code of a disability in force for `10+` years under `§ 4.13` to reflect an updated/corrected diagnosis of the *same underlying symptom complex* **while maintaining service connection**, that diagnostic reclassification is **NOT a severance of service connection** violating `38 U.S.C. § 1159` / `38 C.F.R. § 3.957` (though any reduction in percentage must still satisfy `§§ 3.105(e)` and `3.344`—see [Playbook 05](../../playbooks/05-void-rating-reductions-stabilized-ratings.md)!).

### B. `38 C.F.R. § 4.14` (*Avoidance of Pyramiding*): Symptom Overlap vs. Etiology

`38 C.F.R. § 4.14` commands: *"The evaluation of the same disability under various diagnoses is to be avoided ... The evaluation of the same manifestation under different diagnoses is also to be avoided."* Few VASRD provisions are more frequently misapplied by RO raters to suppress lawful separate ratings:

1. ***Brady v. Brown*, `4 Vet. App. 203, 206 (1993)` & *Amberman v. Shinseki*, `570 F.3d 1377, 1380–81 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`:** The anti-pyramiding rule bars compensating a veteran twice for the **same functional manifestation or symptomatology**—even if doctors attach two separate diagnostic labels (e.g., PTSD + Major Depressive Disorder sharing identical occupational/affective symptoms under `§ 4.130`).
2. ***Esteban v. Brown*, `6 Vet. App. 259, 261–62 (1994)` (The Symptom-Overlap Test — NOT an Etiology Test!):** Reversing the Board's refusal to assign three separate ratings for a single facial trauma injury (`DC 7800` disfiguring scar, `DC 7804` painful scar, and `DC 5325` facial muscle injury), the CAVC established the bedrock rule of `§ 4.14`: **conditions sharing a common etiology or single injury MUST be rated separately whenever none of the symptomatology justifying any one evaluation is duplicative of or overlapping with the symptomatology of the others**!
   * **High-Yield Separate-Rating Multipliers (`cue-and-open-claim` when omitted on undisputed C&P facts):**
     * **Gunshot / Shrapnel Wounds:** Muscle injury (`DC 5301–5323`) + painful/unstable/areal scar (`DC 7801–7804`) + peripheral nerve damage (`DC 8510–8730`) ([Playbook 01](../../playbooks/01-penetrating-wounds-scars-vs-muscles.md), [`scars-dc-7800-7805.md`](../vasrd/scars-dc-7800-7805.md), [`muscle-injuries-dc-5301-5323.md`](../vasrd/muscle-injuries-dc-5301-5323.md)).
     * **Single Knee Triple/Quadruple Stacking:** Lateral instability (`DC 5257`) + limitation of flexion (`DC 5260`) + limitation of extension (`DC 5261`) + symptomatic meniscal tear (`DC 5258`/`5259` where distinct locking/effusion symptoms exist) ([`VAOPGCPREC 23-97`, `9-98`, `9-2004`](../../docs/vaopgcprec-index.md); *Lyles v. Shulkin*, `29 Vet. App. 107 (2017)`).
     * **Spine + Radiculopathy + Bladder/Bowel:** Orthopedic ROM (`DC 5235–5243`) + each affected lower/upper peripheral nerve (`DC 8520` sciatic, `DC 8526` femoral) + neurogenic bladder/bowel (`§ 4.71a` Spine Note 1).
3. ***Morgan v. Wilkie*, `31 Vet. App. 162, 166–68 (2019)` (Actual Compensated Symptoms & Duty to Maximize):** Anti-pyramiding under `§ 4.14` applies **only to *actual* symptoms already compensated** under an assigned rating—**never** to uncompensated manifestations or hypothetical criteria listed in a Diagnostic Code that the veteran was not rated for! Under *Morgan*, VA's duty to maximize benefits requires adjudicators to **exhaust every non-pyramiding separate schedular Diagnostic Code** (`§ 4.14`, `§ 4.20`, `§ 4.7`) before reaching `§ 3.321(b)(1)` extraschedular analysis.

## 4. Total Disability, Age, Analogy & Code-Sheet Math (`38 C.F.R. §§ 4.15–4.27, 4.31`)

### A. Total Disability, Age Prohibition & Aggravation Deductions (`§§ 4.15–4.19, 4.22`)

* **`§ 4.15` (*Total disability ratings*) & `§ 4.16` (*TDIU*):** Distinguishes general/schedular `100%` impairment (measuring average impairment across civil occupations) from individualized unemployability (`§ 4.16(a)–(b)`), taking into account `§ 4.18` static-trauma employment failures and `§ 4.17a` (which prevents a coexisting willful-misconduct disability from blocking P&T/TDIU if innocently acquired disabilities independently meet total criteria). *(For complete `§ 4.16(a)(1)–(5)` single-disability aggregation CUE rules and `1975–1996` historical text, see [`tdiu-digestive-respiratory-diffs.md`](tdiu-digestive-respiratory-diffs.md) and [`38-cfr-4.16-tdiu-evolution.md`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.16-tdiu-evolution.md)).*
* **`§ 4.19` (*Age in service-connected claims* — Categorical Bar):** *"Age may not be considered as a factor in evaluating service-connected disability; and unemployability, in service-connected claims, associated with advancing age or intercurrent disability, may not be used as a basis for a total disability rating."* Conversely, VA **cannot deny TDIU or discount an SC rating** by claiming a 72-year-old veteran is retired or unemployable due to advanced age if SC disabilities alone preclude substantially gainful employment ([`VAOPGCPREC 75-91`](../../docs/vaopgcprec-index.md))!
* **`§ 4.22` (*Rating of disabilities aggravated by active service*):** Mirrors `38 U.S.C. § 1153` (`former § 353`): VA may deduct the pre-service entrance degree of disability **ONLY if**: `(1)` the entrance severity is **affirmatively ascertainable** in terms of the Rating Schedule (if unascertainable, **zero deduction** may be made!), **AND** `(2)` the current post-aggravation disability is **less than `100%`** (**if the disability reaches `100%` total, ZERO deduction is permitted**!).

### B. `38 C.F.R. §§ 4.20 & 4.27`: Analogous Ratings (*Lendenmann* & *Webb v. McDonough*)

When a veteran's service-connected disease or injury is unlisted in the VASRD, **`38 C.F.R. § 4.20`** forbids conjectural or unsubstantiated analogies (`"Conjectural analogies will be avoided, as will the use of analogous ratings for conditions of doubtful diagnosis, or for those not fully supported by clinical and laboratory findings"`) and instead mandates rating under a **"closely related disease or injury"** in which not only the **functions affected**, but the **anatomical localization** and **symptomatology** are closely analogous. Under **`38 C.F.R. § 4.27`**, unlisted analogous evaluations are formatted on the Rating Code Sheet as hyphenated `xx99-xxxx` codes (e.g., `7399-7346` or `8599-8520` — see [VA Forms & Code-Sheet Lexicon](../../docs/forms-and-codesheet-lexicon.md)).

1. ***Lendenmann v. Principi*, `3 Vet. App. 345, 350–51 (1992)`:** Codified the mandatory **three-factor test** requiring the Board to compare competing candidate Diagnostic Codes across **(1) functions affected, (2) anatomical localization, and (3) symptomatology**, providing written reasons or bases why the chosen code is the closest match.
2. ***Webb v. McDonough*, `71 F.4th 1377, 1381–83 (Fed. Cir. 2023)` (Eliminating the Literal-Prerequisite Trap!):**
   * **The VA Error:** VA rated a veteran's medication-induced erectile dysfunction by analogy to `DC 7522` (`7599-7522`, pre-2021 text providing `20%` for *"Penis, deformity, with loss of erectile power"*) at `0%`, claiming the veteran could not receive `20%` because he lacked physical *penile deformity*.
   * **The Federal Circuit Holding:** Vacating the CAVC, the Federal Circuit held that **requiring an unlisted disability rated by analogy under `§ 4.20` to strictly satisfy every literal diagnostic or anatomical prerequisite of the listed Diagnostic Code renders analogous rating illusory**! Once a listed DC is selected as closely related under `§ 4.20`, the unlisted condition need only approximate the functional/symptomatic impairment of that tier, **not** extraneous anatomical preconditions inherent only to the named disease. *(For operational code-selection strategy, see [Playbook 19, Track B](../../playbooks/19-extraschedular-3.321b1-analogous-4.20-and-temp-total.md)).*

### C. `38 C.F.R. § 4.25` Combined Ratings Table & `§ 4.26` Bilateral Factor (Including the Apr. 16, 2023 `§ 4.26(d)` Rule)

VA never adds disability percentages arithmetically (`50% + 30% != 80%`). Under **`38 C.F.R. § 4.25`** (*Table I*), ratings are combined sequentially from **highest to lowest** based on **continuing whole-person efficiency**:

$$\text{Combined Raw Rating after Disability } i = C_{i-1} + \text{round}\!\left(R_i \times \frac{100 - C_{i-1}}{100}\right)$$

1. **Sequential Whole-Person Lookup (`§ 4.25(a)`):** Start at `100%` efficiency. A `50%` disability leaves `50%` efficiency. A second `30%` disability impairs `30%` of the remaining `50%` efficiency (`15%`), yielding **`50 + 15 = 65%`** raw combined value (note: in Table I, each pairwise step rounds to the nearest whole integer before combining the next disability!).
2. **Final `10%` Rounding Rule (`§ 4.25(a)`):** After *all* disabilities are combined, convert the final exact integer (`0–99`) to the nearest multiple of `10`: values ending in **`1` through `4` round DOWN** (e.g., `64% -> 60%`, `94% -> 90%`), while values ending in **`5` through `9` round UP** (`65% -> 70%`, **`95% -> 100%`**!).
3. **Mandatory `10%` Bilateral Factor (`38 C.F.R. § 4.26(a)–(c)`):**
   * **Trigger:** Compensable (`>= 10%`) service-connected disabilities affecting **both upper extremities**, **both lower extremities**, OR **paired skeletal muscles** (`§ 4.26(a)`). *(Upper and lower extremities are distinct pairs; spine trunk ROM `DC 5235–5243` is NOT an extremity disability, whereas bilateral sciatic radiculopathy `DC 8520` IS!)*
   * **Order-of-Operations Rule (`§ 4.26(c)` — CUE Trap!):** Bilateral disabilities **must be combined FIRST** in descending order via Table I; then **exact `10%` of that combined bilateral value** is added (before `10s` rounding, rounded to the nearest integer—e.g., `30%` R knee + `20%` L ankle = `44%` + `4.4` bilateral factor = `48.4 -> 48%`), and that **`48%` single composite unit** is then sorted and combined in descending order with all non-bilateral disabilities!
4. **Historical Evolution — The April 16, 2023 `§ 4.26(d)` Bilateral Exclusion Rule (`88 FR 22914`):**
   * **The `94% -> 90%` Rounding Paradox:** Because Table I rounds intermediate integers at each step, adding a `10%` bilateral factor can shift an intermediate number so that the final raw score lands at **`94%` (`-> 90%`)** whereas combining the exact same disabilities *without* the bilateral factor lands at **`95%` (`-> 100%`)**!
   * **Worked Mathematical Proof (`88 FR 22914` Preamble Archetype):** Suppose a veteran's non-bilateral disabilities (`80%` Heart + `50%` PTSD + `30%` Sleep Apnea) combine to **`93%` raw**, plus paired bilateral lower-extremity ratings of **`10%` Right Knee** and **`10%` Left Knee**:
     * **WITH Bilateral Factor (Pre-Apr. 16, 2023):** `10%` R + `10%` L = `19%` + `1.9` (`10%` bonus) = **`21%`** bilateral unit. Combining `93%` with `21%`: $93 + (21 \times 0.07) = 93 + 1.47 = 94.47 \rightarrow \mathbf{94\% \rightarrow 90\%}$!
     * **WITHOUT Bilateral Factor (`§ 4.26(d)` Exclusion):** Combine `93%` + `10%` R = $93 + 0.7 = 93.7 \rightarrow 94\%$; then `94%` + `10%` L = $94 + 0.6 = 94.6 \rightarrow \mathbf{95\% \rightarrow 100\%}$!
   * **Bitemporal Effective-Date Gate (`88 FR 22914`, published Apr. 14, 2023, effective April 16, 2023):** Enacted **`38 C.F.R. § 4.26(d)`**, mandating exclusion of bilateral disabilities from the bilateral factor whenever inclusion lowers the combined rating. **Prospective liberalizing rule (`38 C.F.R. § 3.114(a)`) effective April 16, 2023**—cannot support CUE prior to April 16, 2023, but **IS mathematical CUE** if omitted on or after April 16, 2023!

### D. `38 C.F.R. § 4.31` (*Zero Percent Evaluations*)

Whenever a Diagnostic Code omits an explicit `0%` tier (for example, `DC 8100` Migraines prior to `0%` or modern `DC 7319` IBS at `10%/20%/30%`, `DC 6260` Tinnitus at `10%`, or `DC 5276` Pes Planus), **`§ 4.31` mandates assigning a `0%` service-connected evaluation** whenever service connection is established (`current diagnosed disability + in-service incurrence + nexus`) even if compensable (`10%+`) severity criteria are unmet. Denying service connection outright for lack of `10%` severity violates `§ 4.31` (`cue-and-open-claim`).
