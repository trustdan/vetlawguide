## 2. Organic Diseases of the Central Nervous System (`DC 8000–8046`, `§§ 4.120, 4.124a`)

### A. Foundational `§§ 4.120 & 4.124a` Residual-Stacking Rule vs. Minimum Floors
Under `38 C.F.R. §§ 4.120` and `4.124a`, organic CNS diseases (`DC 8000–8046`) follow a **two-step evaluation architecture**:
1. **Separate Residual Evaluation:** Specific neurological residuals—loss of use or partial paralysis of extremities (`DC 8510–8530`), cranial nerve palsies (`DC 8205–8212`), impairment of vision/diplopia, hearing/vestibular vertigo, speech/aphonia (`DC 6519`), swallowing, anal/bladder sphincter control (`DC 7332` / `DC 7512`), or neurocognitive/mood disorders (`§ 4.130`)—are rated **separately** under their respective body-system diagnostic codes, plus applicable **`38 U.S.C. § 1114(k)–(s)` Special Monthly Compensation (`SMC`)**.
2. **Minimum Rating Floor Comparison:** Each organic CNS code specifies a **minimum percentage floor** (`10%`, `30%`, `60%`, or `100%`) when ascertainable residuals exist (or upon diagnosis alone for `DC 8004` and `DC 8017`).

## 3. Traumatic Brain Injury (`DC 8045`): Two-Era Historical Diff (`1946` vs. `Oct. 23, 2008`)

See [`38-cfr-4.124a-tbi-and-peripheral-nerves-evolution.md`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.124a-tbi-and-peripheral-nerves-evolution.md) for the verbatim pre- and post-October 23, 2008 regulatory text and complete 10-facet matrix.

| Dimension | Era 1: April 1, 1946 – October 22, 2008 *(Legacy `DC 8045`)* | Era 2: October 23, 2008 – Present *(`73 FR 54693`, pub. Sept. 23, 2008; `73 FR 69554`; `79 FR 45093`)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Architecture** | Split strictly between **(1) Purely neurological disabilities** (hemiplegia, cranial nerve paralysis, epilepsies) vs. **(2) Purely subjective symptomatology** (`DC 8045-9304`). | Evaluates **three main areas of dysfunction**: **(1) Cognitive**, **(2) Emotional/Behavioral**, and **(3) Physical** (including subjective symptoms). |
| **Cognitive & Subjective Rating Ceiling** | **Hard `10%` Ceiling Trap:** Purely subjective complaints recognized as symptomatic of brain trauma (headaches, dizziness, insomnia, memory/concentration complaints) were **capped at `10%` under `DC 9304`**, and **could NOT be combined** with any other brain-trauma rating in the absence of a diagnosis of multi-infarct dementia! | **`10-Facet` Table (`0% / 10% / 40% / 70% / 100%`):** Evaluates cognitive impairment and subjective symptoms across **10 facets** scored `0`, `1`, `2`, `3`, or `Total`. **Highest-Facet Rule:** The veteran's `DC 8045` percentage equals the **highest level assigned on ANY single facet** (`0 = 0%`, `1 = 10%`, `2 = 40%`, `3 = 70%`, `Total = 100%`)—never an average or sum! |
| **The 10 Evaluated Facets** | None (unstructured `DC 9304` dementia schedule if organic dementia proven; otherwise `10%` max). | `1.` Memory, attention, concentration, executive functions (`0–Total`)<br/>`2.` Judgment (`0–Total`)<br/>`3.` Social interaction *(caps at Level `3 = 70%` via `0–3`)*<br/>`4.` Orientation (`0–Total`)<br/>`5.` Motor activity (with intact motor/sensory system — apraxia/initiation) (`0–Total`)<br/>`6.` Visual-spatial orientation (`0–Total`)<br/>`7.` Subjective symptoms *(cluster of headaches/dizziness/fatigue/malaise not otherwise separately rated; caps at Level `2 = 40%` via `0–2`)*<br/>`8.` Neurobehavioral effects *(irritability, impulsivity, unpredictability, aggression; caps at Level `3 = 70%` via `0–3`)*<br/>`9.` Communication *(comprehension/expression; `0–Total`: `0/1/2/3/Total`)*<br/>`10.` Consciousness *(persistent vegetative state or coma = **`Total (100%)`** only)*. |
| **Co-Existing Physical, Mental & Migraine Residuals** | **Crucial Era-1 CUE Loophole:** Separately diagnosed *neurological or physical conditions*—such as **Migraines (`8045-8100` up to `50%`)**, **Peripheral Vestibular Disorder (`8045-6204` up to `30%`)**, **Post-Traumatic Epilepsy (`8045-8910`–`8914`)**, cranial nerve palsies, or comorbid PTSD (`DC 9411`)—were **NOT** "purely subjective complaints" and had to be rated under their dedicated codes! | **Physical/Neurological + Note (1) Overlap Rule:** Distinct physical/neurological diagnoses (migraines `DC 8100`, seizures `DC 8910–8914`, tinnitus `DC 6260`, vertigo `DC 6204`, diplopia `DC 6090`, endocrine `§ 4.119`, skin/scars `§ 4.118`, bowel/bladder, ED `SMC(k)`) are **rated separately** unless the exact same symptom is already used to elevate a `DC 8045` facet (`§ 4.14`). Under **Note (1)**, comorbid mental disorders (`§ 4.130`) are rated separately *unless* manifestations cannot be delineated without speculation (*Mittleider v. West*, `11 Vet. App. 181 (1998)`), in which case VA assigns a single combined evaluation under whichever schedule yields the higher rating ([Playbook 18](../../playbooks/18-tbi-facets-radiculopathy-and-nerve-stacking.md)). |
| **Governing Notes (2)–(5)** | N/A | • **Note (2):** Listed facet symptoms are *illustrative examples*, NOT mandatory cumulative checklists.<br/>• **Note (3):** Distinguishes basic `ADLs` from `IADLs` (finances, shopping, meal prep, medication management, travel).<br/>• **Note (4):** Initial in-service TBI severity label (*"mild"*, *"moderate"*, *"severe"* based on acute Glasgow Coma Scale / LOC / PTA) **does NOT cap or control** current residual ratings *(though initial severity DOES govern `§ 3.310(d)` secondary presumptions!)*.<br/>• **Note (5):** Allowed veterans rated at `10%` pre-Oct. 23, 2008 to request review under the new table without proving worsening. |

## 4. Miscellaneous Neurological Diseases & Migraines (`DC 8100–8108`)

### A. Migraines (`DC 8100`) — Controlling Precedential Matrix

| Rating | Verbatim `DC 8100` Threshold ([Snapshot](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.124a-tbi-and-peripheral-nerves-evolution.md)) | Controlling CAVC Doctrinal Construction |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **`50%`** | *"With very frequent completely prostrating and prolonged attacks productive of severe economic inadaptability"* | • ***Pierce v. Principi*, `18 Vet. App. 440, 445–46 (2004)`:** `"Productive of"` means **capable of producing** (NOT necessarily *currently producing* complete job loss); `"severe economic inadaptability"` denotes substantial work impairment (missed workdays, leaving shifts early, FMLA/sick-leave exhaustion, workplace accommodations, inability to work during attacks)—**continued full-time employment does NOT bar `50%`!**<br/>• ***Johnson v. Wilkie*, `30 Vet. App. 245, 250–55 (2018)`:** `DC 8100` tiers are **successive and conjunctive** (making `§ 4.7` *"nearly approximates"* inapplicable across missing elements), so `50%` requires `(1)` very frequent (`>1/month`, contrasted against `30%` once/month), `(2)` completely prostrating (extreme exhaustion/powerlessness compelling cessation of activity), `(3)` prolonged, AND `(4)` productive of severe economic inadaptability. Crucially, the Board **cannot invent arbitrary, undisclosed numerical thresholds** (e.g., demanding weekly attacks or `>48-hour` duration) without articulating a reasoned medical or dictionary basis!<br/>• ***Ingram v. Collins*, `38 Vet. App. 130 (2025)`:** Because `DC 8100` is completely **silent on medication**, raters and examiners **cannot discount baseline migraine frequency, prostration, or duration** based on ameliorative relief from abortive/prophylactic medications (sumatriptan/rizatriptan, gepants, CGRP monoclonal antibodies, Botox, topiramate, propranolol)! *(Watchlist: VA's Feb. 17, 2026 `§ 4.10` IFR at `91 FR 7118` was **rescinded ab initio** Feb. 27, 2026 at `91 FR 9712`).* |
| **`30%`** | *"With characteristic prostrating attacks occurring on an average once a month over last several months"* | Requires characteristic prostrating attacks averaging **`1` per month** over the last several months (no economic-inadaptability requirement). |
| **`10%`** | *"With characteristic prostrating attacks averaging one in 2 months over last several months"* | Averages **`1` prostrating attack every `2` months** over the last several months. |
| **`0%`** | *"With less frequent attacks"* | Diagnosed SC migraines/headaches with prostrating attacks less frequent than once every 2 months (or non-prostrating headaches). |

> [!WARNING]
> **Bitemporal CUE Gate on `DC 8100`:** Attacking a **final pre-2018** Board decision solely for violating *Johnson*'s anti-arbitrary-definition reasoning or a **final pre-2025** decision solely under *Ingram* is **`open-claim-only-george-restricted`** (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`). Conversely, if a post-2004 final RO decision denied `50%` *solely* on the stated ground that the veteran was employed despite conceding *"very frequent completely prostrating and prolonged attacks"* causing heavy sick-leave loss, evaluate under *Pierce* (`2004`).

## 5. Cranial (`DC 8205–8412`) & Peripheral Nerves (`DC 8510–8730`, `§§ 4.120, 4.123, 4.124, 4.124a`)

### A. The Three-Prefix Architecture & Severity Ceilings (`§§ 4.123–4.124`)

Every cranial and peripheral nerve carries **three parallel Diagnostic Codes** sharing the same final two digits, governed by distinct regulatory ceilings:

| Category | Cranial DCs | Peripheral DCs | Governing `38 C.F.R.` Ceiling & Case Law |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Paralysis** *(Motor / Mixed Loss)* | `8205–8212` | `8510–8530` | Up to **Complete Paralysis** (`100%` of nerve's schedule). **Wholly Sensory Ceiling Note (`§ 4.124a`):** *"When the involvement is **wholly sensory**, the rating should be for the mild, or at most, the moderate degree."* Under ***Miller v. Shulkin*, `28 Vet. App. 376, 380 (2017)`**, this note is a **one-way ceiling** on purely sensory neuropathy—it does **not** create an automatic `"moderate+"` floor merely because reflex/motor signs exist. |
| **Neuritis** *(Inflammatory / Degenerative)* | `8305–8312` | `8610–8630` | **`38 C.F.R. § 4.123`:**<br/>• **With organic changes** *(loss of reflexes, muscle atrophy, sensory disturbances, and constant pain, at times excruciating)*: Maximum = **`Incomplete, Severe`**.<br/>• **Without organic changes:** Maximum = **`Moderate`**—**EXCEPT for the Sciatic Nerve (`DC 8620`), where the cap without organic changes is `Moderately Severe (40%)`!** |
| **Neuralgia** *(Paroxysmal Pain / Sensory)* | `8405–8412` | `8710–8730` | **`38 C.F.R. § 4.124`:** Characterized by dull/intermittent pain, tingling, numbness without motor/trophic loss -> Maximum = **`Moderate`**.<br/>• **CRITICAL EXCEPTION (`§ 4.124` & `DC 8405`):** ***Tic douloureux* (trifacial / trigeminal neuralgia of the Fifth Cranial Nerve, `DC 8405`) may be rated up to `Complete Paralysis (50%)`**! ### C. Upper-Extremity Peripheral Nerves (`DC 8510–8719`) & `§ 4.69` Handedness

## 6. The Epilepsies (`38 C.F.R. §§ 4.121–4.122`, `DC 8910–8914`)

### A. Physician Identification Gate (`§ 4.121`) vs. Competent Lay Seizure Logs
* **Threshold Diagnosis Gate (`38 C.F.R. § 4.121`):** *"To warrant a rating for epilepsy, the seizures must be witnessed or verified at some time by a physician"* (satisfied by direct physician observation OR clinical/EEG verification accepting a credible medical history or eyewitness account; see [Verbatim Snapshot](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.124a-tbi-and-peripheral-nerves-evolution.md)).
* **Lay Competence for Seizure Frequency (*Jandreau* / *Buchanan*):** Once a medical diagnosis of epilepsy is established, **lay witnesses (the veteran, spouse, roommate, or coworker) are fully competent** to testify to the **observable frequency, tonic-clonic convulsions, staring spells, tongue-biting, incontinence, and post-ictal exhaustion** occurring under *"the ordinary conditions of life"* (`§ 4.121`; *Jandreau v. Nicholson*, `492 F.3d 1372, 1377 (Fed. Cir. 2007)`; *Buchanan v. Nicholson*, `451 F.3d 1331 (Fed. Cir. 2006)`). Under `§ 4.121`, frequency **must** reflect ordinary non-hospitalized life rather than sheltered inpatient observation.

### B. Seizure Classifications (`DC 8910–8914`) & General Rating Formula

* **Major Seizure Classifications:**
  * **`DC 8910` (Epilepsy, grand mal):** Generalized tonic-clonic convulsions with unconsciousness.
  * **`DC 8914` (Epilepsy, psychomotor; `§ 4.122`):** Rated as **major seizures** when characterized by **automatic states and/or transient episodes with loss of conscious control** (confusional/fugue episodes, automatisms); otherwise rated as minor seizures when brief and without loss of conscious control. *(Note: Chronic psychotic/neurocognitive disturbances secondary to epilepsy are rated separately under `§ 4.130`!)*
* **Minor Seizure Classifications:**
  * **`DC 8911` (Epilepsy, petit mal):** Brief interruption in consciousness/conscious control (absence staring, rhythmic blinking, myoclonic jerks, akinetic drop) *(also governs **`DC 8108` Narcolepsy**!)*.
  * **`DC 8912` (Jacksonian and focal motor or sensory)** & **`DC 8913` (Diencephalic / autonomic):** Rated as minor seizures, *unless* a focal seizure progresses ("generalizes") into a tonic-clonic major seizure, which counts as a **major seizure**!

| Rating | Major Seizure Threshold | OR Minor Seizure Threshold |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **`100%`** | Averaging **at least `1` major seizure per month** over the last year | *(Minor seizures alone max out at `80%` unless generalized/psychomotor automatic)* |
| **`80%`** | Averaging **at least `1` major seizure in `3` months** over the last year | **More than `10` minor seizures weekly** |
| **`60%`** | Averaging **at least `1` major seizure in `4` months** over the last year | **`9 to 10` minor seizures per week** |
| **`40%`** | **At least `1` major seizure in the last `6` months** OR **`2` in the last year** | Averaging **`5 to 8` minor seizures weekly** |
| **`20%`** | **At least `1` major seizure in the last `2` years** | **At least `2` minor seizures in the last `6` months** |
| **`10%`** | Confirmed diagnosis of epilepsy with history of seizures, **OR continuous anticonvulsant medication shown necessary for control** | Confirmed diagnosis with history of seizures, **OR continuous medication necessary for control** |

*(Note: When both major and minor seizures are present, rate the **predominating type**—i.e., whichever category yields the **higher evaluation** under `§ 4.7`!)*
