## 2. Substantive Gateways (`§§ 4.125–4.128`) & The Mandatory `38 C.F.R. § 4.129` `50%` Traumatic-Discharge Rule

### A. `§§ 4.125–4.128` Operational Rules (`DSM-5`, *Clemons*, Personality Disorders & `6-Month` Hospitalizations)
1. **Formal `DSM-5` Diagnosis Gate (`§ 4.125(a)`) vs. Broad Psychiatric Claim Scope (*Clemons*):**
   * Under ***Martinez-Bodon v. McDonough*, `28 F.4th 1241 (Fed. Cir. 2022)`** (*aff'g* `33 Vet. App. 393 (2020)`), `38 C.F.R. §§ 4.125(a)` and `4.130` make a **formal `DSM-5` diagnosis a mandatory precondition** to psychiatric service connection—distinguishing unlabelled physical pain under *Saunders v. Wilkie*, `886 F.3d 1356 (Fed. Cir. 2018)`. If an examination lacks `DSM-5` substantiation, `§ 4.125(a)` requires the rating agency to **return the report to the examiner**.
   * Crucially, under ***Clemons v. Shinseki*, `23 Vet. App. 1 (2009)`** and `§ 4.125(b)`, a lay veteran's claim for `"PTSD"` encompasses **every psychiatric diagnosis reasonably raised by the record** (e.g., `DC 9434` Major Depressive Disorder, `DC 9400` GAD, `DC 9440` Chronic Adjustment Disorder, `DC 9421` Somatic Symptom Disorder)—often allowing the veteran to win direct SC under **`38 C.F.R. § 3.303(a)`** even when `§ 3.304(f)` PTSD stressor corroboration is contested ([Playbook 16](../../playbooks/16-ptsd-stressors-and-mst-corroboration.md))!
2. **Longitudinal Assessment (`§ 4.126`) & Personality Disorders (`§ 4.127`):**
   * **`§ 4.126(a)–(b)`:** Raters **cannot** base an evaluation on a brief lucid `"good day"` during a 20-minute telehealth DBQ; VA must evaluate longitudinal frequency, severity, duration, length of remissions, and social adaptability across work and personal relationships.
   * **`§ 4.127`:** Intellectual developmental disorder and personality disorders are not compensable diseases per se (`§ 3.303(c)`), **EXCEPT** `(a)` when an SC mental disorder (`PTSD`/`MDD`) is **superimposed upon or aggravates** a personality disorder (`§ 3.310(a)`), or `(b)` when historical in-service *"personality disorder"* Separation Code entries masked incipient **schizophrenia (`§ 3.309(a)`), bipolar disorder, PTSD, or MST** ([Playbook 10](../../playbooks/10-character-of-discharge-insanity-upgrades.md)).
3. **Extended Inpatient Psychiatric Hospitalization (`38 C.F.R. § 4.128`):**
   * Whenever an SC mental disorder receives `100%` due to a **continuous hospitalization lasting `6 months or more`** (building on `§ 4.29` `>21-day` hospital `100%`), VA **MUST continue the `100%` evaluation indefinitely**, schedule a mandatory examination **`6 months` after discharge**, and apply **`38 C.F.R. § 3.105(e)`** before any reduction!

### B. `38 C.F.R. § 4.129` Mandatory `50%` Post-Discharge Minimum (Major Retroactive / CUE Trigger!)
Enacted **November 7, 1996 (`61 FR 52695`)**, `38 C.F.R. § 4.129` (*Mental disorders due to traumatic stress*) mandates:
> *"When a mental disorder that develops in service as a result of a highly stressful event is severe enough to bring about the veteran's release from active military service, the rating agency **shall assign an evaluation of not less than 50 percent** and schedule an examination within the six month period following the veteran's discharge to determine whether a change in evaluation is warranted."*

* **Four Practice Traps & Retroactive Goldmines Under `§ 4.129` (`cue-and-open-claim` >= Nov. 7, 1996):**
  1. **NOT Limited to `DC 9411` PTSD:** By its plain text (`"a mental disorder"`), `§ 4.129` applies to **ANY `DSM` mental disorder** (`DC 9434` Major Depression, `DC 9400` Anxiety, `DC 9412` Panic Disorder, `DC 9440` Adjustment Disorder, `DC 9520–9521` Eating Disorder, or `DC 9201–9211` Psychosis) precipitated in service by a **highly stressful event** (combat, MST/personal assault, fatal crash, shipboard fire, mortuary duty)!
  2. **Administrative vs. Medical Separations (`"Bring About"`):** While `MEB/PEB/IDES` medical retirement or medical separation for a trauma-induced mental disorder is an automatic `§ 4.129` trigger, `§ 4.129` **also applies** when STRs/separation files show a trauma-induced mental disorder precipitated an administrative separation (e.g., `"condition not a disability"` / adjustment-disorder separation later recognized as SC PTSD/MST)!
  3. **Prospective `§ 3.105(e)` Protection vs. Retrospective Staged Ratings:** On a **running** post-discharge `50%+` award, VA **cannot** reduce below `50%` without conducting the mandatory 6-month VA exam AND issuing `60-day` **`38 C.F.R. § 3.105(e)`** notice (*cf. Foster v. McDonough*, `34 Vet. App. 338 (2021)`). Conversely, when `§ 4.129` is awarded **retroactively years later** as a historical staged rating, `§ 4.129` locks in **`>= 50%` for at least the initial post-discharge period** until post-6-month medical evidence affirmatively establishes sustained improvement, though prospective `§ 3.343(a)` hurdles do not block retrospective staged ratings (*Reizenstein v. Shinseki*, `583 F.3d 1331 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`).
  4. **Parallel `DoD` Medical Retirement Upgrade (`10 U.S.C. § 1216a` & *Sabo*):** Between `2001` and `2009`, military Physical Evaluation Boards unlawfully assigned `0%` or `10%` severance ratings for PTSD instead of `§ 4.129`'s **`50%` Temporary Disability Retired List (`TDRL`)** threshold (`>= 30%` = lifetime military medical retirement + `TRICARE` + `CRSC`!). Under **`10 U.S.C. § 1216a`** and ***Sabo v. United States*, `102 Fed. Cl. 619 (2011)`**, always screen `MEB/PEB` separation packets for a `PDBR` / `BCMR` medical-retirement upgrade alongside the VA claim!

## 3. Controlling `§ 4.130` Precedents, Single-Rating Consolidation & Complete `DC 9201–9440` Taxonomy

### A. Controlling Precedential Architecture on `§ 4.130` (`0% / 10% / 30% / 50% / 70% / 100%`)

See [`38-cfr-4.132-mental-disorders-pre-1996.md`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.132-mental-disorders-pre-1996.md) for the verbatim `§ 4.130` General Rating Formula.

| Precedent | Binding Doctrinal Rule Under `38 C.F.R. § 4.130` | Bitemporal Scope (`§ 20.1403(e)`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ***Mauerhan v. Principi*, `16 Vet. App. 436 (2002)`** | The phrase **`"such symptoms as"`** makes the listed symptoms at each tier (`30%`, `50%`, `70%`, `100%`) **non-exhaustive illustrative examples**—raters may neither treat them as a mandatory cumulative checklist nor deny a higher tier when unenumerated symptoms produce equivalent occupational and social impairment. | `open-claim-only-george-restricted` pre-2002; binding on all open/AMA claims. |
| ***Vazquez-Claudio v. Shinseki*, `713 F.3d 112 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`** | `§ 4.130` is **predominantly symptom-driven**: evaluating a mental disorder is a **two-part inquiry** assessing `(1)` what symptoms exist, AND `(2)` their **frequency, severity, and duration** (`§ 4.126(a)`) to determine the resulting level of occupational and social impairment (`"deficiencies in most areas"` at `70%` vs. `"reduced reliability and productivity"` at `50%`). | Binding Federal Circuit holistic test. |
| ***Bankhead v. Shulkin*, `29 Vet. App. 10, 20–22 (2017)`** & ***Golden v. Shulkin*, `29 Vet. App. 221 (2018)`** | **Suicidal Ideation = `70%` Hallmark:** The presence of **suicidal ideation alone**—including **passive suicidal ideation** (*thoughts of one's own death or wishing to be dead*)—signals `70%`-level impairment **without requiring active intent, a lethal plan, preparatory acts, or inpatient psychiatric hospitalization**! If the Board assigns `< 70%` despite documented suicidal ideation, it must explain why the veteran's overall symptom profile fails to approximate `70%`. (*Golden* reinforces alignment with DSM-5 symptom definitions). | **`open-claim-only-george-restricted`** against final pre-2017 decisions; premier direct-appeal / HLR / Board remand weapon! |
| ***Sellers v. Principi*, `372 F.3d 1318 (Fed. Cir. 2004)`** & ***Amberman v. Shinseki*, `570 F.3d 1377 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`** | **Single Mental Rating Consolidation (`§ 4.14`):** All comorbid `DC 9201–9440` psychiatric diagnoses (e.g., `DC 9411` PTSD + `DC 9434` MDD + `DC 9400` GAD + `DC 9421` Somatic Symptom Disorder + secondary substance-use disorder under *Allen v. Principi*) are consolidated into **ONE combined `§ 4.130` rating** reflecting total psychiatric impairment. *(See Section 3.C for mandatory separate physical/neurological/eating-disorder carve-outs!)* | `cue-and-open-claim` (`>= Nov. 7, 1996`). |
| ***McCarroll v. McDonald*, `28 Vet. App. 267 (2016) (en banc)`** vs. ***Ingram v. Collins*, `38 Vet. App. 130 (2025)`** | **Psychotropic Medication Rule:** `§ 4.130`'s **`0%` and `10%` tiers explicitly mention continuous medication** (*"or symptoms controlled by continuous medication"* = `10%`), whereas **`30%`, `50%`, `70%`, and `100%` are silent on medication**. Thus, if SSRIs/mood stabilizers genuinely control symptoms down to mild/asymptomatic levels, `10%` applies (*McCarroll*); however, whenever residual symptoms persist above `10%` (panic attacks, depressed mood, sleep impairment, suicidal ideation), **`30%–100%` are silent on medication**, barring examiners from discounting partially medicated severity under *Ingram* (`91 FR 7118` rescinded `91 FR 9712`)! | Audit both `§ 4.130` residual severity AND **secondary medication side-effect claims**! |

### B. Complete `DSM-5` Diagnostic Code Taxonomy (`DC 9201–9440`, `79 FR 45093` / `80 FR 14308`)

## 4. Eating Disorders (`DC 9520` Anorexia Nervosa & `DC 9521` Bulimia Nervosa, `§ 4.130` Notes 1 & 2)

Enacted **November 7, 1996 (`61 FR 52695`)** and expressly carved out of the `DC 9201–9440` General Rating Formula by **`38 C.F.R. § 4.130` Note 2** (which refers to the codified **`Rating Formula for Eating Disorders`** heading as the *General Rating Formula for Eating Disorders*; see verbatim text in [`38-cfr-4.132-mental-disorders-pre-1996.md`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.132-mental-disorders-pre-1996.md)):

| Rating | Structural Criteria Summary (`DC 9520` Anorexia Nervosa & `DC 9521` Bulimia Nervosa — Verbatim SSOT in [`authorities/`](../../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.132-mental-disorders-pre-1996.md)) |
| :---: | :--- |
| **`100%`** | Weight `< 80%` expected minimum + `>= 6 weeks`/yr incapacitating episodes + `> 2` hospitalizations/yr for parenteral nutrition or tube feeding *(conjunctive `"and"`)*. |
| **`60%`** | Weight `< 85%` expected minimum + `>= 6 weeks`/yr incapacitating episodes. |
| **`30%`** | Weight `< 85%` expected minimum + `> 2` to `< 6 weeks`/yr incapacitating episodes. |
| **`10%`** | Diagnosed purging/weight-resistance behaviors + incapacitating episodes of up to `2 weeks`/yr. |
| **`0%`** | Diagnosed eating disorder (`DC 9520`/`9521`) without physician-prescribed incapacitating episodes. |

> [!NOTE]
> **Structural Cross-Reference & Audit Hooks (`Note 1`, Comorbid `§ 4.130` & Physical Residuals):**
> 1. **`Note 1` Bed-Rest Prerequisite:** `Note 1` defines an `"incapacitating episode"` strictly as *"a period during which bed rest and treatment by a physician are required"*; coupled with the `< 85%` / `< 80%` weight cutoffs at `30%–100%`, normal-weight `DC 9521` Bulimia Nervosa or DSM-5 Binge-Eating Disorder (`§ 4.20` analogy) rarely exceeds `0%` or `10%` under the Eating Disorders table alone.
> 2. **Comorbid `§ 4.130` & Secondary Physical Cross-References:** Because `Note 2` separates `DC 9520–9521` from `DC 9201–9440`, comorbid trauma/mood disorders (`DC 9411` PTSD / `DC 9434` MDD, e.g., post-MST under [Playbook 16](../../playbooks/16-ptsd-stressors-and-mst-corroboration.md)) are evaluated on distinct non-duplicated symptoms under **`§ 4.130` (`30%–100%`)** (`§ 4.14`; [Playbook 19](../../playbooks/19-extraschedular-3.321b1-analogous-4.20-and-temp-total.md) for `§ 3.321(b)(1)` extraschedular). Common secondary physical sequelae (`§ 3.310(a)`) include dental enamel erosion/tooth loss (`§ 4.150` + `38 C.F.R. § 17.161` VA Dental), esophagitis/GERD/stricture (`DC 7206`/`7346`), hypokalemic cardiac arrhythmias (`DC 7010`/`7011`), secondary amenorrhea/infertility (`DC 7613` + `SMC(k)`), and osteoporosis (`DC 5013`).
