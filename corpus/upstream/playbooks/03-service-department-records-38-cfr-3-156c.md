# Playbook 03: Newly Associated Service Department Records (`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)`)

`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)` is the premier **"time-machine" provision** in Veterans Law. Unlike CUE, it requires **no showing of undebatable error**, permits **contemporary medical nexus opinions** to link the newly associated service record to a current disability, and mandates an effective date reaching all the way back to the **original previously denied claim** (or date entitlement arose, whichever is later).

---

## 1. C-File Red Flags (Audit Triggers)

Whenever a veteran was **denied decades ago** and **granted much later** (or is currently denied for lack of in-service documentation), audit the two VBMS upload/receipt dates:
1. **Compare the Evidence List on Denial #1 vs. Grant #2:**
   * Did Denial #1 list *only* the DD-214 or outpatient health jacket (`"SMRs negative for back trauma"`), while Grant #2 followed receipt of **inpatient hospital clinical records**, **Reserve/National Guard drill STRs (`LOD` line-of-duty reports)**, **ship deck logs**, **morning reports**, **military police blotters**, or a **JSRRC / CRUR stressor report**?
   * **Classic Rater Error:** When granting on the later Supplemental/Reopened claim, RVSRs routinely default to the date of the Supplemental Claim (`VA Form 20-0995` / `21-526EZ`) and completely forget to check whether `§ 3.156(c)(3)` mandates the **1972, 1984, or 1996 original claim date**!
2. **Proactive Archive Hunts (When Records Still Haven't Been Pulled):**
   * **Inpatient Clinical Records (NPRC / St. Louis):** Stored by hospital name and year, *not* always merged into the veteran's outpatient STR folder!
   * **1973 NPRC Fire Cases:** SGO (Surgeon General's Office) hospital admission punch-card index extracts, unit morning reports, pay vouchers, and VA hospital folders.
   * **DD Form 215 Corrections / Awards:** Retroactive Purple Heart, CIB, CAR, or Air Medal citations verifying combat stressors previously rejected as uncorroborated.
   * **Classified / Declassified DoD Exposure Records (`§ 3.156(c)(1)(ii)`):** Project 112 / SHAD, Edgewood Arsenal, mustard gas, radiation logs, Camp Lejeune / Thailand / Johnston Atoll military unit histories.

---

## 2. The Three-Element Test Under `38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1) & (3)`

To unlock the earlier claim's effective date, prove:

1. **Official Service Department Record:** Generated by a military branch, DoD, Coast Guard, PHS/NOAA, JSRRC/CRUR, NARA/NPRC archival holdings of military unit records, or `§ 3.156(c)(1)(iii)` reconstructed NPRC fire records. (*Note:* Even if publicly accessible in National Archives in 1980, what matters is when it was **associated with the VA C-file**—*Emerson v. McDonald*, 28 Vet. App. 200 (2016)).
2. **Not Associated With the C-File at the Time of the Prior Decision:** Verify VBMS date stamps or paper C-file date-received stamps on the reverse/bottom margin of the STR pages.
3. **"Relevant" & Basis of Award "All or in Part" (*Vigil*, *Blubaugh*, *Kisor*):**
   * **Relevance (*Kisor v. McDonough*, 995 F.3d 1316 (Fed. Cir. 2021)):** The newly associated record must speak to a **matter that was in dispute** in the prior denial (e.g., if the prior denial rejected in-service stressor, exposure, or injury, and the new record corroborates that stressor, exposure, or injury).
   * **"All or in Part" (*Vigil v. Peake*, 22 Vet. App. 63 (2008)):** The service record **does NOT need to establish service connection all by itself**! Even if VA also needed a brand-new 2025 C&P medical nexus opinion to connect the newly found 1971 sick-call slip to the veteran's current arthritis, the grant is still based *"in part"* on the 1971 service record—triggering `§ 3.156(c)(3)`!

---

## 3. Effective Date & Staged Rating Mechanics (`§ 3.156(c)(3)–(4)`)

* **Effective Date Formula:** The **LATER** of:
  1. The date VA received the **previously decided claim**, OR
  2. The **date entitlement arose** (i.e., the earliest date the disability existed and met compensable/service-connected criteria).
* **Critical Corollary for PTSD / Chronic Conditions:** Even if a formal PTSD diagnosis first appeared on a VA exam years later, audit contemporaneous lay evidence, employment history, VAMC notes, Social Security disability records, or private medical notes back to the original claim date to prove **"entitlement arose"** at (or shortly after) the original claim (*Mayhue v. Shinseki*, 24 Vet. App. 273 (2011) — watch out for when the veteran provided sufficient information for VA to request JSRRC records!).
* **Retroactive Staged Ratings (`§ 3.156(c)(4)`):** Once `§ 3.156(c)(3)` sets an effective date in (say) 1982, VA must assign staged disability percentages (`10%`, `30%`, `50%`, `70%`, `TDIU/100%`) across the entire 1982–2026 period!

---

## 4. Legal Pathway Selection, Post-Grant Reconsideration & Kill-Switches

* **Reconsideration EVEN AFTER Service Connection Was Already Granted (*Emerson*, 28 Vet. App. at 208; `M21-1 X.ii.2.B`):** Even if VA **already granted** service connection years later via a liberalizing rule (e.g., `2010` PTSD stressor relaxation under `38 C.F.R. § 3.304(f)` or `2022` PACT Act presumption), newly associating official service department records that corroborate the **original** 1975 or 1992 denial still compels `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration and a retroactive effective date back to that original claim!
  * **Filing Practice Tip:** When submitting newly obtained service department records under AMA on `VA Form 20-0995`, **explicitly label the top of the cover brief and form**: *"REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION AND EARLIER EFFECTIVE DATE UNDER 38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c) (NOT A STANDARD § 3.2500 SUPPLEMENTAL EFFECTIVE-DATE CLAIM)"* so intake processors do not assign the `20-0995` receipt date!
* **Direct Appeal / HLR / Supplemental Claim of Effective Date:** If VA just granted service connection within the last year using a wrong modern effective date despite newly associated STRs/JSRRC records, file an **HLR (`20-0996`) or Board Appeal (`10182`)** within 1 year challenging the effective date under `38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(3)` (see verbatim snapshot [`38-cfr-3.156c-service-records-evolution.md`](../authorities/part-3/38-cfr-3.156c-service-records-evolution.md)).
* **If the Later Grant Already Became Final Years Ago:**
  * Can VA's failure to apply `§ 3.156(c)(3)` in a now-final grant be attacked via **CUE (`§ 3.105(a)`)**? **YES**, where the newly associated service records and continuous disability since the original claim were undebatably in the record at the time of that grant! Alternatively, if yet *additional* relevant service records are associated, file directly for `§ 3.156(c)(1)` reconsideration (*George v. Shulkin*, 29 Vet. App. 199 (2018)).
* **The *Blubaugh* Intermediate-Denial Exhaustion Trap (`773 F.3d at 1314`):** If VA associated the missing service record during an **intermediate** reopening and denied that reopening for *lack of a current diagnosis*, `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration was exhausted in that intermediate decision—a *third* claim granted years later when a diagnosis finally appears will not reach back to Claim #1.
* **The *Mayhue* Address/Unit Info Trap:** Under *Mayhue v. Shinseki*, 24 Vet. App. 273 (2011), if VA could *not* locate unit records in Claim #1 ONLY because the veteran failed to provide unit name/dates (`§ 3.156(c)(2)`), and the veteran first supplied the unit name/dates in Claim #2 enabling JSRRC verification, VA may limit the effective date to Claim #2. Always verify whether the veteran's `DD-214` or personnel file (`OMPF`) *already* listed the unit during Claim #1!
