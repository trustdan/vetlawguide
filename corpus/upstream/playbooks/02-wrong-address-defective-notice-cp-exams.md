# Playbook 02: Wrong-Address Mailings, Rebutting Regularity & `§ 3.655` C&P Exam Denials

A VA rating decision or Statement of the Case **never becomes final**—and its 1-year appellate clock **never begins to run**—if VA failed to mail notice in strict compliance with `38 U.S.C. § 5104(a)` (`former § 3004(a)`, enacted by `Pub. L. 101-237`, eff. Feb. 1, 1990; renumbered by `Pub. L. 102-40`, May 7, 1991) and `38 U.S.C. § 7105` (`former § 4005`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.103`) (*Hauck v. Brown*, 6 Vet. App. 518 (1994)). Likewise, summary denials for "failing to report" for a C&P exam (`38 C.F.R. § 3.655`) frequently harbor either defective exam notice or illegal disregard of existing evidence.

---

## 1. C-File Red Flags (Audit Triggers)

1. **Returned Mail Scans:** Search VBMS / paper C-file for scanned envelopes, `VA Form 20-8992` / `21-0820` Report of General Information notes saying *"Mail returned undeliverable"*, or handwritten `"RTS"` notations.
2. **Chronological Address Audit:** Build a mini-table of every address appearing in the C-file (`DD-214` mailing address, `21-526`, `21-4138`, change-of-address cards, VAMC `10-10` admission facesheets, VSO `21-22` POA forms) in the 24 months prior to a denial. Compare character-by-character against:
   * The C&P exam scheduling letter (if preserved), AND
   * The Rating Decision / SOC notification letter (and copy to representative!).
3. **Summary `§ 3.655` Denials:** Any rating decision reading *"Veteran failed to report for examination without good cause; claim denied"*—especially on an **original** claim where STRs + medical evidence were already in the file, or an **increased-rating / reopened / supplemental** claim where exam notice went to an outdated address!

---

## 2. Substantive Rules on `38 C.F.R. § 3.655` (Failure to Report for C&P Exam)

Since **December 31, 1990**, `38 C.F.R. § 3.655` establishes a sharp dichotomy based on **claim type**:

| Claim Type | Mandatory Rule Under `38 C.F.R. § 3.655(b)–(c)` | Classic VA Error |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Original Compensation Claim (`§ 3.655(b)` first sentence)** | Claim **shall be rated based on the evidence of record**! It **cannot** be automatically denied solely as punishment for missing an exam! | Rater issued a knee-jerk automatic denial despite STRs + medical evidence already establishing SC or a partial rating -> **CUE (`§ 3.105(a)`)** if existing evidence undebatably warranted a grant! |
| **Increased-Rating or Reopened / Supplemental Claim (`§ 3.655(b)` second sentence)** | May be denied for failure to report *only if* claimant failed to report **without good cause**! | Exam notice went to a wrong/outdated address ("good cause" per `§ 3.655(a)` examples includes illness, hospitalization, or death in immediate family, plus lack of notice!). |
| **Reexamination of Running Award (`§ 3.655(c)`)** | Requires **60-day pretermination notice**; if veteran expresses willingness to report before cut, VA must reschedule! | RO reduced or stopped an existing award after a single missed reexamination without issuing `§ 3.655(c)` / `§ 3.105(e)` 60-day advance notice -> **Void reduction**! |

> **Exact Federal Register History of `38 C.F.R. § 3.655` (Publication vs. Effective Date — Verbatim Snapshot at [`38-cfr-3.655-examinations-evolution.md`](../authorities/part-3/38-cfr-3.655-examinations-evolution.md)):**

> * **1990 Overhaul (`55 FR 49521`):** Published **November 29, 1990**, **effective December 31, 1990** (revising `§§ 3.326, 3.327, 3.330, 3.655` to separate original claims rated on the record from reopened/increased-rating claims denied for failure to report without good cause; subsequently amended at `58 FR 46865`, Sept. 3, 1993; `80 FR 18117`, Apr. 3, 2015; and `84 FR 138, 171`, Jan. 18, 2019, eff. Feb. 19, 2019 substituting "supplemental" for "reopened"). *(Citation Guardrail: Do not confuse `55 FR 49521` on `§ 3.655` with `55 FR 31579`, published Aug. 3, 1990, eff. Sept. 4, 1990, which amended `38 C.F.R. § 4.16(a)` TDIU marginal employment!)*

## 3. Rebutting the Presumption of Regularity (Notice of Decisions & Exams)

Under *Ashley v. Derwinski*, 2 Vet. App. 307, 309 (1992) and *Mindenhall v. Brown*, 7 Vet. App. 271, 274 (1994), VA enjoys a rebuttable **presumption of regularity** that it properly mailed decisions and exam notices to the latest address of record.

### Step 1: Claimant Produces "Clear Evidence" of Irregularity
Any of the following satisfies "clear evidence" to **burst the presumption**:
* **Wrong / Typo Address on the Letter:** Missing apartment number, transposed digits, wrong city/ZIP, or using an older C-file address when a newer address was already in the claims folder (*Crain v. Principi*, 17 Vet. App. 182 (2003); *Davis v. Principi*, 17 Vet. App. 29 (2003)).
* **Returned Undeliverable Mail + Alternate Address Available:** When a decision letter comes back undeliverable and **any other plausible address** exists in the claims file or VAMC records (*Cross v. Brown*, 9 Vet. App. 18, 19–20 (1996); *Woods v. Gober*, 14 Vet. App. 214 (2000)), VA cannot sit on the returned envelope—it must check its records and remail!
* **Representative Nonreceipt + Systemic Mailing Irregularity (*Romero v. Tran*, 33 Vet. App. 252 (2021)):** *Romero* specifically governs mailing an SOC/decision to a claimant's **accredited representative** (rather than wrong-address C&P notices), holding that nonreceipt **combined with** clear evidence of systemic VA mailing irregularity (practitioner affidavits, NOVA declaration, GAO report) bursts regularity.
* **No Copy of Notice Letter in C-File + Corroborating Procedural Anomalies:** E.g., rating sheet marked `"File Only — Do Not Notify"` or blank address block.

### Step 2: Burden Shifts to the Secretary
Once clear evidence bursts the presumption, the burden shifts to VA to establish by affirmative evidence that the decision was **actually mailed properly or actually received** by the claimant (*Ashley*, 2 Vet. App. at 309; *Romero*, 33 Vet. App. at 262). If VA cannot prove proper mailing/receipt, **the appeal period never started**—the veteran can file a timely Notice of Disagreement / appeal **today** on a 1979 or 1998 rating decision!

## 4. Bitemporal Pathway Selection & Kill-Switches

* **Pathway A — Defective Decision Notice (Pending Appeal / Non-Final Decision — Bypasses *George*!):** File a timely NOD / direct response asserting the prior RO decision never became final due to defective `§ 5104(a)` mailing notice, supported by exhibits of the address discrepancy or returned envelope. Because the claim remains **open on direct review**, **benefit of the doubt (`§ 5107(b)`), full duty to assist, and post-decision judicial precedents apply without triggering *George v. McDonough*, 596 U.S. 740 (2022) / `38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`**!
* **Pathway B — Defective Exam Notice + Later Re-filing:** If a later decision *validly* noticed and adjudicated the exact same condition before Feb. 19, 2019, check whether that later decision closed the pending claim (*Adams* / *Williams*)—if the later decision *granted* service connection and simply set the effective date as the *new* claim date while overlooking that the earlier claim was still pending from defective notice, appeal/challenge the effective-date assignment (or raise CUE under `§ 3.105(a)` if final and undebatable on the face of the C-file!).
* **Kill-Switch (Bare Nonreceipt & `CAVC Rule 30(a)` Nonprecedential Warning on *Marion v. Collins*):** Never rely solely on an affidavit from the veteran or representative saying *"I have good mail-handling habits and never received the decision"* (*Mindenhall*, 7 Vet. App. at 274; see [Authority Verification Log](../docs/authority-verification-log.md)). Note that *Marion v. Collins*, No. 24-6964 (Vet. App. Dec. 23, 2025) (mem. dec.) is a **single-judge nonprecedential memorandum decision (`CAVC Rule 30(a)` — do NOT cite as binding precedent)**, illustrating that counsel's bare affidavit of nonreceipt of an HLR decision without systemic or C-file corroboration fails.
