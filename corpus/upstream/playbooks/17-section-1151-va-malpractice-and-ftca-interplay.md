# Playbook 17: `38 U.S.C. § 1151` VA Medical Malpractice, Unforeseen Facility Injuries (*Viegas*), Ancillary Limits & `FTCA` (`§ 3.362`) Offset Engineering

Under **`38 U.S.C. § 1151`** (`former 38 U.S.C. § 351`), a veteran (even one with **zero service-connected disabilities**) who suffers "additional disability or death" proximately caused by VA medical/surgical care, C&P examination, **`Chapter 31` `VR&E`**, or **Compensated Work Therapy (`CWT`)** receives tax-free monthly Chapter 11 compensation (`0%–100%`, `TDIU`, and **`SMC(k)–(t)`**) or Chapter 13 survivor `DIC` **"in the same manner as if such additional disability or death were service-connected."** Unlike Federal Tort Claims Act (`FTCA`) suits, **`§ 1151` has ZERO statute of limitations** and applies the claimant-friendly **`38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)` "at least as likely as not" equipoise standard**!

## 1. Preconditions: VBMS, VistA/CAPRI & `SF-95` Audit Triggers

Screen every catastrophic VAMC surgical complication, delayed cancer diagnosis, medication error, VAMC equipment fall, or `Ch. 31` `VR&E` injury for these exact records:

* **Pleading Forms (`1-Year` Retroactive Lookback Gate):**
  * Living veteran: **`VA Form 21-526EZ`** (explicitly specifying *"Compensation under 38 U.S.C. § 1151 for [condition] resulting from VAMC [facility] care on [date]"*). If filed **within `1 year` of the date the injury/aggravation occurred**, **`38 U.S.C. § 5110(b)(3)` / `38 C.F.R. § 3.400(i)`** locks in the **exact date of the VA medical injury** as the effective date!
  * Survivor: **`VA Form 21P-534EZ`** (within `1 year` of death -> effective first day of month of death under `§ 5110(d)` / `§ 3.400(c)(2)`).
  * Parallel `FTCA` Tort Track: **`Standard Form 95` (`SF-95`)** filed with the **VA Office of General Counsel Torts Law Group** within the **strict `2-year` accrual deadline** (`28 U.S.C. § 2401(b)`).
* **High-Value VHA / VistA Discovery Targets (Request via FOIA/Privacy Act Beyond Standard `CAPRI`):**
  * **`iMedConsent` Electronic Informed-Consent Packets (`38 C.F.R. § 17.32`)** (verify signature, witness, procedure scope, and disclosed risks);
  * **VHA Institutional Disclosure of Adverse Event** notes (VHA policy mandates formal disclosure to the veteran/family when VA care causes serious injury/death);
  * **Radiology "View Alert" / Unread Imaging Logs**, **Pathology Addendum Timestamps**, **Medication Administration Records (`BCMA`)**, and **VAMC Police / Patient Safety Incident Reports** (crucial for *Viegas* equipment/facility defects!).

## 3. Three Substantive Causation Engines (`38 U.S.C. § 1151(a)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.361`)

First, establish **`"additional disability"` (`§ 3.361(b)`)** by comparing the veteran's actual post-treatment clinical condition with the condition that VA shows would have existed from the natural progression of the underlying disease (*Look v. Derwinski*, `2 Vet. App. 157 (1992)`). Then prove **any ONE** of these three statutory proximate-cause prongs at **`§ 5107(b)` equipoise**:

### Prong 1: VA Fault, Diagnostic Delay (*Roberson*) or Defective Informed Consent (*McNair* / *Hatfield*) (`§ 3.361(d)(1)`)
1. **Active Negligence / Error in Judgment (`§ 3.361(d)(1)(i)`):** Carelessness, negligence, lack of proper skill, error in judgment, or similar fault in furnishing care, surgery, medication, or C&P examination (e.g., severing a recurrent laryngeal or peroneal nerve outside standard surgical planes, prescribing nephrotoxic drugs despite stage-4 CKD labs, or tearing a rotator cuff during an aggressive C&P range-of-motion exam).
2. **Acts of Omission & Diagnostic Delay (*Roberson v. Shinseki*, `607 F.3d 809, 813–16 (Fed. Cir. 2010)`):**
   * When VAMC clinicians fail to follow up a pulmonary nodule on a CT scan, elevated PSA, or positive fecal occult blood test until cancer metastasizes, your private specialist IMO **must satisfy both *Roberson* elements**: `(a)` a reasonable provider under the standard of care **should have diagnosed/treated** the condition earlier, **AND** `(b)` timely diagnosis/intervention **at least as likely as not would have prevented** the stage progression, radical surgery, or terminal outcome!
3. **Lack of Informed Consent (`38 C.F.R. §§ 3.361(d)(1)(ii) & 17.32`; *McNair* vs. *Hatfield*):**
   * Under `§ 3.361(d)(1)(ii)`, furnishing surgical/medical treatment **without informed consent** (`38 C.F.R. § 17.32`) establishes proximate fault **even if the surgeon performed the procedure with flawless technical skill**!
   * **Navigating the *McNair* / *Hatfield* Divide:** In ***McNair v. Shinseki*, `25 Vet. App. 98, 104–07 (2011)`**, the CAVC held that a *"minor, immaterial deviation"* in the consent process (such as omitting one foreseeable risk) is excused if a reasonable patient in similar circumstances would still have consented. **HOWEVER**, in ***Hatfield v. McDonough*, `33 Vet. App. 327 (2021)` (*Hatfield I*) and `36 Vet. App. 97 (2023)`**, the CAVC held that *McNair*'s reasonable-patient exception **NEVER applies when VA made NO attempt to obtain or document `§ 17.32` informed consent**! Always pull the raw `iMedConsent` audit trail—a missing consent form wins `§ 1151` under *Hatfield*!

### Prong 2: Event Not Reasonably Foreseeable & Facility/Equipment Defects (*Viegas*) (`§ 3.361(d)(2)`)
* Even when **zero VA negligence occurred**, `§ 1151(a)(1)(B)` and `§ 3.361(d)(2)` award full compensation if the additional disability/death was proximately caused by **"an event not reasonably foreseeable"**—judged by what a **reasonable health-care provider** would have foreseen (rare idiosyncratic anaphylaxis/paralysis not recognized as a known complication of the procedure, as opposed to routine disclosed surgical risks).
* **The *Viegas* Premises & Equipment Doctrine (*Viegas v. Shinseki*, `705 F.3d 1374, 1378–83 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`):**
  * VA historically denied injuries caused by broken hospital beds, wheelchair lifts, exam tables, or bathroom grab bars, claiming `§ 1151` required direct hands-on clinician treatment.
  * The Federal Circuit **reversed**, holding that `"hospital care, medical or surgical treatment, or examination"` encompasses the VA's maintenance of the **physical facility and medical equipment** used during care: when a veteran at a VAMC for medical treatment is injured because a **restroom grab bar detaches from the wall**, that mechanical failure is an **"event not reasonably foreseeable"** compensable under `§ 1151`!

### Prong 3: `Chapter 31` `VR&E` & `§ 1718` `CWT` Injuries — Zero Fault Required! (`§ 1151(a)(2)`; `§ 3.361(d)(3)`)
* Under `38 U.S.C. § 1151(a)(2)` and `38 C.F.R. § 3.361(d)(3)` (expanded to `CWT` eff. Nov. 1, 2000 by `Pub. L. 106-419, § 403(b)`), any additional disability or death proximately caused by the pursuit of **`Chapter 31` Vocational Rehabilitation** or **`38 U.S.C. § 1718` Compensated Work Therapy (`CWT`)** requires **ZERO proof of VA fault or unforeseeability**—only direct causation between the training/work activity (e.g., lifting requirements in a `VR&E` apprenticeship or `CWT` shop herniating a lumbar disc) and the disability!

## 4. Covered vs. Excluded Care Settings (`§ 3.361(f)` — The Community Care Kill-Switch)

> [!CAUTION]
> **The `38 U.S.C. § 1703` Community Care / MISSION Act Trap (`38 C.F.R. § 3.361(f)(1)–(3)`):** Under `38 U.S.C. § 1151(a)(1)` and `38 C.F.R. § 3.361(f)`, `§ 1151` covers care **"furnished by a Department employee or in a Department facility"** (over which the Secretary has direct jurisdiction, including VBA/contract C&P examinations conducted for VA adjudication). By explicit regulation, `§ 1151` **EXCLUDES**:
> 1. **Private Community Care / MISSION Act providers under `38 U.S.C. § 1703` contracts (`§ 3.361(f)(1)`)**;
> 2. **Non-VA contract nursing homes under `38 U.S.C. § 1720` (`§ 3.361(f)(2)`)** and State Veterans Homes;
> 3. **Non-VA sharing-agreement facilities under `38 U.S.C. § 8153` outside VA direct jurisdiction (`§ 3.361(f)(3)`)**; and
> 4. **Pure domiciliary residential custody** untethered to active medical/hospital care (*Mangham v. Shinseki*, `23 Vet. App. 284, 288–90 (2009)`).
>
> **Two Immediate Intake Saves When Injury Occurred in `§ 1703` Community Care:**
> * **Save 1 (State Tort Referral):** Private `§ 1703` doctors are **not** federal employees shielded by the FTCA—immediately refer or co-counsel a **state-court medical malpractice action** before the state `1–3 year` statute of limitations runs!
> * **Save 2 (VHA Administrative Referral Delay):** If **VA employees** at the VAMC negligently delayed approving the `§ 1703` Community Care consult for months while a tumor or retinal detachment progressed, *that VAMC administrative/clinical delay* **IS** furnished by a Department employee and **DOES** support both `§ 1151` and `FTCA` (*Roberson*)!

## 6. Postconditions: `FTCA` Dual-Track Interplay & `38 C.F.R. § 3.362` Offset Engineering

### A. Why Catastrophic Malpractice Cases Should Often Pursue BOTH `FTCA` (`SF-95`) AND `§ 1151`
* **`FTCA` (`28 U.S.C. §§ 1346(b), 2675`):** Strict **`2-year` administrative filing bar** (`28 U.S.C. § 2401(b)`); governed by state malpractice law (and state non-economic damage caps!); pays an **immediate tax-free lump sum** (pain & suffering, lost wages, life-care plan); attorney fees capped at **`20%` administrative (`§ 2672`) / `25%` federal-court litigation (`28 U.S.C. § 2678`)**.
* **`38 U.S.C. § 1151`:** **ZERO statute of limitations**; governed by uniform federal `§ 3.361` + **`§ 5107(b)` equipoise** (defeating harsh state tort caps and tort-reform hurdles!); pays **lifetime COLA-indexed `100%` / `SMC(l)–(r)(2)` monthly annuities** + `SAH` + `Ch. 39 Auto`; **`§ 5904` fees** (`20%` of past-due retroactive award prior to FTCA offset date).

### B. Three Mandatory Pre-Settlement `38 C.F.R. § 3.362` Offset Rules (Judgments/Settlements `>= Dec. 1, 1962`, `§ 3.363`)
1. **The `§ 3.362(b)` Veteran GROSS-Offset Trap (Attorney Fees Are Recouped!):**
   * Under **`38 U.S.C. § 1151(b)` and `38 C.F.R. § 3.362(b)`**, when a living veteran recovers an `FTCA` settlement/judgment for the same injury, VA withholds prospective monthly `§ 1151` payments starting the month after the FTCA award becomes final until VA has recouped **the ENTIRE gross veteran share—INCLUDING the `20%–25%` FTCA attorney fees**! *(E.g., on a `$500,000` FTCA settlement where the veteran nets `$375,000` after a `25%` litigation fee, VA still withholds the full **`$500,000`** before monthly `§ 1151` checks resume!)* Never accept a modest `$75,000–$150,000` FTCA nuisance offer without modeling whether immediate `§ 1151` monthly cash flow is superior!
2. **Preserving Survivor `§ 1151` `DIC` via `38 C.F.R. § 3.362(c)` Settlement Allocation:**
   * Unlike `§ 3.362(b)`, **`38 C.F.R. § 3.362(c)`** offsets a surviving spouse's `§ 1151` `DIC` **ONLY** by the portion of the FTCA settlement/judgment compensating **harm suffered by that individual DIC-eligible survivor** (received individually or distributed via the estate for that survivor's harm, plus proportional attorney fees)!
   * **Drafting Play:** In multi-beneficiary FTCA wrongful-death/survival settlements with DOJ/OGC, explicitly allocate separate, bona fide state-law shares to **(a) adult non-DIC children** for their independent loss of parental companionship/guidance and **(b) the decedent's estate survival action** (if distributed under the will/intestacy to non-DIC heirs or used to pay estate debts/funeral expenses)—every dollar legitimately allocated to non-DIC beneficiaries is **100% exempt from offsetting the widow's monthly `§ 1151` `DIC`**! *(Compare `38 U.S.C. § 1318(d)` / `VAOPGCPREC 3-97` in [Playbook 08](08-survivor-dic-direct-and-1318-hypothetical.md)).*
3. **Keeping `Ch. 21` `SAH` & `Ch. 39` Auto Un-Offset Under `38 C.F.R. § 3.362(e)` (`>= Dec. 10, 2004`):**
   * Under **`38 C.F.R. § 3.362(e)(1)`**, an FTCA settlement reduces `Ch. 21` `SAH`/`SHA` (`~$117,000+`) or `Ch. 39` Auto (`~$25,000+`) grants **ONLY if the settlement agreement *"includes an amount that is specifically designated for a purpose for which benefits are provided under 38 U.S.C. chapter 21 ... or chapter 39"***! When drafting FTCA release/settlement stipulations with Assistant U.S. Attorneys, **never** insert line-item designations for home modifications or adapted vehicles unless DOJ requires it—general lump-sum damages offset monthly Chapter 11 compensation under `§ 3.362(b)` while leaving the veteran's **`$140,000+` in immediate `SAH` + `Ch. 39` grants completely untouched**!
