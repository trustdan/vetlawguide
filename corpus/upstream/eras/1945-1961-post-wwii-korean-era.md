# Era Lens 1: Post-WWII & Korean War Era (1945–1961)

## 1. Regulatory & Statutory Landscape
* **Launch of the 1945 VASRD (Effective April 1, 1946):** Although titled the *1945 Schedule for Rating Disabilities*, it took effect **April 1, 1946** (`38 C.F.R. § 3.952`; `38 C.F.R. Part 4, App. A`), creating the foundational 4-digit Diagnostic Code system designed around massive numbers of WWII combat casualties (shrapnel, GSW, cold injury/trench foot, tropical disease, "psychoneurosis").
* **Pre-1959 Statutory Citations & Savings Protection (`38 C.F.R. § 3.953`):** Prior to **January 1, 1959** (effective date of `Pub. L. 85-857`, `72 Stat. 1105`, enacted Sept. 2, 1958), Title 38 U.S.C. did not exist as a positive-law title. Rating sheets from 1945–1958 cite **Veterans' Regulation No. 1(a), Part I** (wartime service connection) or **Part II** (peacetime service connection) (briefly consolidated by the Veterans' Benefits Act of 1957, `Pub. L. 85-56`, `71 Stat. 83`, eff. Jan. 1, 1958). Even from January 1, 1959 until 1991 (`Pub. L. 102-40`, `105 Stat. 187`, eff. May 7, 1991 for `3000`–`4000` administrative/appellate sections; `Pub. L. 102-83`, `105 Stat. 378`, eff. Aug. 6, 1991 for `300`–`500` benefit sections), compensation statutes carried earlier section numbers (e.g., `38 U.S.C. § 310` instead of `§ 1110`; `§ 314` instead of `§ 1114`; `§ 3010` instead of `§ 5110`). Crucially, **`38 C.F.R. § 3.953(a)–(b)`** preserves statutory grandfathering under `Pub. L. 85-56` and `Pub. L. 85-857`, protecting disability compensation/service connection payable on **December 31, 1957** (`Pub. L. 85-56`) or **December 31, 1958** (`Pub. L. 85-857`) against termination or reduction absent fraud, clear and unmistakable error as to fact/law, or material misrepresentation, alongside the general 10-year service-connection shield in **`38 C.F.R. § 3.957`**.
* **Pre-1962 Appellate & Informal Claim Mechanics:** Before `Pub. L. 87-666` (`76 Stat. 553`, enacted Sept. 19, 1962, eff. Jan. 1, 1963), there was **no Statement of the Case (SOC)** procedure; veterans appealed via `VA Form P-9` / `Form 1-9` within one year of notification (see [`Forms & Code-Sheet Lexicon`](../docs/forms-and-codesheet-lexicon.md)). Meanwhile, VA's 1961 Part 3 recodification (`26 FR 1561, 1571`, published and effective Feb. 24, 1961) formally codified `38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)` making VA hospital admissions and outpatient exam reports automatic informal claims for increase (see [`38-cfr-3.155-3.157b-informal-claims-pre-2015.md`](../authorities/part-3/38-cfr-3.155-3.157b-informal-claims-pre-2015.md)).

## 2. Signature C-File Artifacts to Look For
* **`VA Form 8-526`:** Often completed right at the separation center before demobilization. Every symptom written in the veteran's own handwriting on a discharge `8-526` is an **explicit formal claim**! If the veteran listed five conditions on the `8-526` and the 1946 rating sheet only adjudicated three, the other two may have remained **legally pending** (subject to pre-AMA implicit denial analysis under *Deshotel*/*Adams*).
* **Separation Physicals & Hospital Summaries:** `WD AGO Form 38`, `AGO Form 53-55` (Enlisted Record and Report of Separation), `WD MD Form 55` clinical records, and `DD Form 214` (introduced in 1950 for Korean War era).
* **Carbon-Copy Rating Code Sheets:** Sparse 1-page sheets with virtually zero narrative "reasons or bases" (narrative explanations on RO ratings were not required until Feb. 1, 1990 under `38 U.S.C. § 3004(b)`, renumbered `§ 5104(b)` in 1991).

## 3. High-Yield Retroactive Opportunities & Bitemporal Era Traps
* **The WWII / Korea Penetrating Wound Goldmine (`cue-and-open-claim`):** Mass demobilization boards routinely granted `0%` or `10%` under `DC 7800–7805` (scars) for gunshot, mortar, and artillery shrapnel wounds when separation exams on the *same desk* recorded retained metallic foreign bodies (`SFB` / `MFB`), muscle hernia, loss of deep fascia, surgical debridement, or through-and-through muscle tracks across `Muscle Groups I–XXIII` (`DC 5301–5323`). Because the separation physical was **physically in the C-file** at the time of the 1946 or 1953 rating, this avoids the *Bell* constructive-possession bar (`VAOPGCPREC 12-95`, `60 FR 43186`, Aug. 18, 1995) and supports pure **RO CUE** (`38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a)`), quoting the verbatim historical `38 C.F.R. § 4.56` text in [`38-cfr-4.56-muscle-injuries-1946-to-present.md`](../authorities/part-4/38-cfr-4.56-muscle-injuries-1946-to-present.md).
* **1973 NPRC St. Louis Fire Overlap (`§ 3.156(c)` Open Reconsideration):** Millions of Army (discharged Nov. 1, 1912 – Jan. 1, 1960) and Air Force (discharged Sept. 25, 1947 – Jan. 1, 1964, surnames Hubbard–Z) records burned on July 12, 1973. If a Korean War veteran was denied in the 1970s/1980s for "no STRs due to fire," and morning reports, VA hospital admission summaries, or Surgeon General's Office (`SGO`) index extracts are later located and associated, **`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1)(iii)`** (see [`38-cfr-3.156c-service-records-evolution.md`](../authorities/part-3/38-cfr-3.156c-service-records-evolution.md)) unlocks an open-record reconsideration effective date back to the post-fire denied claim!
* **Bitemporal *George v. McDonough* Gate (`596 U.S. 740 (2022)`; `38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`):** When attacking a final 1945–1961 decision via CUE, anchor your argument strictly to the unambiguous text of `Veterans' Regulation No. 1(a)` / `Pub. L. 85-857` and the contemporaneous 1945 VASRD edition in force on the rating date—never on a post-decision judicial reinterpretation or invalidated regulation (`§ 20.1403(e)`). Conversely, if finality is pierced via `§ 3.156(c)` newly associated service records or an unadjudicated discharge `8-526` claim, modern precedent and benefit-of-the-doubt (`§ 5107(b)`) apply in full.
