# Judicial Review, Extraordinary Writs & Class Actions Treatise (`38 U.S.C. ch. 72`; `38 U.S.C. § 502`; `28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)`)

From `1933` until `November 18, 1988`, the Veterans' Administration operated in "splendid isolation" behind the judicial-review bar of `former 38 U.S.C. § 211(a)`. Enactment of the **Veterans' Judicial Review Act (`VJRA`)**, `Pub. L. 100-687, Div. A, 102 Stat. 4105 (Nov. 18, 1988)`, dismantled that insulation by creating a two-tiered federal appellate architecture ([title-38-statutory-crosswalk.md](title-38-statutory-crosswalk.md), [1988-2000-vjra-revolution.md](../eras/1988-2000-vjra-revolution.md)):

1. **First-Tier Article I Appellate Review (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7251–7288`):** Exclusive review of final adverse Board of Veterans' Appeals (`BVA`) decisions before the **U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (`CAVC`)**—originally enacted as `former 38 U.S.C. §§ 4051–4092` establishing the *U.S. Court of Veterans Appeals (`COVA`)*, renumbered to `Chapter 72` by `Pub. L. 102-40, § 402(b)(1), 105 Stat. 187, 238 (May 7, 1991)`, and renamed `CAVC` by `Pub. L. 105-368, § 511, 112 Stat. 3315, 3341 (Nov. 11, 1998)` (effective March 1, 1999). *(Note: `Pub. L. 107-103, § 603(a), 115 Stat. 976, 999 (Dec. 27, 2001)` repealed the VJRA's original post-Nov. 18, 1988 Notice of Disagreement jurisdictional floor).*
2. **Second-Tier Article III Law-Declaration & Direct Rulemaking Review (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7292, 502`):** Exclusive review of pure questions of statutory, regulatory, and constitutional interpretation—plus pre-enforcement review of VA regulations and `M21-1` manual rules—in the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit**, followed by certiorari to the **U.S. Supreme Court (`28 U.S.C. § 1254(1)`)**.

Pair this appellate treatise with [AMA Three-Lane & BVA Litigation Manual](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md), [Attorney Fees (`§ 5904`) & EAJA Practice](attorney-fees-5904-and-eaja-practice.md), [Part 20 & Part 14 Regulatory Snapshots](../authorities/part-20/38-cfr-part-20-ama-evid-windows-and-part-14-fees.md), [Part 19/20 & Part 14 Evolution Diff](../cfr-diffs/part-20/ama-part-19-20-and-part-14-fee-evolution.md), and the [CAVC Appeal, EAJA & Fee Compliance Gate](../checklists/cavc-appeal-eaja-and-fee-compliance-gate.md).

## 1. CAVC Exclusive Jurisdiction & Finality Prerequisites (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7252, 7261, 7266`)

Under `38 U.S.C. § 7252(a)`, the CAVC possesses **exclusive jurisdiction** to review decisions of the Board of Veterans' Appeals. That jurisdiction is bounded by four structural rules that govern every intake and briefing decision.

### A. Final Adverse Board Decision Requirement (*Kirkpatrick* & *Breeden* vs. *Maggitt*)
* **Board Remands Are NOT Final Appealable Decisions:** Under `38 U.S.C. § 7266(a)`, a claimant may appeal only a **"final decision"** of the Board. Because a Board remand neither grants nor denies benefits, **a Board remand order is not a final decision** over which the CAVC can exercise `§ 7252(a)` appellate jurisdiction—even where the veteran contends the remand instructions apply an erroneous legal standard (*Kirkpatrick v. Nicholson*, `417 F.3d 1361, 1364–65 (Fed. Cir. 2005)`; *Breeden v. Principi*, `17 Vet. App. 475, 478 (2004)` (per curiam order)).
* **Mixed Board Decisions (Bifurcated Finality):** When a single Board decision denies Issue A (e.g., lumbosacral spine service connection) while remanding Issue B (e.g., radiculopathy or TDIU), the denial of Issue A is immediately final and appealable within `120 days`, whereas remanded Issue B remains non-final before the agency—unless Issue B is inextricably intertwined with a reversed/vacated denial on Issue A.
* **Claim-Level Jurisdiction vs. Issue Exhaustion (*Maggitt v. West*):** Once a claimant obtains a final adverse Board decision on a *claim* and timely invokes `§ 7252(a)`, the CAVC has subject-matter jurisdiction over that decided claim. Under *Maggitt v. West*, `202 F.3d 1370, 1377–78 (Fed. Cir. 2000)`, the CAVC possesses case-by-case discretion to entertain newly raised legal arguments supporting that same claim—or to require administrative issue exhaustion where factual development or agency expertise outweighs immediate resolution (cf. *Scott v. McDonald*, `789 F.3d 1375, 1378–81 (Fed. Cir. 2015)`). By contrast, if the Board never adjudicated a **distinct claim or distinct CUE theory** at all, the CAVC lacks `§ 7252(a)` jurisdiction over that unadjudicated matter (*Andre v. Principi*, `301 F.3d 1354, 1361 (Fed. Cir. 2002)`).

### B. One-Way Asymmetry of Review (`38 U.S.C. § 7252(a)`)
Congress intentionally designed CAVC review to be **one-directional**:
> *"The Secretary may not seek review of any such [Board] decision."* (`38 U.S.C. § 7252(a)`).

If the Board grants service connection, assigns a `70%` evaluation, or awards an earlier effective date, **VA Office of General Counsel (`OGC`) cannot appeal or cross-appeal that favorable Board ruling to the CAVC**. (Conversely, once the *CAVC* enters judgment, *either* the claimant or the Secretary may appeal a pure legal ruling to the Federal Circuit under `38 U.S.C. § 7292(a)`).

### C. Inviolability of Favorable Factual Findings (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5104A, 7261(a)(4)`; *Medrano*)
In the Veterans Benefits Act of 2002 (`Pub. L. 107-330, § 401, 116 Stat. 2820, 2832`), Congress amended `38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(4)` so that the CAVC may set aside or reverse *only* a finding of material fact **"adverse to the claimant."**
* **Absolute Bar Against Disturbing Favorable Board Findings:** Under ***Medrano v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 165, 170 (2007)`** and ***Roberson v. Principi*, `17 Vet. App. 135, 139 (2003)` (per curiam order)**, **the CAVC lacks statutory authority or jurisdiction to reverse, vacate, or disturb findings of fact made by the Board that are favorable to the claimant**—full stop. Even if VA OGC argues on appeal that a favorable Board predicate finding (e.g., confirmed in-service injury, current diagnosis, credibility of lay symptomatology, or exposure) lacked a plausible basis, the CAVC **cannot** overturn it.
* **Agency-Level Binding Force (`38 U.S.C. § 5104A`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.104(c)`):** Enacted by the Appeals Modernization Act (`Pub. L. 115-55`, eff. Feb. 19, 2019), any favorable finding of fact made by an AOJ rater or the Board is **binding on all subsequent VA and Board adjudicators** unless rebutted by **clear and unmistakable evidence** ([ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md)).

### D. Precedential Discipline & `CAVC Rule 30(a)`
Under *Frankel v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 23, 25–26 (1990)` and *Bethea v. Derwinski*, `2 Vet. App. 252, 254 (1992)`, only published **three-judge panel** and **en banc** CAVC decisions establish binding precedent. Single-judge memorandum decisions (`mem. dec.`) are strictly **nonprecedential (`CAVC Rule 30(a)`)** and may never be cited as binding authority ([authority-verification-log.md](authority-verification-log.md)). If an appeal raises an issue of first impression, systemic statutory/regulatory conflict, or split single-judge outcomes, counsel should move for panel consideration under `CAVC Rules 30` and `35`.

> [!NOTE]
> **Post-Mortem Substitution at the CAVC (`CAVC Rule 43`):** If an appellant dies while a CAVC appeal is pending (or during the `120-day` NOA window after a final Board decision), an eligible accrued-benefits survivor (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5121, 5121A`) must move to substitute under `CAVC Rule 43` (*Breedlove v. Shinseki*, `24 Vet. App. 7 (2010)`). See [Playbook 09: Accrued Benefits, Substitution & Nehmer Estates](../playbooks/09-accrued-benefits-substitution-nehmer-estates.md) for the *Merritt* procedural trap when a veteran dies before CAVC mandate issues.

## 2. The `120-Day` Notice of Appeal (`NOA`) Clock, *Rosler* Abatement & Equitable Tolling (`38 U.S.C. § 7266(a)`)

Under `38 U.S.C. § 7266(a)`, a Notice of Appeal (`NOA`) must be filed with the CAVC within **`120 days` after the date on which notice of the Board's decision is mailed** (presumed to be the date stamped on the BVA decision unless rebutted by clear evidence of non-mailing or wrong-address mailing under *Ashley v. Derwinski*, `2 Vet. App. 307, 309 (1992)`).

### A. *Henderson v. Shinseki* & The Non-Jurisdictional `120-Day` Rule
In ***Henderson v. Shinseki*, `562 U.S. 428, 438–42 (2011)`**, the U.S. Supreme Court unanimously held that `38 U.S.C. § 7266(a)`'s `120-day` filing deadline is an **"important procedural" claim-processing rule, NOT a jurisdictional bar**, emphasizing the uniquely pro-claimant structure of Title 38. On remand in ***Bove v. Shinseki*, `25 Vet. App. 136, 140–43 (2011)` (per curiam order)**, the CAVC confirmed two operational consequences:
1. **`§ 7266(a)` Is Subject to Equitable Tolling:** Reinstating the pre-*Bowles* equitable-tolling jurisprudence of *Bailey v. West*, `160 F.3d 1360 (Fed. Cir. 1998) (en banc)` and *Barrett*.
2. **Waiver / Forfeiture:** Because `§ 7266(a)` is non-jurisdictional, the CAVC will not dismiss an untimely NOA *sua sponte* if the Secretary fails to raise untimeliness—though in practice VA OGC systematically audits NOA dates and files motions to dismiss before the record is transmitted.

### B. Controlling Federal Circuit Categories of Equitable Tolling
To establish equitable tolling of `§ 7266(a)`, an appellant must show: `(1)` an **extraordinary circumstance** beyond the claimant's control; `(2)` **causation** (the extraordinary circumstance prevented timely filing); and `(3)` **due diligence** in pursuing appellate rights.

| Tolling Category | Controlling Precedents | Substantive Proof Standard & Practitioner Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Mental or Physical Incapacity** | ***Barrett v. Principi*, `363 F.3d 1316, 1321 (Fed. Cir. 2004)`**; ***Arbas v. Nicholson*, `403 F.3d 1379, 1381 (Fed. Cir. 2005)`** | *Barrett* holds mental illness tolls `§ 7266(a)` where the condition rendered the veteran **incapable of "rational thought or deliberate decision making,"** or **incapable of handling their own affairs or unable to function in society** (a medical diagnosis alone is insufficient without functional linkage; corroborate via clinician declarations or hospitalization records). *Arbas* extends the identical functional-incapacity test to **physical** illness or acute hospitalization. |
| **2. Homelessness & "Stop-Clock" Diligence** | ***Checo v. Shinseki*, `748 F.3d 1373, 1378–81 (Fed. Cir. 2014)`** | Two landmark rules: **(a)** Homelessness preventing receipt of the mailed BVA decision is an extraordinary circumstance causing late filing; and **(b)** Veterans law applies the **"stop-clock" standard**, requiring proof of due diligence **only *during* the period of extraordinary circumstances** to be tolled—*not* throughout the entire residual `120-day` period after the impediment ends! |
| **3. Attorney Abandonment vs. Neglect** | ***Sneed v. Shinseki*, `737 F.3d 719, 726–28 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`** *vs.* ***Nelson v. Nicholson*, `489 F.3d 1380, 1383 (Fed. Cir. 2007)`** | Under *Sneed*, **complete attorney abandonment** (e.g., counsel abruptly terminating representation immediately before the `120-day` deadline expires, leaving the veteran unable to retain substitute counsel or file) constitutes an extraordinary circumstance. By contrast, under *Nelson*, **"garden-variety" attorney negligence** (missed calendar reminders, late mailing by active counsel) is imputed to the client and **never** tolls `§ 7266(a)`. |
| **4. Timely Misfiling at VA & Postal Anomalies** | ***Santana-Venegas v. Principi*, `314 F.3d 1293, 1297–98 (Fed. Cir. 2002)`**; ***James v. Wilkie*, `917 F.3d 1368, 1372–74 (Fed. Cir. 2019)`** | Under *Santana-Venegas* (and *Jaquay v. Principi*, `304 F.3d 1276 (Fed. Cir. 2002) (en banc)`), misfiling a written expression of intent to appeal to the Court at the **VARO where the claim originated within `120 days`** tolls the clock. Under *James*, the Federal Circuit barred rigid *per se* exclusions (reversing CAVC's categorical rejection of a fallen residential mailbox flag on Day 120): **a veteran has zero duty to file prior to Day 120**, and CAVC must conduct a flexible, case-specific equitable inquiry. |

### C. Board Motion for Reconsideration Abatement (*Rosler* & *Linville*) vs. AMA Supplemental Claims
When a final Board decision contains an obvious error—or when `120 days` have not yet elapsed—practitioners must distinguish **three** post-Board filings whose clock mechanics differ radically:

1. **Motion for BVA Reconsideration Within `120 Days` (`38 U.S.C. § 7103`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 20.1000–20.1004`):**
   * Under ***Rosler v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 241, 249 (1991)`** and ***Linville v. West*, `165 F.3d 1382, 1385–86 (Fed. Cir. 1999)`** (enforcing `38 C.F.R. § 20.1002`'s USPS postmark rule on reconsideration filings), if a claimant files a Motion for Reconsideration with the Board **within `120 days`** of the mailing of the Board decision, **the finality of the Board decision is abated**.
   * If the Board Chairman denies reconsideration, a **brand-new `120-day` CAVC NOA clock** begins on the date the Chairman mails notice of the denial! If the claimant then files a CAVC NOA within `120 days` of the Chairman's denial, the CAVC has jurisdiction over the underlying Board decision (and may review the Chairman's denial to the extent permitted under *Mayer v. Brown*, `37 F.3d 618 (Fed. Cir. 1994)`).
   * *Post-120-Day Trap:* A Motion for BVA Reconsideration may be filed at *any* time (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1002(b)`), **but** if filed **more than `120 days`** after the BVA decision (absent equitable tolling of the initial gap), its denial by the Chairman **does NOT revive CAVC jurisdiction** over the underlying BVA decision!
2. **AMA Post-Board Supplemental Claim Within `1 Year` (`38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)(2)(D)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(c)(3)`):**
   * Following *Military-Veterans Advocacy v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs (`MVA`)*, `7 F.4th 1110 (Fed. Cir. 2021)`, a claimant who receives an adverse AMA Board decision may preserve the continuous-pursuit effective date by filing a `VA Form 20-0995` Supplemental Claim supported by **new and relevant evidence** within **`1 year`** of the BVA decision ([effective-dates-and-staged-ratings-treatise.md](effective-dates-and-staged-ratings-treatise.md)).
   * **CRITICAL KILL-SWITCH:** Filing a `VA Form 20-0995` Supplemental Claim at the AOJ **does NOT abate or toll the `120-day` CAVC NOA deadline** under `§ 7266(a)`! *(Note: While `38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(d)` bars pursuing two administrative lanes simultaneously, **MVA v. Sec'y**, `7 F.4th at 1134` **invalidated `38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(b)`**'s former regulatory bar against filing a protective `§ 5108` Supplemental Claim while a federal-court appeal is pending).*
   * **Sequential Safety Valve (`38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)(2)(E)`):** Always prioritize CAVC review within `120 days` when the closed Board record contains reversible error (preserving `EAJA` fee recovery!). Even if the CAVC later affirms the Board denial, **`38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)(2)(E)` (`38 C.F.R. § 3.2500(c)(4)`)** grants the veteran a fresh **`1-year` window after CAVC disposition** to file a `VA Form 20-0995` Supplemental Claim with new and relevant evidence while still keeping the original effective date alive ([ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md), [attorney-fees-5904-and-eaja-practice.md](attorney-fees-5904-and-eaja-practice.md)).

## 3. CAVC Standards of Review, `Rule 33` JMPR Engineering & Outright Reversal (`38 U.S.C. § 7261`)

### A. Statutory Standards-of-Review Matrix (`38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)–(c)`)

| Issue Type on Appeal | Statutory Hook | CAVC Standard of Review | Controlling Authorities & Guardrails |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Questions of Law, Statutory & Regulatory Construction** | `38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(1)` | ***De novo*** (zero agency deference) | ***Lane v. Principi*, `339 F.3d 1331, 1339 (Fed. Cir. 2003)`** (reinforced post-*Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, `603 U.S. 369 (2024)`). |
| **Adverse Findings of Material Fact** | `38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(4)` & `(c)` | **`Clearly erroneous`** | ***Gilbert v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 49, 52–53 (1990)`**: Set aside or reversed when there is no plausible basis in the record or the Court holds a "definite and firm conviction that a mistake has been committed" (*Deloach*). Trial *de novo* of facts is barred (`§ 7261(c)`). |
| **`§ 5107(b)` "Approximate Balance" Weighing** | `38 U.S.C. §§ 5107(b), 7261(b)(1)` | **`Clear error`** on evidentiary weighing (*de novo* only if BVA misstated legal test) | ***Bufkin v. Collins*, `604 U.S. 369 (2025)`**: `§ 7261(b)(1)`'s directive to "take due account" of VA's application of the benefit of the doubt does **not** create *de novo* appellate reweighing; factual weight assessments are reviewed for clear error. |
| **Board Decisions Adjudicating CUE Motions (`§§ 5109A, 7111`)** | `38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(3)(A)` | **`Arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with law`** | ***Russell v. Principi*, `3 Vet. App. 310, 315 (1992) (en banc)`**. *(Note: Whether a CUE motion satisfied pleading specificity or whether the Board misinterpreted a statute/regulation remains reviewed de novo).* |
| **Adequacy of Reasons or Bases** | `38 U.S.C. § 7104(d)(1)` | **Legal adequacy** enabling claimant understanding & judicial review | *Gilbert*, `1 Vet. App. at 56–57`; *Caluza v. Brown*, `7 Vet. App. 498, 506 (1995)`, *aff'd*, `78 F.3d 604 (Fed. Cir. 1996)` (must address all material favorable evidence and credibility). |
| **Rule of Prejudicial Error (Harmless Error)** | `38 U.S.C. § 7261(b)(2)` | **Appellant bears ordinary civil burden** of showing prejudice, **WITHOUT initial CAVC fact-finding** | ***Shinseki v. Sanders*, `556 U.S. 396, 406–11 (2009)`** **bounded by** ***Tadlock v. McDonough*, `5 F.4th 1327, 1334–38 (Fed. Cir. 2021)`**: CAVC **cannot make initial factual findings** (e.g., weighing conflicting medical or lay evidence in the first instance) to deem a Board error "harmless"! |

### B. `CAVC Rule 33` Joint Motion for Partial Remand (`JMPR`) Engineering & The *Carter* Waiver Trap
Prior to merits briefing, `CAVC Rule 33` mandates a pre-briefing settlement conference between appellant's counsel and VA OGC Group VII counsel based on a structured **Rule 33 Memorandum**. Roughly `65%–75%` of CAVC appeals resolve via a negotiated **Joint Motion for Remand (`JMR`)** or **Joint Motion for Partial Remand (`JMPR`)**—which confers "prevailing party" status for **EAJA attorney fees (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)`)** provided the remand is predicated on administrative error ([cavc-appeal-eaja-and-fee-compliance-gate.md](../checklists/cavc-appeal-eaja-and-fee-compliance-gate.md)).

> [!CAUTION]
> **The *Carter* Restrictive-JMPR Waiver Trap (*Carter v. Shinseki* / *Carter v. McDonald*):**
> In ***Carter v. Shinseki*, `26 Vet. App. 534, 541–43 (2014)`**, *vacated on other grounds sub nom.* ***Carter v. McDonald*, `794 F.3d 1342, 1346–47 (Fed. Cir. 2015)`**, the courts established a dangerous trap for careless appellate practitioners. In *Carter*, counsel negotiated a narrow JMR focused on a single evidentiary notice point while omitting other substantive errors in the Board's decision. Although the Federal Circuit ultimately vacated in *Carter v. McDonald*, `794 F.3d at 1346–47`, because VA failed to mail the post-remand 90-day submission letter to the veteran's new attorney (holding that burying an expired notice letter inside a voluminous C-file copy did not cure defective notice), both decisions underscore that **a negotiated JMPR operates as a bilateral stipulation defining the scope of the Board's remand mandate**. If counsel stipulates to a narrow, closed-ended JMPR ("the Board erred *solely* by failing to address X") without reserving open argument/evidence rights—and then fails to raise additional theories on remand—omitted errors can be deemed **abandoned or foreclosed** in a subsequent appeal!

Every firm-approved `Rule 33` JMPR **must** enforce four contractual clauses:
1. **Targeted Partial Vacatur & *Medrano* Shield:** Explicitly vacate **only** those portions of the Board decision that denied the appealed benefits, and state expressly that **all grants and favorable findings of fact remain undisturbed and binding** (`38 U.S.C. § 5104A`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.104(c)`; *Medrano v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 165, 170 (2007)`).
2. **Predicate Administrative Error Recital (EAJA Lock):** State clear Board legal or `§ 7104(d)(1)` reasons-or-bases error in the body of the JMPR (never stipulate to a purely "equitable" remand without error, which destroys EAJA "prevailing party" status under *Sumner v. Principi*, `15 Vet. App. 256 (2001) (en banc)`).
3. **Anti-*Carter* Non-Waiver & Open-Submission Clause:** Expressly stipulate: *"Nothing in this Joint Motion waives any other factual or legal error; on remand, Appellant is free to submit additional evidence and argument (`38 C.F.R. § 20.800` / post-remand window), and the Board must conduct a critical reexamination of the record and address all issues reasonably raised by the appellant or the evidence of record (`Fletcher v. Derwinski`, `1 Vet. App. 394, 397 (1991)`)."*
4. ***Stegall* Compliance Mandate:** Incorporate an express instruction that the Board and AOJ must ensure substantial compliance with the remand directives under ***Stegall v. West*, `11 Vet. App. 268, 271 (1998)`**—establishing reversible legal error if VA ignores the JMPR's exam or rationale instructions on remand.

### C. When to Reject OGC's Remand Offer and Brief for Outright CAVC Reversal
When VA OGC spots a fatal flaw in a Board denial where the record overwhelmingly favors the veteran, OGC routinely offers a `Rule 33` remand so the Board can "provide an adequate statement of reasons or bases" or "obtain a clarifying medical addendum." **Accepting an unnecessary remand costs the veteran `1–3+ years` of remand churn and hands VA a roadmap to manufacture negative evidence.**

Counsel must **reject a JMPR and brief for outright reversal (`38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(4)`)** in two primary scenarios:

1. **Uncontroverted Record / Only One Permissible View (*Deloach*, *Gutierrez* & *Adams*):**
   * ***Deloach v. Shinseki*, `704 F.3d 1370, 1379–81 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`**: Construing Congress's 2002 addition of `"or reverse"` to `38 U.S.C. § 7261(a)(4)`, the Federal Circuit held that where the Board has performed the necessary fact-finding, its adverse finding is clearly erroneous, and the uncontroverted evidence of record permits **only one permissible view of the evidence**, the CAVC has the full statutory power and duty to **reverse outright** and order an award of benefits.
   * ***Gutierrez v. Principi*, `19 Vet. App. 1, 8–9 (2004)`**: Reversing a Board denial outright because all competent medical and lay evidence of record supported entitlement, making reversal—not remand—mandatory.
   * ***Adams v. Principi*, `256 F.3d 1318, 1321–22 (Fed. Cir. 2001)`**: While CAVC may remand to clarify a genuinely ambiguous exam, **the CAVC may NOT remand merely to give VA a "second bite at the apple" to develop new negative medical evidence** to shore up an unsupported denial!
2. **Failed Affirmative VA Burden (*Horn* Soundness & `§ 3.344` Void Rating Reductions):**
   * Whenever **VA—not the veteran—bears the affirmative burden of proof**, and the existing record fails as a matter of law to satisfy VA's heightened standard, **reversal is mandatory as a matter of law**:
     * **Presumption of Soundness (`38 U.S.C. § 1111`):** In ***Horn v. Shinseki*, `25 Vet. App. 231, 243–44 (2012)`**, VA's medical opinion legally failed to prove by *clear and unmistakable evidence* both preexistence and non-aggravation. The CAVC **reversed** the Board's finding rather than remanding for a new VA medical opinion, holding VA does not get a remand to cure its failure to carry an affirmative rebuttal burden ([service-connection-direct-soundness-aggravation.md](service-connection-direct-soundness-aggravation.md)).
     * **Void Rating Reductions & Severance (`38 C.F.R. §§ 3.105(d)–(e), 3.343, 3.344`):** Where VA reduced a stabilized (`>= 5-year`) or total evaluation without demonstrating sustained material improvement under the ordinary conditions of life and work, the reduction is **void *ab initio***, requiring **outright CAVC reversal and retroactive reinstatement** from the effective date of the unlawful reduction (*Kitchens v. Brown*, `7 Vet. App. 320, 325 (1995)`; [Playbook 05: Void Rating Reductions](../playbooks/05-void-rating-reductions-stabilized-ratings.md)).

## 4. Federal Circuit & Supreme Court Review (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7292, 502`)

### A. The Jurisdictional Wall of `38 U.S.C. § 7292(d)(2)`
Appealing an adverse CAVC decision to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit crosses the sharpest jurisdictional razor's edge in federal appellate practice:
* **Affirmative Grant (`38 U.S.C. § 7292(a), (c), (d)(1)`):** The Federal Circuit has exclusive jurisdiction to review a CAVC decision with respect to the validity of a decision of the Court on a **rule of law** (`Pub. L. 107-330, § 402`; *Morgan v. Principi*, `327 F.3d 1357, 1361 (Fed. Cir. 2003)`) or the validity or **interpretation of any statute or regulation** relied upon by the CAVC.
* **Absolute Fact & Law-to-Fact Prohibition (`38 U.S.C. § 7292(d)(2)`):** Except to the extent an appeal presents a **constitutional issue**, the Federal Circuit **"may not review `(A)` a challenge to a factual determination, or `(B)` a challenge to a law or regulation as applied to the facts of a particular case"** (*Conway v. Principi*, `353 F.3d 1369, 1372 (Fed. Cir. 2004)`; *Fagan v. Shinseki*, `573 F.3d 1282, 1287 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`).

### B. Issue-Framing Engineering to Defeat DOJ Motions to Dismiss
Department of Justice (`DOJ`) Commercial Litigation Branch counsel move to dismiss a substantial percentage of veterans' Federal Circuit appeals under `§ 7292(d)(2)`. Survival turns entirely on **isolating a pure rule-of-law or statutory/regulatory construction error** in the CAVC's opinion rather than disputing record weight:

| Dismissed Framing (Barred by `§ 7292(d)(2)`) | Jurisdiction-Unlocking Legal-Standard Framing (`§ 7292(a)`) | Precedential Federal Circuit Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"CAVC erred in finding the veteran's combat lay statement outweighed by post-service records."* | *"VA's regulation is facially invalid because it imposes an evidentiary burden conflicting with `38 U.S.C. § 1154(b)`'s statutory combat-relaxation mandate."* | ***Jensen v. Brown*, `19 F.3d 1413, 1415–17 (Fed. Cir. 1994)`** |
| *"CAVC erred in affirming that the veteran's knee pain was not a compensable condition."* | *"CAVC misinterpreted `'disability'` in `38 U.S.C. § 1110` by requiring a formal pathological diagnosis rather than functional impairment of earning capacity."* | ***Saunders v. Wilkie*, `886 F.3d 1356, 1360–61 (Fed. Cir. 2018)`** |
| *"CAVC erred in failing to apply the benefit of the doubt (`§ 5107(b)`) to the PTSD evidence."* | *"CAVC misinterpreted `'approximate balance'` in `38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)` by requiring an exact mathematical tie rather than nearly equal evidence."* | ***Lynch v. McDonough*, `21 F.4th 776, 779–82 (Fed. Cir. 2021) (en banc)`** |

### C. Strict Article III Deadlines & Direct `38 U.S.C. § 502` Rulemaking Petitions
* **Strict Jurisdictional `60-Day` Federal Circuit NOA (`38 U.S.C. § 7292(a)`; `28 U.S.C. § 2107(b)`; `Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(1)(B)`):** Unlike CAVC's `120-day` `§ 7266(a)` claim-processing deadline (*Henderson*), the **`60-day` deadline** from entry of CAVC judgment to file a Notice of Appeal to the Federal Circuit is **strictly jurisdictional** under *Bowles v. Russell*, `551 U.S. 205, 209–14 (2007)` and **cannot be equitably tolled**! (A petition for writ of certiorari to the U.S. Supreme Court under `38 U.S.C. § 7292(c)` and `28 U.S.C. § 1254(1)` must be filed within **`90 days`** of Federal Circuit judgment per `Sup. Ct. R. 13.1`).
* **Direct Federal Circuit Pre-Enforcement Challenges (`38 U.S.C. § 502`):** Under `38 U.S.C. § 502`, veterans' organizations and affected claimants may bypass administrative adjudication altogether and petition the Federal Circuit **directly** to invalidate substantive VA regulations, interpretive rules, or generally applicable `M21-1` Adjudication Procedures Manual provisions (`5 U.S.C. §§ 552(a)(1), 553`).
  * ***NOVA v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs*, `981 F.3d 1360, 1365–69 (Fed. Cir. 2020) (en banc)`:** Held that `(1)` generally applicable `M21-1` manual rules are reviewable interpretive rules under `§ 502`, and `(2)` `§ 502` petitions are governed by the **`6-year` statute of limitations in `28 U.S.C. § 2401(a)`**—striking down former `Fed. Cir. Rule 15(f)`'s `60-day` deadline!
  * ***MVA v. Secretary of Veterans Affairs*, `7 F.4th 1110 (Fed. Cir. 2021)`:** Exercising `§ 502` jurisdiction to invalidate unlawful AMA regulations, including VA's `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i)` ban on attorney fees for CUE motions and non-continuous Supplemental Claims ([attorney-fees-5904-and-eaja-practice.md](attorney-fees-5904-and-eaja-practice.md)).

> [!WARNING]
> **Bitemporal *George v. McDonough* Gate (`596 U.S. 740 (2022)`; `38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`):** Winning a Federal Circuit `§ 502` regulatory invalidation (*MVA*) or `§ 7292` reinterpretation (*Saunders*, *Barry*, *Hamill*) applies with full retroactive force to **all open, pending, timely-appealed, `§ 3.156(b)`, and `§ 3.156(c)` claims** (*Harper v. Virginia Dep't of Taxation*, `509 U.S. 86, 97 (1993)`). However, under ***George v. McDonough*, `596 U.S. 740 (2022)`** and `38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`, **that judicial invalidation or reinterpretation CANNOT be used to attack a prior final RO or Board decision via CUE (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5109A, 7111`)** if the adjudicator faithfully applied the regulation or settled interpretation extant when the prior decision became final ([authority-verification-log.md](authority-verification-log.md), [board-cue-pre-filing-gate.md](../checklists/board-cue-pre-filing-gate.md)).

## 5. Extraordinary Writs of Mandamus (`28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)`) & `CAVC Rule 23` Class Actions

### A. All Writs Act Mandamus (`28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)`): *Cheney*, *Martin* (`TRAC`) & *Wolfe*
Under the **All Writs Act (`28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)`)**, the CAVC may issue extraordinary writs of mandamus "in aid of its prospective appellate jurisdiction" wherever agency action or inaction frustrates a claimant's path to a reviewable Board decision (*Cox v. West*, `149 F.3d 1360, 1363 (Fed. Cir. 1998)`). Every petitioner must satisfy the three-prong test of ***Cheney v. U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia*, `542 U.S. 367, 380–81 (2004)`**:
1. **Clear and Indisputable Right:** The petitioner has a clear and indisputable legal right to the relief sought;
2. **No Adequate Alternative Remedy:** The petitioner lacks adequate alternative means (such as an ordinary appeal) to obtain the relief; and
3. **Equitable Appropriateness:** The issuing court, in its discretion, concludes the writ is appropriate under the circumstances.

#### 1. Unreasonable Agency Delay: The Six-Factor `TRAC` Standard (*Martin v. O'Rourke*)
For nearly twenty years, the CAVC evaluated delay petitions under *Costanza v. West*, `12 Vet. App. 133, 134 (1999)`, requiring proof of an "arbitrary refusal to act." In ***Martin v. O'Rourke*, `891 F.3d 1338, 1344–49 (Fed. Cir. 2018)`**, the Federal Circuit **abrogated *Costanza*** and mandated the six-factor balancing framework of *Telecommunications Research & Action Center v. FCC (`TRAC`)*, `750 F.2d 70, 80 (D.C. Cir. 1984)`:
1. The time agencies take to make decisions must be governed by a **"rule of reason"**;
2. Any **statutory timetable** enacted by Congress supplies content for that rule of reason;
3. Delays are **far less tolerable when human health and welfare are at stake** (weighing heavily in veterans' benefits claims);
4. The effect of expediting delayed action on agency activities of a **higher or competing priority**;
5. The **nature and extent of the interests prejudiced** by the delay; and
6. **Zero Bad-Faith Requirement:** The Court **need not find any agency impropriety or bad faith** behind agency lassitude to hold delay unreasonable! *(Note: Under Mote v. Wilkie, `976 F.3d 1337, 1343–46 (Fed. Cir. 2020)`, CAVC cannot summarily dismiss a delay petition without applying the TRAC factors merely because VA took a minor ministerial step after the petition was filed).*

#### 2. The "No Substitute for Appeal" Kill-Switch (*Wolfe* & *Lamb*)
Mandamus **cannot** be used to short-circuit an available administrative appeal:
* ***Lamb v. Principi*, `284 F.3d 1378, 1384 (Fed. Cir. 2002)`** and ***Wolfe v. McDonough*, `28 F.4th 1348, 1354–59 (Fed. Cir. 2022)`**: In *Wolfe*, even though the Federal Circuit agreed on the statutory merits that `38 C.F.R. § 17.1005(a)(5)` unlawfully barred reimbursement of private-insurance **coinsurance** under `38 U.S.C. § 1725` (while upholding exclusion of deductibles), the Federal Circuit **reversed the CAVC's mandamus writ and class certification** under *Cheney* Prong 2 because claimants possessed an **adequate ordinary remedy** via direct administrative appeal to the BVA and CAVC! See [Playbook 22: Emergency Medical Reimbursement (`§§ 1725/1728`) & PCAFC](../playbooks/22-emergency-medical-reimbursement-1725-1728-and-pcafc.md).

### B. `CAVC Rule 23` Aggregate Litigation & Landmark Class Actions
In ***Monk v. Shulkin*, `855 F.3d 1312, 1318–21 (Fed. Cir. 2017)`**, the Federal Circuit reversed nearly three decades of CAVC refusal to hear aggregate claims, holding that the CAVC possesses full authority under the All Writs Act (`28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)`), `38 U.S.C. § 7264(a)`, and inherent judicial power to certify **class actions**—prompting promulgation of **`CAVC Rule 23`**. Four landmark precedents define the strategic boundaries of `CAVC Rule 23` practice:

| Precedent | Procedural Vehicle | Core Jurisdictional & Substantive Holding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ***Godsey v. Wilkie*, `31 Vet. App. 207 (2019)`** | `28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)` Mandamus Class | Certified a nationwide class of legacy appellants waiting **`> 18 months`** after filing a timely Substantive Appeal (`VA Form 9`) for VA to certify and transfer their appeals to the Board (`38 C.F.R. § 19.35`), granting classwide `TRAC` relief ordering certification or affirmative explanation. |
| ***Skaar v. McDonough*, `48 F.4th 1323 (Fed. Cir. 2022)`** | `38 U.S.C. § 7252(a)` **Direct Appeal** Class *(Vacated in part)* | **CRITICAL JURISDICTIONAL LIMIT:** In a `§ 7252(a)` *appellate* class action (challenging Palomares radiation dose methodology), **CAVC lacks jurisdiction to include class members who have NOT obtained a final Board decision** (such as veterans with pending RO claims or veterans who never filed a claim)! Every member of an *appeal* class must satisfy `§ 7252(a)`'s Board-decision predicate—sharply distinguishing `§ 7252` appeal classes from `§ 1651(a)` mandamus classes protecting *prospective* jurisdiction! |
| ***Beaudette v. McDonough*, `93 F.4th 1361 (Fed. Cir. 2024)`** (*aff'g* `34 Vet. App. 95 (2021)`) | `28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)` Mandamus Class | Unanimously affirmed CAVC class certification and held that `38 U.S.C. § 1720G(c)(1)` ("medical determinations") does **NOT** bar Board (`§ 7104(a)`) and CAVC (`§ 7252(a)`) review of **PCAFC Family Caregiver** eligibility and stipend denials/revocations. *Why mandamus succeeded despite Wolfe:* VHA refused to accept NODs or route caregiver appeals to the Board at all, **blocking the gateway to ordinary appellate review** ([Playbook 22](../playbooks/22-emergency-medical-reimbursement-1725-1728-and-pcafc.md)). |
| ***Freund v. McDonough*, `114 F.4th 1371 (Fed. Cir. 2024)`** & ***Freund v. Collins*, `No. 21-4168 (Vet. App. class certified Mar. 18, 2026)`** | `28 U.S.C. § 1651(a)` Mandamus Class | **Defeating VA "Pick-Off" Mootness & Restoring Closed VACOLS Legacy Appeals:** After automated VACOLS sweeps silently deactivated tens of thousands of timely perfected legacy appeals (`VA Form 9`) due to mail-logging lags, VA reactivated the named petitioners' files and claimed mootness. In *Freund*, `114 F.4th at 1378–83`, the Federal Circuit vacated dismissal under the **"inherently transitory" mootness exception** and held systemic VACOLS closures satisfied `Rule 23` commonality and adequacy. On **March 18, 2026**, CAVC certified the nationwide class (`Dec. 12, 1990 – Feb. 6, 2025` VACOLS closures for purported lack of timely Substantive Appeal), establishing a mandatory **Audit Track (`~28,258` files)** and **Notice Track (`~64,599` files)** that restore **original legacy claim effective dates** ([forms-and-codesheet-lexicon.md](forms-and-codesheet-lexicon.md), [ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md)). |
