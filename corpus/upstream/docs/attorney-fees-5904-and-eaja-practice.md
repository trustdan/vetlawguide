# Accreditation, `38 U.S.C. § 5904` Contingency Fees, Reasonableness Litigation & Equal Access to Justice Act (`EAJA`, `28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)`) Practice Treatise

Ethical, compliant revenue architecture allows a Veterans Law firm to litigate multi-decade retroactive claims and appellate precedents sustainably while maximizing the veteran's or survivor's net recovery. Unlike general civil contingency practice, Veterans Law representation is governed by a **dual federal statutory regime**: **`38 U.S.C. §§ 5901–5905`** and **`38 C.F.R. Part 14`** regulate agency accreditation, power of attorney (`POA`), and past-due contingency/non-direct-pay fees, while the **Equal Access to Justice Act (`EAJA`, `28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)`)** shifts hourly litigation fees onto the federal government at the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (`CAVC`) and Federal Circuit—linked together by the mandatory **`Pub. L. 102-572, § 506(c)` `"same work"` lesser-fee client refund rule**.

## 1. Accreditation, Power of Attorney (`VA Form 21-22a`) & Prohibition on Unaccredited `"Claim Sharks"`

 *(Statutory Crosswalk Note: Enacted in `Pub. L. 85-857` (`1958`) as **`former 38 U.S.C. §§ 3401, 3404, and 3405`**, these provisions were renumbered **`38 U.S.C. §§ 5901, 5904, and 5905`** on **May 7, 1991** by `Pub. L. 102-40, § 402(b)(1)`, `105 Stat. 187, 238`; see [Title 38 Statutory Crosswalk](title-38-statutory-crosswalk.md)).*

### A. Prohibition on Unaccredited Representation (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5901, 5905`)
Under **`38 U.S.C. § 5901`**, **no individual may act as an agent or attorney in the preparation, presentation, or prosecution of any claim under laws administered by the Secretary unless formally recognized (`accredited`) by VA**.
* **The Unaccredited `"Claim Shark"` Bar:** Unaccredited for-profit "consulting," "DBQ mill," or "claim coaching" companies are **categorically barred** by `§§ 5901` and `5904(c)(1)` from preparing or prosecuting VA benefit claims or charging fees on initial claims—even if disguised as "medical consulting" or deferred subscription contracts. Under **`38 U.S.C. § 5905`**, whoever wrongfully withholds from any claimant or beneficiary any part of a benefit or claim allowed and due is subject to criminal misdemeanor penalties, and accredited practitioners are barred under **`38 C.F.R. § 14.632`** (Standards of Conduct) from splitting fees with or assisting unlawful unaccredited claim-preparation schemes.

### B. OGC Accreditation & Mandatory Post-Accreditation CLE Windows (`38 U.S.C. § 5904(a)`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.629(b)`)
Attorneys (members in good standing of a State bar, `VA Form 21a`) and claims agents (who additionally pass OGC's written Veterans Law examination and background check) must obtain formal accreditation from the **VA Office of the General Counsel (`OGC`)** *before* representing a claimant on a VA benefit claim (`38 C.F.R. § 14.629(b)(1)`). To maintain accreditation under **`38 C.F.R. § 14.629(b)(1)(iii)–(iv)`** (`73 FR 29852`, pub. May 22, 2008, **eff. June 23, 2008**):
1. **Initial `3-Hour` CLE Window (`§ 14.629(b)(1)(iii)` — Critical Timing Trap!):** Both newly accredited attorneys and claims agents must complete at least **`3 hours` of State-bar-approved qualifying CLE** covering core Veterans Law topics (representation before VA, claims procedures, basic eligibility, right to appeal, `Ch. 11` disability compensation, `Ch. 13` DIC, and `Ch. 15` pension) **during the first `12-month` period *following* the date of initial accreditation by VA**.
   * **Practitioner Kill-Switch:** Because `§ 14.629(b)(1)(iii)` expressly mandates completion *"during the first 12-month period **following** the date of initial accreditation,"* **OGC rejects CLE courses taken *prior* to the date on the OGC accreditation letter!** Never rely on a pre-accreditation CLE course to satisfy Year 1 VA compliance; take the `3-hour` qualifying course *after* OGC issues initial accreditation and submit written certification (title, date, time, provider) to OGC with the annual `§ 14.629(b)(4)` good-standing certification.
2. **Biennial Ongoing `3-Hour` CLE (`§ 14.629(b)(1)(iv)`):** An additional **`3 hours`** of State-bar-approved Veterans Law CLE must be completed **not later than `3 years` from the date of initial accreditation, and every `2 years` thereafter**, certified in writing to OGC.

### C. Power of Attorney Mechanics (`VA Form 21-22a`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.631`)
* **Individual Appointment + Firm-Affiliate Authorization (`§ 14.631(a)`):** Unlike VSOs (`VA Form 21-22`), VA accredits **individual** attorneys and agents—not law firms as entities. **`VA Form 21-22a`** (*Appointment of Individual as Claimant's Representative*) must name an individually accredited attorney/agent, signed by both the claimant and the representative, with the firm/associate disclosure block checked so accredited partners, associates, and supervised paralegals (`§ 14.629(c)`) can access VBMS.
* **Single-POA Exclusivity & Limited-Claim Carveouts (`§ 14.631(e)–(f)`):** A claimant may hold only **one** unrestricted `POA` across all VA claims at a time; filing a new unrestricted `VA Form 21-22a` automatically **revokes** any prior VSO or attorney `POA` unless the new `21-22a` explicitly restricts representation to a specific claim/issue (`§ 14.631(f)`).
* **Automatic Extinguishment at Death (`§ 14.631(g)`):** A veteran's `VA Form 21-22a` and fee contract **terminate by operation of law at the moment of the veteran's death**—counsel must immediately execute a **brand-new `VA Form 21-22a` and a brand-new `§ 5904` fee agreement** signed by the eligible `§ 5121A` / `§ 5121` survivor ([Playbook 09](../playbooks/09-accrued-benefits-substitution-nehmer-estates.md); [`Survivor Triage Gate`](../checklists/survivor-post-mortem-triage-gate.md)).

## 2. Four-Era Chronology of `38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(1)` Fee-Charging Triggers (`1862–Present`)

Under **`38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(1)`**, charging a fee for VA administrative representation is lawful **only** after the statutory trigger in force for that procedural era has occurred:

| Era & Controlling Authority | Statutory Trigger Event Allowing Fees | Key Precedential Construction & Scope Traps |

National Ass'n of Radiation Survivors*, `473 U.S. 305 (1985)`** based on the informal, non-adversarial design of pre-VJRA VA proceedings. |

### Deep Synthesis: *Military-Veterans Advocacy v. Secretary* (`7 F.4th 1110 (Fed. Cir. 2021)`) & Immediate Supplemental / CUE Fee Eligibility
In VA's January 2019 AMA final rule (`84 FR 138, 174`), VA promulgated `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(c)(1)(i)`, which allowed fees on a `§ 5108` Supplemental Claim (`VA Form 20-0995`) *only* if continuously pursued within `1 year` of a prior decision—treating Supplemental Claims filed `> 1 year` after a final denial (as well as `§§ 5109A / 7111` CUE motions) as brand-new "initial" claims on which attorneys had to work for free unless VA denied the Supplemental Claim/CUE motion *again*.
* **The Federal Circuit's Invalidation (`7 F.4th at 1135–41`):** On direct `38 U.S.C. § 502` review, the Federal Circuit **vacated that restriction**, holding it violated the unambiguous text of amended **`38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(1)`**. Under the AMA statute, Congress shifted the fee-charging line to notice of `"the agency of original jurisdiction's initial decision under section 5104 with respect to the case"`. Because **every** `§ 5108` Supplemental Claim (`VA Form 20-0995`)—whether filed `6 months` or `30 years` after a prior denial—and **every** `§ 3.105(a)` / `§ 7111` CUE motion or `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration seeks readjudication/revision of an issue on which VA **already issued an initial AOJ decision**, **the statutory `§ 5904(c)(1)` prerequisite is already satisfied at intake!**
* **Operational Rule & Bitemporal *George* Gate:**
  * **All Open / Current Retainers:** Accredited attorneys and agents may enter into a valid `§ 5904` fee agreement and collect `20%` direct-pay withholding **immediately upon filing any `VA Form 20-0995` Supplemental Claim or `RO`/`BVA` CUE motion** attacking a prior denied or under-rated issue! *(Conversely, a **brand-new original `VA Form 21-526EZ` contention** never previously adjudicated by VA still lacks an initial AOJ decision and remains strictly fee-exempt until VA issues its initial `§ 5104` decision on that contention).*
  * **Bitemporal *George* Note (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`):** Under ***George v. McDonough*, `596 U.S. 740 (2022)`**, *MVA v. Sec'y* governs all post-July 30, 2021 fee determinations and timely-appealed/pending fee disputes (`open-claim`), whereas collaterally attacking a *prior final* pre-July 30, 2021 fee-denial decision via CUE faces `§ 20.1403(e)`'s change-in-interpretation bar.

## 3. Fee Agreement Architecture, Direct-Pay (`<= 20%`) vs. Non-Direct-Pay (`<= 33 1/3%`), Reasonableness & *Scates* Quantum Meruit

### A. Mandatory Contract Clauses & The Strict `30-Day` Dual-Office Filing Gate (`38 C.F.R. § 14.636(g)`)
Every `§ 5904` fee agreement must be in writing, signed by both the claimant and the accredited attorney/agent, list the claimant's name and VA file number, identify the specific terms of calculation, and comply with **`38 C.F.R. § 14.636(g)(1)–(3)`**:

1. **Direct-Pay Contingency Agreements (`<= 20%`, `38 U.S.C. § 5904(d)`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(g)(2), (h)`):**
   * Must be `(i)` **contingent solely on a favorable outcome** (claim resolved in a manner favorable to the claimant), `(ii)` **capped at `<= 20%`** of `"the total amount of any past-due benefits awarded on the basis of the claim"`, and `(iii)` authorize VA to withhold and pay the fee directly out of the cash past-due award.
   * **WHERE & WHEN TO FILE (`§ 14.636(g)(3)`):** Direct-pay fee agreements **MUST be filed with the Agency of Original Jurisdiction (`AOJ`)** (via VBA Centralized Mail Intake Center, P.O. Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444, `VA QuickSubmit`, or direct VBMS upload—**NOT** OGC!) **within `30 days` of execution**!
   * **Sovereign Immunity Kill-Switch (***Ravin v. Wilkie*, `956 F.3d 1346, 1350–52 (Fed. Cir. 2020)`**):** Missing `§ 14.636(g)(3)`'s `30-day` filing deadline strictly forfeits VA direct-pay withholding. Moreover, even if counsel timely complied and VA *erroneously* released `100%` of the retroactive award to the veteran without withholding the `20%` hold, *Ravin* holds that **sovereign immunity bars forcing VA to pay a second time out of public funds**—counsel's sole remedy is private collection from the client! *(For exact fee-base rules on incarcerated awards under **Snyder v. Nicholson**, `489 F.3d 1213 (Fed. Cir. 2007)`, debt-invalidation cash limits under **Gumpenberger v. Wilkie**, `973 F.3d 1379 (Fed. Cir. 2020)`, and `DFAS AEW` CRDP holds, see [Playbook 20, Section 6](../playbooks/20-backpay-offsets-crdp-crsc-severance-recoupment.md)).*
2. **Non-Direct-Pay Fee Agreements (`> 20%` up to `33 1/3%`, Hourly, Flat, Hybrid, or Disinterested Third-Party Payer; `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(f), (g)(3), (j)`):**
   * VA performs **zero** withholding or collection assistance; counsel collects directly from the claimant (or disinterested third-party payer under `§ 14.636(j)`).
   * **WHERE & WHEN TO FILE (`§ 14.636(g)(3)`):** Non-direct-pay fee agreements **MUST be filed with the VA Office of the General Counsel (`OGC (022D)`, Department of Veterans Affairs, 810 Vermont Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20420)** **within `30 days` of execution**!

### B. Three-Tier Reasonableness Presumptions (`38 C.F.R. § 14.636(e)–(f)`)
Under **`38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(3)(A)`** (and **`38 U.S.C. § 7263(c)–(d)`** for CAVC fee agreements), all fees must be **reasonable** as measured by the eight factors in **`38 C.F.R. § 14.636(e)`** (extent/type of services, complexity, level of skill/competence required, time spent, results achieved including prospective benefits, level of review to which the claim was taken and stage of retention, customary local/veterans-bar rates, and whether the fee is fixed or contingent):
* **Tier 1 — Presumption of Reasonableness (`<= 20%`, `§ 14.636(f)(1)`):** Any continuous-representation fee that **does not exceed `20%`** of past-due benefits (direct-pay or non-direct-pay) is **presumed reasonable**, rebuttable **only by clear and convincing evidence** that the fee is unreasonable!
* **Tier 2 — Neutral Middle Band (`> 20%` up to `33 1/3%`, `§ 14.636(e), (f)(1)`):** Non-direct-pay fees exceeding `20%` up to **`33 1/3%`** carry **NO regulatory presumption either way** (neither presumed reasonable nor presumed unreasonable)—if challenged before `OGC (022D)`, reasonableness is evaluated *de novo* on the eight `§ 14.636(e)` factors without VA withholding assistance.
* **Tier 3 — Presumption of Unreasonableness (`> 33 1/3%`, `§ 14.636(f)(1)`):** Any fee that **exceeds `33 1/3%`** of past-due benefits is **presumed unreasonable**, and the **attorney or agent bears the heavy burden of rebutting that presumption by clear and convincing evidence** before OGC!

### C. Discharged / Prior Counsel *Quantum Meruit* & Bifurcated Jurisdiction (*Scates v. Principi*, `282 F.3d 1362 (Fed. Cir. 2002)`)
When a claimant discharges an attorney/agent mid-stream (or retains sequential representatives across a multi-year claim) before the past-due award issues, ***Scates v. Principi*, `282 F.3d 1362, 1365–69 (Fed. Cir. 2002)`** (*aff'g Scates v. Gober*, `14 Vet. App. 64 (2000) (en banc)`) governs both the **substantive fee cap** and **adjudicative forum**:
1. **Substantive *Quantum Meruit* Allocation (`§ 14.636(e)–(f)`):** Implicit in every attorney-client contingency contract is the veteran's unconditional right to discharge counsel at any time without penalty. Once representation terminates prior to the favorable award, **the `<= 20%` presumption of reasonableness no longer applies**, and the discharged attorney is **not** automatically entitled to the full `20%` contingency share. Instead, VA must calculate a reasonable ***quantum meruit*** fee reflecting **the actual value of the discharged attorney's services and causal contribution to the ultimate award** under the `§ 14.636(e)` factors (weighing the work performed post-discharge by the veteran *pro se*, a VSO, or successor counsel!).
2. **Bifurcated Forum Jurisdiction (`RO/AOJ` Eligibility vs. `OGC (022D)` Reasonableness Review):**
   * **Step 1 — `RO/AOJ` Initial Jurisdiction Over Fee *Eligibility* (`Cox v. West`, `149 F.3d 1360 (Fed. Cir. 1998)`; *Scates*, `282 F.3d at 1367–68`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(h)`):** Whether a representative met the statutory prerequisites for direct-pay fee *entitlement* (`§ 5904(c)(1)` trigger, valid `30-day` filing, cash past-due base) must be decided **in the first instance by the RO/AOJ**, NOT by OGC or the BVA! Adverse AOJ *eligibility* or past-due math decisions are appealed through AMA review lanes (`HLR` `20-0996` or `Board NOD` `10182` within `1 year`).
   * **Step 2 — `60-Day` Allocation Hold & `120-Day` `OGC (022D)` *Reasonableness* Review (`38 U.S.C. § 5904(c)(3)`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(i)`):** When multiple direct-pay agreements or discharged counsel exist, the AOJ withholds a single global `20%` pool and issues a **Fee Allocation Notice** giving the claimant and all representatives **`60 days`** before VA releases funds, and **`120 days`** from notice of the final past-due amount to file a motion for **fee reasonableness / *quantum meruit* review with `OGC (022D)`** (`73 FR 29852` shifted reasonableness adjudication from BVA to OGC in the first instance, with OGC's final reasonableness decision then appealable to the **BVA**!).

## 4. Equal Access to Justice Act (`EAJA`, `28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)`) Practice at the CAVC & Federal Circuit

Under **`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A)`**, a court *shall* award reasonable hourly attorney fees and litigation expenses to a prevailing veteran/survivor in judicial review of VA action (`CAVC` or `Federal Circuit`), **paid by the government out of agency funds (NOT deducted from the veteran's benefits!)**, unless the government proves its position was substantially justified or special circumstances make an award unjust.

### A. The `"60 + 30 = 90-Day"` Window vs. Immediate `JMPR` Mandates (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(B)`; `CAVC Rules 36, 39, 41`)
An EAJA application must be filed **within `30 days` of `"final judgment"`**—defined by `28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(2)(G)` as a judgment that is *"final and not appealable"*. Mastering the **two distinct CAVC finality clocks** prevents fatal malpractice:

1. **Contested CAVC Single-Judge Memorandum (`nonprecedential, CAVC Rule 30(a)`) or Panel Decision (`60 + 30 = 90 Days`):**
   * **Day `0`:** CAVC enters judgment (`CAVC Rule 36`; note that after a single-judge decision, timely filing a `21-day` motion for reconsideration/panel review suspends judgment finality until resolved).
   * **Days `1–60`:** Statutory `60-day` window to appeal to the Federal Circuit runs (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7291(a), 7292(a)`). If no appeal is filed, CAVC judgment becomes final and **mandate issues (`CAVC Rule 41(a)`)** after Day `60`.
   * **Days `61–90` (`30-Day` EAJA Window):** Counsel has **`30 days` after judgment becomes final (`CAVC Rule 39(a)`)**—i.e., `90 days` total from judgment entry—to file the complete EAJA application! *(At the Federal Circuit, the `90-day` Supreme Court certiorari period yields a `90 + 30 = 120-day` total window unless mandate/finality is accelerated by stipulation).*
2. **Consent Joint Motion for Remand (`JMR` / `JMPR`) — Immediate `30-Day` Mandate Trap (`CAVC Rule 41(b)` & *Bly*):**
   * When the parties file a consent `JMR` or `JMPR` and waive appellate review so the Clerk's order **serves as the immediate mandate of the Court under `CAVC Rule 41(b)`**, the order is `"final and not appealable"` **on the exact day it is entered**!
   * **CRITICAL DEADLINE RULE:** There is **NO `60-day` wait** when an order issues an immediate `Rule 41(b)` consent mandate—**the `30-day` EAJA clock expires `30 calendar days` after the JMPR order date!** *(Historical Note: In ***Bly v. Shulkin*, `883 F.3d 1374 (Fed. Cir. 2018)`**, the Federal Circuit held that if a remand order does *not* legally foreclose appealability, the `60-day` window still applies under `§ 2412(d)(2)(G)`; however, modern CAVC `JMPR` practice expressly incorporates `Rule 41(b)` finality/mandate stipulations—**always** file EAJA within **`30 days`** of a `JMPR` order!).*
3. **Premature Applications & *Scarborough* Relation-Back Curing (*Scarborough v. Principi*, `541 U.S. 401 (2004)`):**
   * Under *Scarborough*, `541 U.S. at 413–19`, `§ 2412(d)(1)(B)`'s `30-day` deadline is a mandatory claim-processing rule (not subject-matter jurisdictional): if a timely EAJA application omits the required allegation that the government's position was `"not substantially justified"`, counsel may **cure the defect by relation-back amendment** after the `30-day` window closes.
   * Under CAVC practice (`CAVC Rule 39(a)`), if an EAJA application is lodged **prematurely** (after decision/judgment entry, *before* the `60-day` appeal period expires), the Clerk holds it and **treats it as timely filed on the date judgment becomes final**!

### B. Four Substantive EAJA Eligibility Elements
1. **Complete Itemized Application (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(B)`; `CAVC Rule 39`):** Contemporaneous tenth-of-an-hour (`0.1 hr`) billing records identifying exact dates, tasks, and timekeepers, excluding clerical overhead or unproductive/duplicative hours (*Hensley v. Eckerhart*, `461 U.S. 424 (1983)`).
2. **Net Worth `<= $2,000,000` (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(2)(B)`):** Individual appellant's net worth did not exceed **`$2,000,000`** on the date the CAVC Notice of Appeal was filed (satisfied via net-worth declaration or cross-referencing a granted CAVC *in forma pauperis* motion).
   * **Treasury Offset Check (***Astrue v. Ratliff*, `560 U.S. 586 (2010)`**):** Because `§ 2412(d)(1)(A)` awards fees to the `"prevailing party"` (the veteran), Treasury's `31 U.S.C. § 3716` Administrative Offset program intercepts EAJA awards if the veteran owes delinquent federal non-tax debt (e.g., defaulted federal student loans or SBA/child-support offsets); execute a written **EAJA Fee Assignment** with the client so VA/Treasury pays counsel directly whenever zero federal offset debt exists!
3. **`"Prevailing Party"` Status — The Administrative-Error Requirement:**
   * **Controlling Rule (***Sumner v. Principi*, `15 Vet. App. 256, 264–65 (2001) (en banc)`**, *aff'd sub nom. Vaughn v. Principi*, `336 F.3d 1351 (Fed. Cir. 2003)`):** A CAVC remand confers `"prevailing party"` status **only** if the remand was **predicated, explicitly or implicitly, upon administrative error**—either conceded by the Secretary in a `JMPR` or found by the Court!
   * **Qualifying Error Remands (***Kelly v. Nicholson*, `463 F.3d 1349, 1353–55 (Fed. Cir. 2006)`** & ***Zuberi v. Nicholson*, `19 Vet. App. 541, 546–48 (2006)`**):** Agency error need not prove entitlement on the ultimate disability merits—a remand for `§ 7104(d)(1)` inadequate reasons or bases, ignoring an alternative diagnostic/service-record theory (*Kelly*), or relying on an inadequate VA medical opinion (*Zuberi*) fully establishes prevailing-party status!
   * **Disqualifying Non-Error Remands (*Vaughn* & ***Robinson v. O'Rourke*, `891 F.3d 976, 980–83 (Fed. Cir. 2018)`**):** Remands based **solely** on `(a)` an **intervening / supervening change in statute, regulation, or precedent** issued after the BVA decision (*Vaughn*, `336 F.3d at 1355–60`; *Akers v. Nicholson*, `409 F.3d 1356 (Fed. Cir. 2005)`), `(b)` newly discovered post-BVA evidence, OR `(c)` directing the Board under *Maggitt* discretion to consider **an argument or issue raised for the first time at the CAVC** where the Board committed zero error in not addressing it *sua sponte* (*Robinson*, `891 F.3d at 980–83`) **forfeit `100%` of EAJA fees**! **Always anchor every `JMPR` in at least one concrete BVA `§ 7104(d)(1)` or `§ 5103A` administrative error!**
4. **Absence of `"Substantial Justification"` (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(1)(A)`):**
   * Once alleged by the appellant, the **Secretary bears the burden** of proving the government's position had a `"reasonable basis both in law and fact"` (***Pierce v. Underwood*, `487 U.S. 552, 565 (1988)`**) at **BOTH** the administrative (`BVA`) stage **AND** the judicial (`CAVC` OGC litigation) stage under the totality of circumstances (***Patrick v. Shinseki*, `668 F.3d 1325, 1330–33 (Fed. Cir. 2011)`**).

### C. `CPI-U` Rate Escalation, *Mannino* Midpoint Math, *Richlin* Paralegal Billing & `"Fees-on-Fees"`
* **Statutory `$125/hr` Base + `CPI-U` Escalation (`28 U.S.C. § 2412(d)(2)(A)`, `Pub. L. 104-121, § 232`, eff. `Mar. 29, 1996`):**
  $$\text{EAJA Attorney Hourly Rate} = \$125.00 \times \left(\frac{\text{Applicable Regional or National CPI-U}}{\text{March 1996 Base CPI-U}}\right)$$
  *(Capped at counsel's prevailing market rate; in 2024–2026 practice, `CPI-U` adjustments routinely yield `~$245–$260+/hr` depending on the regional/MSA `CPI-U` index).*
* ***Mannino* Anti-Interest Midpoint Rule (***Mannino v. West*, `12 Vet. App. 242, 244–45 (1999)`**):** Applying *today's* `CPI-U` index to hours worked in *earlier* years constitutes impermissible interest against the United States. Counsel must calculate the `CPI-U` multiplier using either `(1)` the separate historical `CPI-U` for each year/month services were rendered, or `(2)` the **single `CPI-U` index for the temporal midpoint** between the first and last dates of compensated appellate work!
* **Paralegal & Law-Clerk Prevailing Market Rates (***Richlin Security Service Co. v. Chertoff*, `553 U.S. 571, 581–90 (2008)`**):** Non-attorney paralegals, law clerks, and non-admitted practitioners supervised by CAVC counsel are billed at **prevailing market rates** (supported by *Laffey* / *USAO* / *Morimura* / local market surveys, capped below the `CPI-U` attorney rate), **never** restricted to the law firm's internal payroll cost!
* **`"Fees-on-Fees"` (***Commissioner, INS v. Jean*, `496 U.S. 154, 161–66 (1990)`**):** Reasonable hours spent drafting the initial EAJA application and litigating any government opposition to EAJA entitlement or quantum are **automatically compensable** without re-litigating substantial justification for the fee phase.

## 5. Mandatory `"Same Work"` Offset & Ethical Refund Rule (`Pub. L. 102-572, § 506(c)`)

### A. Controlling Federal Statutory Command (`28 U.S.C. § 2412 note`)
When Congress extended `EAJA` explicitly to the CAVC in the **Federal Courts Administration Act of 1992 (`Pub. L. 102-572, § 506(c)`, `106 Stat. 4506, 4513`, Oct. 29, 1992, codified at `28 U.S.C. § 2412 note`)**, it enacted an ironclad anti-double-dipping mandate (identical in structure to Social Security's `42 U.S.C. § 406(b)` / EAJA savings clause recognized in ***Gisbrecht v. Barnhart*, `535 U.S. 789, 796 (2002)`**):

> *"Section 5904(d) of title 38, United States Code, shall not prevent an award of fees and other expenses under section 2412(d) of title 28, United States Code. Section 5904(d) of title 38, United States Code, shall not apply with respect to any such award **but only if, where the claimant's attorney receives fees for the same work under both section 5904 of title 38, United States Code, and section 2412(d) of title 28, United States Code, the claimant's attorney refunds to the claimant the amount of the smaller fee**."*

### B. Unified Continuous Retainers vs. Segregated Representation Models (*Shaw* & *Carpenter*)
Understanding what constitutes **`"the same work"`** under ***Shaw v. Gober*, `10 Vet. App. 498, 503–05 (1997)`** and ***Carpenter v. Principi*, `15 Vet. App. 64, 74–79 (2001) (en banc)`** (*appeal dismissed for lack of jurisdiction*, `327 F.3d 1371 (Fed. Cir. 2003)`) is a mandatory ethical and trust-accounting gate for every Veterans Law firm:

| Representation Model | Does `Pub. L. 102-572, § 506(c)` Trigger? | Legal Rule & Mandatory Disbursement Protocol |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Model 1: Unified Continuous Representation (`RO/BVA -> CAVC -> Post-Remand Grant` by Same Counsel/Firm)** | **YES — MANDATORY DOLLAR-FOR-DOLLAR CLIENT REFUND!** | Under ***Carpenter*, `15 Vet. App. at 76`**, when counsel represents a claimant at the CAVC (earning `EAJA` fees on a remand) **and** continues representing the claimant on remand under a `§ 5904` contingency agreement to win past-due benefits on that claim, **the CAVC appeal and post-remand success constitute `"the same work"`** (advancing the single continuous claim to an award)! **Kill-Switch:** *Carpenter* (`15 Vet. App. at 76–79`) **invalidated `"unless remanded"` / "post-remand-only" carveout clauses** inserted into unified contingency agreements to evade the `EAJA` offset—keeping `100%` of both `EAJA` and `20%` of the full past-due award is **unlawful and per se "excessive and unreasonable"** (`38 U.S.C. § 7263(d)`; `38 C.F.R. § 14.636(f)`). **Action:** Immediately upon receiving the second fee, **refund `100%` of the smaller fee (`min(EAJA_fee, §5904_fee)`) directly to the claimant**! *(Note: Out-of-pocket court filing costs reimbursed as EAJA *expenses*—e.g., the `$50` CAVC filing fee—are not attorney "fees" and are not offset unless already paid by the client).* |
| **Model 2: Segregated CAVC-Only Referral Counsel (`EAJA Only`, `$0` `§ 5904`) + Separate Agency Merits Counsel (`§ 5904 Only`, `$0` `EAJA`)** | **NO *(if strictly segregated with zero fee-sharing!)*** | Firm A (agency counsel) handles RO/BVA representation under a `§ 5904` contingency retainer (`0` CAVC appearance, `$0` `EAJA`). For the CAVC appeal, the veteran retains independent CAVC specialist Firm B under a **court-only `EAJA`-only retainer** in which Firm B **expressly charges `$0` under `§ 5904` and waives any share of past-due benefits**, returning the case solely to Firm A upon CAVC mandate. Because Firm B receives *only* `EAJA` (for court work) and Firm A receives *only* `§ 5904` (for agency work) **with zero fee-splitting between Firm A and Firm B**, neither `"claimant's attorney"` receives dual fees for the same work. **Trap:** If Firm B takes *any* referral cut of Firm A's `20%` `§ 5904` fee—or if Firm A bills co-counsel `EAJA` hours—`§ 506(c)` **immediately attaches** and mandates refunding the overlapping smaller fee to the veteran! |
| **Model 3: Post-Remand-Only Initial Retention *(Pro Se or Prior Counsel at CAVC -> Brand-New Post-Mandate Agency Counsel)*** | **NO *(subject to `§ 14.636(e)/(i)` reasonableness)*** | Veteran wins a CAVC remand *pro se* (or via prior CAVC-only counsel who exits at mandate) and **retains a brand-new agency attorney for the first time *after* CAVC mandate** solely for post-remand development. Because the post-mandate attorney never received an `EAJA` fee, `§ 506(c)` does not apply to that attorney (though if prior CAVC counsel *also* filed a `§ 5904` agreement, VA allocates the single `20%` direct-pay cap under *Scates* / `§ 14.636(i)` and prior counsel must still offset its own `EAJA` recovery!). |

### C. Exact Trust/Operating Accounting Formula for Model 1 Refunds
Whenever a unified-representation claim grants retroactive past-due benefits after an earlier `EAJA` award on the same issue:
1. **Case A (`§ 5904` Contingency Fee `>` Prior `EAJA` Fee):** Suppose counsel previously received **`$6,800.00`** in CAVC `EAJA` fees (deposited into operating revenue when earned at the close of the CAVC case), and VA subsequently awards `$150,000.00` in past-due benefits on remand, disbursing **`$30,000.00`** (`20%`) directly to counsel under `§ 5904(d)`. Counsel **must immediately issue a `$6,800.00` refund check (`the smaller fee`) to the veteran** (leaving counsel with `$30,000.00` net total fees and boosting the veteran's net recovery from `$120,000.00` to **`$126,800.00`**—`84.53%` of the gross award!).
2. **Case B (Prior `EAJA` Fee `>` `§ 5904` Contingency Fee):** Suppose counsel previously received **`$6,800.00`** in `EAJA` fees, and the post-remand grant yields `$20,000.00` in past-due benefits (`20%` `§ 5904(d)` withholding = **`$4,000.00`**). Counsel **must immediately refund the entire `$4,000.00` `§ 5904(d)` payment (`the smaller fee`) to the veteran** (retaining the `$6,800.00` `EAJA` fee while the veteran nets **`100%` (`$20,000.00`)** of the past-due VA award!).
3. **Multi-Issue Proration Audit:** If a CAVC `EAJA` award covered Issues `A` and `B`, and VA grants past-due benefits *only* on Issue `A` while denying Issue `B`, counsel still received `EAJA` for the CAVC remand that produced Issue `A`'s award—refund the full overlapping `EAJA` fee (up to `min(EAJA, §5904_A)`) rather than risking an `OGC` / `CAVC` disciplinary violation by unilateral hair-splitting, and track any remaining credit so `EAJA` is never refunded more than once (`cumulative refunds <= total EAJA fee received`).
