# Procedural Vehicles for Piercing Finality ("Time-Machine" Mechanisms)

Under `38 U.S.C. § 5110(a)`, the default effective date of an award of disability compensation cannot precede the date VA received the claim (or Intent to File) that resulted in the grant. When a veteran was wrongly denied or underrated decades ago and failed to timely appeal, **finality attaches** (`38 U.S.C. §§ 7104, 7105`).

Securing multi-decade retroactive compensation requires invoking a specific statutory or regulatory **exception to finality**—or proving that **finality never legally attached in the first place**.

---

## 1. Clear and Unmistakable Error (CUE)

CUE is a collateral attack on an otherwise final Agency of Original Jurisdiction (RO/AOJ) or Board of Veterans' Appeals (BVA) decision. If established, the corrected decision takes effect **as if the original decision had been made properly on the date of the prior decision** (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5109A(b), 7111(b)`).

### A. RO CUE vs. Board CUE (Critical Jurisdictional Split)

| Feature | RO / AOJ CUE | Board (BVA) CUE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Statute & Regulation** | `38 U.S.C. § 5109A`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a)` | `38 U.S.C. § 7111`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 20.1400–20.1411` |
| **Where Filed** | Regional Office / AOJ (now via supplemental/motion filing to AOJ) | Directly with the Board of Veterans' Appeals |
| **Number of Motions Allowed** | Multiple distinct CUE theories may be raised over time if not previously decided on the merits | **ONE-SHOT RULE (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1409(c)`):** Once the Board issues a final decision on a CUE motion attacking a Board decision, **no further CUE motions** may ever be filed against that same Board issue! |
| **Pleading Specificity** | Specific allegation required (`Fugo`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a)(1)(vii)`) | Strict pleading requirements under `38 C.F.R. § 20.1404(b)`; dismissed without prejudice if facially non-specific, but *with* prejudice if decided on the merits |
| **Merger / Subsumption Trap** | **Subsumed if BVA later decided the same issue on the merits** (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1400(b)`; *Manning v. Principi*, 16 Vet. App. 534 (2002)) | Survives unless reversed/vacated by CAVC on that specific issue |

> [!CAUTION]
> **Always Check Subsumption & Complete the Board Pre-Filing Gate First:** If a 1973 RO decision underrated a gunshot wound, and a 1985 BVA decision reviewed and affirmed that exact rating on the merits, the 1973 RO decision merged into the 1985 BVA decision. Attacking the 1973 RO decision via `§ 3.105(a)` is jurisdictionally dead; you must verify whether CUE existed in the 1985 BVA decision—and complete the mandatory [`Board CUE Pre-Filing Gate`](../checklists/board-cue-pre-filing-gate.md) before filing any `38 U.S.C. § 7111` motion!

### B. The Strict Three-Prong Test (*Russell* & *Fugo*)
Under *Russell v. Principi*, 3 Vet. App. 310, 313–14 (1992) (en banc) and *Fugo v. Brown*, 6 Vet. App. 40, 43–44 (1993), the claimant must establish:
1. **Undebatable Factual or Legal Error:** Either the correct facts, **as they were known at the time**, were not before the adjudicator, OR the statutory/regulatory provisions **extant at the time** were incorrectly applied.
2. **Undebatable / Non-Judgmental Nature:** The error must be "undebatable"—reasonable minds could only conclude that the original decision was fatally flawed at the time it was rendered. Mere disagreement with how the adjudicator **weighed and evaluated** the evidence can *never* rise to CUE (`38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a)(1)(iii)`).
3. **Manifestly Changed Outcome:** Had the error not been made, the outcome would have **manifestly changed** at the time (i.e., resulted in service connection, a higher compensable evaluation, SMC, or an earlier effective date).

### C. Constructive Possession (*Bell v. Derwinski* & The July 21, 1992 Cutoff)
In CUE claims, the "record at the time" normally means documents physically in the C-file before the adjudicator. However, under *Bell v. Derwinski*, 2 Vet. App. 611 (1992) and `VAOPGCPREC 12-95`:
* **Decisions rendered ON OR AFTER July 21, 1992:** VA medical records generated by VA clinicians (e.g., VAMC treatment notes, VistA/CAPRI entries) and documents in VA's possession are **constructively part of the record** before the adjudicator—even if they were not physically associated with the paper C-file when the rating decision was signed, provided they existed prior to the decision and their relationship to the claim was reasonably apparent.
* **Decisions rendered BEFORE July 21, 1992:** *Bell*'s constructive possession doctrine **does not apply retroactively** to CUE attacks on pre-July 21, 1992 RO or BVA decisions (`VAOPGCPREC 12-95`; *Damrel v. Brown*, 6 Vet. App. 242 (1994)). For pre-July 21, 1992 decisions, the document must have been **actually in the claims folder** at the time of the decision (though look immediately to `38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)` informal-claim rules or `§ 3.156(c)` service records if the document was outside the C-file!).

### D. Categorical CUE Bars
* **Duty-to-Assist Failures Are NEVER CUE:** Failure to obtain medical records, schedule a C&P exam, or provide an adequate C&P opinion does not constitute CUE (*Cook v. Principi*, 318 F.3d 1334 (Fed. Cir. 2002) (en banc); *Caffrey v. Brown*, 6 Vet. App. 377 (1994)). It yields an *incomplete* record, not an *incorrect* application of law to a complete record.
* **Retroactive Application of Later Judicial Interpretations:** A later court decision Changing an established VA interpretation generally cannot support CUE unless the court held that the statutory text was unambiguous from inception (*George v. McDonough*, 596 U.S. 740 (2022) — invalidation of a regulation years later does not make prior reliance on that regulation CUE).
* **Benefit of the Doubt (`38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)`):** Failure to give the benefit of the doubt on equiponderant evidence is inherently a weighing error and cannot be CUE.

## 2. Newly Associated Service Department Records (`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)`)

`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)` is frequently **far more powerful than CUE** because it carries **no "undebatable" standard**, allows **full development and contemporary medical nexus opinions**, and still awards an effective date reaching back to the **original previously denied claim**.

### Core Mechanics (`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1) & (3)`)
At any time after VA issues a decision on a claim, if VA receives or associates **relevant official service department records** that existed and had not been associated with the claims file when VA first decided the claim, VA **shall reconsider** the claim (`§ 3.156(c)(1)`).
* **Effective Date Rule (`§ 3.156(c)(3)`):** An award made based *all or in part* on those newly associated records is effective on the **date entitlement arose** OR the **date VA received the previously decided claim**, whichever is **later**.
* **Retroactive Ratings Schedule (`§ 3.156(c)(4)`):** Where retroactive evaluation spans multiple decades, VA conducts a staged rating analysis across the entire retroactive period!

### Qualifying "Service Department Records"
* Missing Service Treatment Records (STRs), inpatient clinical records (as distinct from outpatient health folders—a classic trap!), Dental records, Entrance/Separation physicals.
* Unit morning reports, company rosters, ship deck logs, war diaries, after-action reports.
* Joint Services Records Research Center (JSRRC) / Armed ForcesCenter for Research of Unit Records (CRUR) stressor verification reports.
* DoD / military archival records (e.g., declassified chemical/radiation/herbicide exposure logs, Fort Sill or Edgewood Arsenal testing logs).
* Reconstructed records following the 1973 NPRC St. Louis fire (*38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)(1)(iii)*).

### Controlling Precedent
* ***Vigil v. Peake*, 22 Vet. App. 63 (2008):** Clarified that the 2005 revision to `§ 3.156(c)` confirms effective-date retroactivity back to the original claim whenever the grant is based "all or in part" on the newly associated service records—even if a modern C&P nexus exam was also required to connect the service record to a current diagnosis.
* ***Blubaugh v. McDonald*, 773 F.3d 1310, 1314 (Fed. Cir. 2014):** Critical limitation! `§ 3.156(c)` applies **only when VA actually reconsiders** a prior denial based on newly associated records that remedy the reason for the prior denial (or bear on a contested element). Furthermore, if VA already associated and evaluated that service record in an *intermediate* reopening denied for lack of current diagnosis, `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration is exhausted and will not carry a *later* grant back to the original claim.
* ***Emerson v. McDonald*, 28 Vet. App. 200, 208 (2016) & `M21-1 X.ii.2.B`:** Even if the service records were publicly available in an archive at the time of the earlier decision, what matters is when they were **associated with the veteran's VA claims file**. Crucially, *Emerson* and `M21-1` also compel `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration of an original denial **even after VA already granted service connection on a later date via a liberalizing statute or rule** (e.g., `38 C.F.R. § 3.304(f)` PTSD stressor relaxation or the PACT Act)!
* ***Kisor v. Wilkie*, 969 F.3d 1333 (Fed. Cir. 2020), *reh'g en banc denied sub nom. Kisor v. McDonough*, 995 F.3d 1316 (Fed. Cir. 2021):** Defined "relevant" under `§ 3.156(c)(1)`—to be "relevant," the newly associated record must speak to a matter in dispute (i.e., relate to the basis of the prior denial rather than an uncontested fact).

## 3. Unadjudicated & Still-Pending Claims

If a claim was legally filed (formally, informally, or reasonably raised) and **never validly adjudicated or noticed**—or if Legacy finality was abated within the 1-year appeal period—finality **never attached**. Even 40 years later, the veteran is pursuing an **open, original claim** under standard preponderance/benefit-of-the-doubt (`38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)`) rules—not CUE!

### A0. Post-Decision Evidence Within 1 Year Abating Legacy Finality (`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(b)` — *Beraud* & *Lang*)
Under `38 C.F.R. § 3.156(b)` (applicable strictly to **Legacy** claims prior to Feb. 19, 2019), new and material evidence received prior to the expiration of the **1-year appeal period** is considered as having been filed in connection with the claim that was pending at the beginning of the appeal period:
* ***Beraud v. McDonald*, 766 F.3d 1402 (Fed. Cir. 2014):** If a claimant submitted evidence within 1 year of a Legacy RO denial and VA never issued a determination on whether that submission was new and material, **the Legacy RO decision never became final**—even if VA later granted a "reopened" claim decades afterward!
* ***Lang v. Wilkie*, 971 F.3d 1348 (Fed. Cir. 2020):** Combined *Beraud* with *Bell* constructive possession! If a **VAMC generated clinical treatment records** relevant to the denied/rated condition within **1 year *after*** a post-July 21, 1992 Legacy RO decision, VA constructively "received" that evidence within the 1-year appeal window under `§ 3.156(b)`. If VA never adjudicated whether those VAMC notes were new and material, **finality never attached to the Legacy rating decision**!

### A. Defective Notice & Wrong-Address Mailings (Rebutting the Presumption of Regularity)
A rating decision or Statement of the Case (SOC) does not become final until VA mails notice to the claimant's **latest address of record** (and representative, if applicable) (`38 U.S.C. § 5104(a)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.103`). Similarly, VA cannot penalize a claimant under `38 C.F.R. § 3.655` for failing to report for a C&P exam if notice of the exam was defective.
* **The Presumption of Regularity:** Courts presume VA properly mailed notice on the date of the letter unless the claimant produces **clear evidence** to the contrary (*Ashley v. Derwinski*, 2 Vet. App. 307 (1992); *Mindenhall v. Brown*, 7 Vet. App. 271, 274 (1994)).
* **How the Presumption Is Rebutted:**
  * The C-file contains **more than one address**, and VA mailed the decision or C&P scheduling notice to an outdated address after the veteran informed VA (including VAMC records under *Woods v. Gober*, 14 Vet. App. 214 (2000) and *Cross v. Brown*, 9 Vet. App. 18 (1996)) of a newer address.
  * The envelope is in the C-file stamped **"Returned Undeliverable / Moved Left No Address"**, and VA failed to check other divisions/records in its possession for an updated address.
  * **Representative Mailing & Systemic Irregularity (*Romero v. Tran*, 33 Vet. App. 252 (2021)):** *Romero* specifically addressed whether an SOC was mailed to a claimant's **representative** (not a wrong-address C&P exam). It held that nonreceipt **plus** clear evidence of **systemic VA mailing irregularity** (e.g., practitioner declarations, NOVA evidence, GAO reports) bursts the presumption and shifts the burden to the Secretary to prove actual mailing/receipt. *(Caution: *Marion v. Collins*, No. 24-6964 (Vet. App. Dec. 23, 2025) is a **nonprecedential single-judge memorandum decision** under CAVC Rule 30(a) holding that counsel's bare affidavit of nonreceipt of an HLR decision without systemic or C-file corroboration does not burst regularity—do not cite *Marion* as binding precedent).*
* **VACOLS Systemic Sweeps (*Freund v. Collins*, No. 21-4168 (Vet. App. class certified Mar. 2026)):** Class action covering ~28,000+ legacy appeals improperly closed in VACOLS between Dec. 12, 1990 and Feb. 6, 2025 for a purportedly missing Substantive Appeal—always audit legacy appeals marked "withdrawn/closed" in VACOLS!

### B. Pre-March 24, 2015 Informal Claims & `38 C.F.R. § 3.157(b)` Hospital/Exam Reports
Prior to **March 24, 2015** (when `79 FR 57660` mandated standardized forms and created the Intent to File under `38 C.F.R. § 3.155`), **any written communication** indicating an intent to apply for benefits (`historical 38 C.F.R. § 3.155(a)`) could constitute an informal claim if it identified the benefit sought (and, for reopening/increase, if service connection was already in effect).
* **The Golden `§ 3.157(b)` Rule (Repealed March 24, 2015, preserved for historical claims):** For any condition **already service-connected** (even at 0% noncompensable!), the **date of admission to a VA or uniformed services hospital** or the **date of an outpatient VA examination report** showing worsening **automatically operated as an informal claim for an increased rating**! If VA received or generated that VAMC treatment report in 1978 showing worsening of a 0% service-connected disability and never issued a rating decision on it, an unadjudicated increased-rating claim remained pending from 1978 (unless terminated by a subsequent formal adjudication of that exact condition).
* **Reasonably Raised Claims & TDIU (*Roberson* & *Rice*):** Under *Roberson v. Principi*, 251 F.3d 1378 (Fed. Cir. 2001) and *Rice v. Shinseki*, 22 Vet. App. 447 (2009), VA must sympathetically read pro se submissions and evidence of record. Whenever a veteran seeks a higher evaluation for a service-connected disability and the record contains medical or lay evidence of **unemployability due to service-connected disabilities**, TDIU (`38 C.F.R. § 4.16`) is raised as part and parcel of the rating claim—no formal `VA Form 21-8940` is required to trigger the claim date (though VA will later request one in development).

### C. The Implicit Denial Doctrine vs. AMA Abolition (*Hamill v. Collins*, 2026)
Historically, VA defeated decades-old pending claims using the judge-made **implicit denial doctrine** (*Deshotel v. Nicholson*, 457 F.3d 1258 (Fed. Cir. 2006); *Adams v. Shinseki*, 568 F.3d 956 (Fed. Cir. 2009)): if VA adjudicated Claim A in a decision whose surrounding context would lead a reasonable person to understand that related Claim B was also rejected (even without mentioning Claim B explicitly), Claim B was deemed "implicitly denied" and became final.

* **Legacy Decisions (Before February 19, 2019):** *Deshotel* and *Adams* **still apply**—including to reasonably raised TDIU where a legacy decision denied a schedular rating increase (*Hampton v. McDonough*, 68 F.4th 1376 (Fed. Cir. 2023); *Steele v. Collins*, 135 F.4th 1353 (Fed. Cir. 2025), *cert. pet. pending*, `No. 25-1123`). Practitioners must analyze the timing, relatedness of conditions, and exact phrasing of the rating narrative/cover letter to prove a reasonable claimant would *not* have understood the silent decision to deny the pending claim (*Ingram v. Nicholson*, 21 Vet. App. 232 (2007); *Cogburn v. McDonald*, 809 F.3d 1232 (Fed. Cir. 2016)).
* **AMA Decisions (On or After February 19, 2019) — *Hamill v. Collins*, 166 F.4th 1030 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 4, 2026) (`No. 24-1543`) & *Cash v. Collins*, No. 24-1811 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 5, 2026):**
  * ***Hamill*:** Landmark Federal Circuit decision holding that **the implicit denial doctrine is dead under the Appeals Modernization Act (AMA)** (arising specifically from an unaddressed AMA request to reopen a Character of Discharge determination—see [`Playbook 10: Character of Discharge, Insanity & Upgrades`](../playbooks/10-character-of-discharge-insanity-upgrades.md) and [`§ 3.12 COD Evolution`](../cfr-diffs/part-3/character-of-discharge-3.12-evolution.md)). Because Congress amended `38 U.S.C. § 5104(b)` to mandate explicit identification of issues adjudicated, evidence considered, favorable findings, and reasons for denial, **VA cannot deny an AMA-governed claim by silence**. Any claimed or reasonably raised issue omitted from an AMA decision **remains legally pending and unadjudicated**! *(Note: Within 1 year of a silent AMA decision, file an HLR or Board appeal rather than a writ of mandamus—Hamill, fn. 6).*
  * ***Cash*:** Under `38 U.S.C. § 7113(c)`, when a Board NOD (`VA Form 10182`) clearly identifies or incorporates probative evidence submitted during an AMA "in-between" window that is already in VA's possession, the Board must consider it without requiring physical resubmission.

## 4. Ancillary Retroactive Vehicles

### A. Mandatory Consideration of Special Monthly Compensation (SMC) & Multi-Step Bumps (*Barry*)
Under `38 U.S.C. § 1114`, entitlement to statutory SMC—such as `SMC(k)` (anatomical loss or loss of use of a creative organ, foot, hand, etc.), `SMC(l)` (Aid and Attendance), and `SMC(s)` (statutory housebound: a single 100% rating—including TDIU premised on a single disability under *Bradley v. Peake*, 22 Vet. App. 280 (2008)—plus additional independent disabilities rated at 60% or more)—**does not require a separate claim**.
* **Mandatory Adjudication (*Akles* & *Buie*):** Under *Akles v. Derwinski*, 1 Vet. App. 118 (1991) and *Buie v. Shinseki*, 24 Vet. App. 242 (2010), VA is **obligated to adjudicate SMC whenever the medical evidence or rating percentages of record reasonably raise or establish statutory criteria**. Failure to award statutory `SMC(s)` when the combined rating code sheet itself showed a schedular `100%` + `60%` independent disabilities is a textbook, mathematical **RO CUE** (`38 C.F.R. § 3.105(a)`).
* **Multi-Step Intermediate SMC Bumps (*Barry v. McDonough*, 101 F.4th 1348 (Fed. Cir. May 16, 2024)):** Overturned VA's longstanding single-step restriction on `38 C.F.R. § 3.350(f)(3)–(4)`, holding that veterans receiving base `SMC(l)–(n)` are entitled to **multiple** half-step (`§ 3.350(f)(3)`) and full-step (`§ 3.350(f)(4)`) intermediate rate increases (up to the `SMC(o)` cap) for each separate, independent `50%` or `100%` disability!
* ***George v. McDonough* (`38 C.F.R. § 20.1403(e)`) CUE Caution:** Attacking a final **pre-2008** decision via CUE for failing to treat single-disability TDIU as the "total" rating under *Bradley*, or attacking a final **pre-May 2024** decision via CUE for applying a single-step cap prior to *Barry*, faces a substantial defense that *Bradley* and *Barry* changed the prevailing legal interpretation. Always look first for an **open/pending claim, `§ 3.156(b)` *Lang* non-finality, or `§ 3.156(c)` reconsideration** where *Bradley* and *Barry* apply retroactively across the entire open period without any CUE bar!

### B. Cross-System Multiplier (Title 10 DoD, Not Title 38 VA): CRSC & *Soto v. United States* (2025)
> [!NOTE]
> **Jurisdictional Boundary:** CRSC is military retired pay governed by **Title 10 (`10 U.S.C. § 1413a`)** and administered by the military branches/DFAS—**not** a Title 38 VA effective-date exception.

For military retirees (20-year or Chapter 61 medical retirees) whose retirement pay was offset by VA compensation and who qualify for CRSC under `10 U.S.C. § 1413a`:
* Historically, the military branches capped retroactive CRSC payouts at **six years** prior to the CRSC application under the Barring Act (`31 U.S.C. § 3702(b)`).
* In ***Soto v. United States*, 605 U.S. 360 (2025)** (decided June 12, 2025, 9–0), the U.S. Supreme Court held that `10 U.S.C. § 1413a` confers independent claim-settlement authority that **displaces the Barring Act's six-year statute of limitations**.
* **Implementation & Forum Split:** On **August 20, 2025**, DoD issued implementation guidance directing military departments and DFAS to audit previously 6-year-capped CRSC awards; simultaneously, retirees with unpaid pre-6-year arrears exceeding **\$10,000** have pursued Tucker Act enforcement in the U.S. Court of Federal Claims (e.g., *Christopher Carey v. United States*). Screen every Chapter 61 or 20-year retiree after any retroactive VA combat-related win!
