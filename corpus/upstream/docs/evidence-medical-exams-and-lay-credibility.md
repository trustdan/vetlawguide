# Duty to Assist, Medical Nexus Adequacy, Lay Credibility & Benefit of the Doubt Treatise (`38 U.S.C. §§ 5103A, 5107(b)`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.102, 3.159`)

More than `70%` of Joint Motions for Partial Remand (`JMPRs`) at the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims (`CAVC`) and merits reversals/remands at the Board of Veterans' Appeals (`BVA`) turn on four interconnected evidentiary doctrines: **(1) VA's statutory duty to assist in obtaining records and medical nexus opinions (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.159`)**, **(2) demolishing inadequate negative Compensation & Pension (`C&P`) opinions (*Nieves-Rodriguez*, *Stefl*, *Dalton*, *Barr*)**, **(3) protecting lay competence and credibility against Service Treatment Record (`STR`) silence (*Jandreau*, *Buchanan*, *AZ*, *Kahana*)**, and **(4) enforcing the `38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)` benefit-of-the-doubt equipoise rule after *Lynch* and *Bufkin v. Collins*, `604 U.S. 369 (2025)`**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Single Source of Truth & Cross-References:** Keep doctrinal synthesis distinct from verbatim regulatory text and lane mechanics. For verbatim primary-source text of `38 C.F.R. § 3.159`, consult [`38-cfr-3.159-17.1002-17.4000-21.282-evidence-emergency-pcafc-vre.md`](../authorities/part-3/38-cfr-3.159-17.1002-17.4000-21.282-evidence-emergency-pcafc-vre.md). For tactical selection among AMA Higher-Level Review, Supplemental Claim, and Board dockets, see the [`AMA Three-Lane Strategy & BVA Litigation Manual`](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md). For substantive direct, chronicity (`§ 3.303(b)`), and soundness/aggravation (`§§ 1111, 1153`) elements, consult [`Substantive Service Connection, Soundness & Aggravation`](service-connection-direct-soundness-aggravation.md), [Playbook 14 (Secondary SC & Obesity)](../playbooks/14-secondary-service-connection-aggravation-obesity.md), [Playbook 15 (Gulf War, PACT & `§ 1168` TERA)](../playbooks/15-gulf-war-mucmi-burn-pits-pact-tera.md), and [Playbook 16 (PTSD Stressors & MST)](../playbooks/16-ptsd-stressors-and-mst-corroboration.md).

## 1. Bitemporal Gate: Open-Claim Power vs. The *Cook* & *George* CUE Bars

Before pleading any evidentiary or examination defect, counsel must verify the **procedural posture** of the target decision ([`Procedural Vehicles for Piercing Finality`](procedural-vehicles-piercing-finality.md)):

| Procedural Posture | Applicability of `§ 5103A` / Exam Adequacy / Lay Weighing / `§ 5107(b)` | Governing Authority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Open, Pending, Timely-Appealed (`AMA` / Legacy), `§ 3.156(b)` *Lang* Abated, or `§ 3.156(c)` Reconsidered Claim** | **100% OPERATIVE:** Full enforcement of *McLendon* exam triggers, *Nieves-Rodriguez*/*Barr* exam adequacy, *Buchanan*/*Kahana* lay-evidence rules, and `§ 5107(b)` equipoise across the entire open retroactive period. | `38 U.S.C. §§ 5103A, 5107(b)`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.156(b)–(c), 3.159, 3.2500`; *Lang v. Wilkie*, `971 F.3d 1348 (Fed. Cir. 2020)` |
| **Collateral `CUE` Attack on a Final RO (`§ 5109A`) or Board (`§ 7111`) Decision — Duty to Assist / Inadequate Exam** | **CATEGORICALLY BARRED:** Failure to obtain federal/private records, schedule a C&P exam, or cure a glaringly inadequate C&P opinion yields an **incomplete record**, never an incorrect application of law to a complete record. | ***Cook v. Principi*, `318 F.3d 1334, 1344–47 (Fed. Cir. 2002) (en banc)`**; *Caffrey v. Brown*, `6 Vet. App. 377, 384 (1994)`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.105(a)(1)(iii), (vi), 20.1403(d)(2)` |
| **Collateral `CUE` Attack — Weighing Conflicting Opinions, Credibility, or `§ 5107(b)` Equipoise** | **CATEGORICALLY BARRED** *(with one narrow *Russell* exception)*: Disagreement over how an adjudicator weighed competing medical opinions, assessed lay credibility, or declined to find `§ 5107(b)` equipoise can **never** constitute CUE—*unless* **all** material evidence in the contemporaneous record was uncontradicted in establishing every statutory criterion and the adjudicator completely overlooked it. | `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.105(a)(1)(vi), 20.1403(d)(3)`; *Russell v. Principi*, `3 Vet. App. 310, 313–14 (1992) (en banc)` |
| **Collateral `CUE` Attack Based on Later Judicial Reinterpretation** | **CATEGORICALLY BARRED:** Later judicial invalidation or reinterpretation of a statute or regulation does not retroactively create CUE in a prior final decision. | ***George v. McDonough*, `596 U.S. 740 (2022)`**; `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.105(a)(1)(iv), 20.1403(e)` |

> [!TIP]
> **Historical Statutory Caution (`1988–2000` "Well-Grounded Claim" Era):** Between the Veterans' Judicial Review Act (`Pub. L. 100-687`, Nov. 18, 1988) and the enactment of the **Veterans Claims Assistance Act of 2000 (`VCAA`, `Pub. L. 106-475`, `114 Stat. 2096`, Nov. 9, 2000)**, `former 38 U.S.C. § 5107(a)` (`former § 3007(a)`) conditioned VA's duty to assist on the claimant first submitting a **"well-grounded claim"** (*Caluza v. Brown*, `7 Vet. App. 498 (1995)`). On November 9, 2000, Congress repealed the well-grounded gate (`Pub. L. 106-475, § 4`) and enacted **`38 U.S.C. § 5103A`**, implemented at **`66 FR 45620` (`RIN 2900-AK69`, published Aug. 29, 2001, retroactively effective November 9, 2000)** ([`Three-Era Title 38 Statutory Crosswalk`](title-38-statutory-crosswalk.md); [`1988–2000 Era Lens`](../eras/1988-2000-vjra-revolution.md)).

## 2. Duty to Assist in Obtaining Records (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(b)–(c)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.159`)

Once a claimant submits a substantially complete application (`38 C.F.R. § 3.159(a)(3)`), VA must make **"reasonable efforts"** to assist in obtaining relevant records unless **"no reasonable possibility exists that such assistance would aid in substantiating the claim"** (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(a)(2)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.159(d)`—restricted to claims barred as a matter of law, such as lack of qualifying military service or an unwaivable statutory bar, or inherently incredible claims).

### A. Federal Agency Records vs. Private Records (`§ 3.159(c)(1)–(3)`)

VA's statutory burden differs sharply depending on whether the custodian is a **federal department/agency** or a **non-federal/private entity**:

| Feature | Federal Agency Records (`§ 5103A(c)`; `§ 3.159(c)(2)–(3)`) | Private / State / Local Records (`§ 5103A(b)`; `§ 3.159(c)(1)`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Covered Custodians** | Service Treatment Records (`STRs`), military personnel/unit archives (`NPRC`, `JSRRC`/`CRUR`), `VAMC` & **Vet Center** clinical charts, **Social Security Administration (`SSA`)** disability files (both the `ALJ` decision *and* underlying consultative/treating medical exhibits!), `OWCP`, `OSHA`, federal prisons. | Private physicians/hospitals, civilian employers (`VA Form 21-4192` unemployability verification), state workers' compensation, local police/EMS reports. |
| **Claimant Identification & Release** | Claimant need only provide enough information to identify and locate existing records (`§ 3.159(c)(2)`); no HIPAA release required for `STRs`/`VAMC` (though Vet Center readjustment counseling notes require specific veteran release). | Claimant must adequately identify provider, approximate dates, and conditions treated, **and** sign a valid medical release (`VA Form 21-4142` / `21-4142a`) where required by custodian. |
| **Exhaustion Standard** | **HEIGHTENED CONTINUING DUTY:** VA **must continue efforts** to obtain identified relevant federal records ***"unless it is reasonably certain that such records do not exist or that further efforts to obtain those records would be futile"*** (`§ 5103A(c)(2)`; `§ 3.159(c)(2)`). | **FINITE TWO-ATTEMPT DUTY:** VA must make **an initial request and, if not received, at least one follow-up request (`>= 2` reasonable attempts)** unless a response establishes the records do not exist or a follow-up would be futile (`§ 3.159(c)(1)`). |
| **Mandatory Unavailability Notice (`§ 3.159(e)(1)`)** | VA must notify the claimant orally or in writing of `(i)` the identity of unavailable records, `(ii)` efforts made, `(iii)` summary of actions VA will take, and `(iv)` the claimant's right to submit alternative evidence. *(For 1973 NPRC fire / lost STRs, triggers heightened obligation to assist with buddy statements, `NA Form 13055` extracts, and `BVA` heightened reasons-or-bases scrutiny — *Moore*; *Washington v. Nicholson*, `19 Vet. App. 362 (2005)`).* | Same mandatory `§ 3.159(e)(1)` notice—once VA completes `>= 2` reasonable attempts and issues proper unavailability notice, ultimate responsibility to obtain private records shifts to the claimant. |

* **Controlling Federal-Record Precedents (*Golz* & *Moore*):**
  * ***Golz v. Shinseki*, `590 F.3d 1317, 1321–23 (Fed. Cir. 2010)`:** Clarified that VA's duty to obtain `SSA` disability records attaches whenever there is a **"reasonable possibility that the records may be relevant"** to any contested element (diagnosis, nexus, continuity, staging, or `TDIU`), though VA need not fetch `SSA` files affirmatively shown on their face to concern solely unrelated non-claimed conditions.
  * ***Moore v. Shinseki*, `555 F.3d 1369, 1373–75 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`:** Held VA violated `§ 5103A(c)` by failing to obtain **inpatient military psychiatric hospitalization charts** explicitly referenced in outpatient STRs; the Board could not dismiss the missing clinical charts as "irrelevant" without ever seeing them! *(Note: If missing military inpatient or unit records are later associated after a final denial, immediately invoke **`38 C.F.R. § 3.156(c)` mandatory reconsideration** — [Playbook 03](../playbooks/03-service-department-records-38-cfr-3-156c.md)!)*

### B. AMA Temporal & Lane Boundaries (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(e)(2)`)

Under the Appeals Modernization Act (`Pub. L. 115-55`, eff. Feb. 19, 2019; `84 FR 138, 169`), **`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(e)(2)`** and **`38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c)`** draw a hard temporal boundary:
1. **Initial Claims (`21-526EZ`) & Supplemental Claims (`20-0995`):** Full `§ 5103A` duty to assist applies—including VA's affirmative obligation under **`38 C.F.R. § 3.2501(b)`** to gather adequately identified federal and private records to help the claimant satisfy the `"new and relevant"` readjudication threshold!
2. **Higher-Level Review (`20-0996`) & Board Appeals (`10182`):** Proactive `§ 5103A` record-gathering and exam ordering **cease upon issuance of the AOJ decision notice** (`§ 5103A(e)(2)`). **CRITICAL EXCEPTION — Pre-Decisional DTA Error Return (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(f)(2)(A)`; `38 C.F.R. §§ 3.2601(g), 20.802(a)`):** Whenever a Higher-Level Reviewer or Veterans Law Judge discovers that the AOJ **failed to fulfill its duty to assist *prior* to the appealed AOJ decision** (e.g., failed to order a *McLendon*-triggered exam, relied on an inadequate *Nieves-Rodriguez*/*Barr* exam, or ignored identified `SSA`/`VAMC` records) and cannot grant the maximum benefit outright, **the HLR reviewer or Board MUST remand/return the claim to the AOJ for full corrective development** ([`AMA Lanes & BVA Manual`](ama-lanes-and-bva-litigation-manual.md))!

## 3. Medical Examination & Nexus Trigger (`38 U.S.C. § 5103A(d)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c)(4)`)

### A. The Four-Element *McLendon* Test & *Waters* Low-Threshold Rule

Under `38 U.S.C. § 5103A(d)` and `38 C.F.R. § 3.159(c)(4)(i)`, VA **must** provide a medical examination or obtain a medical nexus opinion in a compensation claim whenever four cumulative elements are met (***McLendon v. Nicholson*, `20 Vet. App. 79, 81–86 (2006)`**):

1. **Element 1 — Current Disability or Persistent/Recurrent Symptoms (`§ 3.159(c)(4)(i)(A)`):** There is `(a)` competent medical evidence of a current disability, **OR** `(b)` **competent lay evidence of persistent or recurrent symptoms of disability** (meaning VA *cannot* refuse an exam merely because an uninsured veteran lacks a formal clinical diagnosis!).
2. **Element 2 — In-Service Event, Injury, Disease, or Presumptive Manifestation (`§ 3.159(c)(4)(i)(B)`):** Established by STRs, personnel records, `38 U.S.C. § 1154(b)` combat lay evidence, `§ 3.304(f)(5)` MST markers, `38 U.S.C. § 1168` Toxic Exposure Risk Activity (`TERA` concession), or credible lay/buddy statements (or, for secondary claims under `§ 3.310`, an established primary SC disability).
3. **Element 3 — Indication of Possible Association (`§ 3.159(c)(4)(i)(C)`):** The evidence **"indicates that the disability or persistent or recurrent symptoms of disability *may be associated* with the claimant's active military, naval, air, or space service"** (or aggravated by an SC disability).
4. **Element 4 — Insufficient Medical Evidence to Decide (`§ 5103A(d)(2)`):** The record does not already contain sufficient competent medical evidence to make a decision on the claim.

> [!CAUTION]
> **The Classic *Waters* Reversal/Remand Trap on *McLendon* Element 3:**
> In ***Waters v. Shinseki*, `601 F.3d 1274, 1277–79 (Fed. Cir. 2010)`**, the Federal Circuit affirmed that *McLendon* Element 3 (`"indicates... may be associated"`) establishes a **deliberately low threshold**. Adjudicators routinely commit reversible legal error by conflating the threshold for **ordering an exam** (`may be associated`) with the ultimate merits burden for **granting service connection** (`at least as likely as not`):
> * **Illegal Reasoning:** *"Because the veteran is a layperson incompetent to diagnose etiology and there is no medical nexus opinion in the record, no VA exam is warranted."*
> * **Correct Rule (*Waters* / *McLendon*):** Competent lay testimony describing post-service symptom continuity, a treating note mentioning historical military exposure, or temporal proximity suffices to satisfy Element 3—even if that lay statement alone would be insufficient to *win* the claim on the merits without an exam!
> * **Parallel `38 U.S.C. § 1168` TERA Exam Trigger:** For non-presumptive toxic-exposure claims (`Pub. L. 117-168, § 303`, eff. Aug. 10, 2022), `§ 1168(a)` independently mandates a VA exam + synergistic toxic-effect nexus opinion whenever the veteran participated in a `TERA` and has current disability/symptoms, unless `§ 1168(b)`'s narrow exception applies ([Playbook 15](../playbooks/15-gulf-war-mucmi-burn-pits-pact-tera.md)).

*(Note on Increased-Rating & Staged-Rating Claims: While `§ 5103A(d)` by text references establishing service connection, VA's general `§ 5103A(a)` duty to assist requires ordering a **contemporaneous disability evaluation exam** whenever credible lay or clinical evidence indicates worsening since the last rating exam or when the existing C&P report is too stale or incomplete to rate every functional criterion under the governing Diagnostic Code — *Snuffer v. Gober*, `10 Vet. App. 400, 403 (1997)`; *Hart v. Mansfield*, `21 Vet. App. 505, 508–09 (2007)`).*

## 4. Demolishing Inadequate Negative C&P Opinions (Six Scalpels + The *Francway* Waiver Trap)

When a contract (`VES`, `Optum/LHI`, `QTC`, `Leidos`) or VAMC examiner issues a negative `"less likely than not"` nexus opinion or checks under-evaluating `DBQ` boxes, appellate counsel attack the opinion's **legal adequacy** using six controlling precedents:

### A. The Six Adequacy Scalpels

1. ***Nieves-Rodriguez v. Peake*, `22 Vet. App. 295, 301–04 (2008)` — Informed Factual Premise + Cogent Medical Rationale Over "C-File Reviewed" Boilerplate:**
   * A medical opinion's probative weight flows from **(a) an accurate, factually informed premise** and **(b) fully articulated, reasoned clinical explanation** connecting medical principles to the veteran's facts—"neither a VA medical examination report nor a private medical opinion is entitled to any weight... if it contains only data and conclusions."
   * **Destroying the `"C-File Not Reviewed"` Excuse (`22 Vet. App. at 302–04`):** The Board **cannot** discount a private treating physician's or IME specialist's positive nexus opinion merely because the private doctor did not review the entire C-file, if the private doctor was otherwise informed of the relevant medical facts (via STRs, discharge summaries, or accurate patient history)—nor can the Board automatically prefer a cursory VA C&P opinion simply because the VA examiner checked `"C-file was reviewed."`
2. ***Stefl v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 120, 124–25 (2007)` — Conclusory Opinions & Unaddressed Theories:**
   * An opinion that merely recites medical history and leaps to `"less likely than not"` without explaining *why* the facts cut against causation—or that addresses only direct in-service onset while ignoring reasonably raised alternative theories (`§ 3.310(b)` secondary aggravation, `§ 3.303(b)` continuity, medication side effects)—is legally inadequate and cannot support a denial.
3. ***Monzingo v. Shinseki*, `26 Vet. App. 97, 105–06 (2012)` — The "General Medical Principles" Floor:**
   * While an examiner need not cite peer-reviewed medical treatises or epidemiological studies unless the medical question is unusually complex or novel, the examiner **must still articulate the general medical principles** supporting the conclusion. Turn *Monzingo* offensively against bare latency denials: an examiner who writes *"no nexus because 15 years elapsed between discharge and diagnosis"* without explaining any pathophysiological principle why a condition noted in service or bridged by lay symptoms could not progress subclinically fails even *Monzingo*'s baseline!
4. ***Dalton v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 23, 39 (2007)` & *McKinney v. McDonald*, `28 Vet. App. 15, 29–31 (2016)` — Ignoring Lay History or Treating STR Silence as Dispositive:**
   * **Single Most Common C&P Defect:** A VA examiner **cannot** ignore a veteran's or buddy's lay account of in-service injury and post-service symptoms, nor base a negative nexus opinion **solely on the absence of contemporaneous `STRs` or post-service treatment records**!
   * **Role Separation Rule:** Assessing lay **credibility** belongs exclusively to the **adjudicator (Board/AOJ)**, not the C&P clinician. Unless the Board first makes a legally valid finding that the veteran's lay history is *not credible* (cf. *Reonal v. Brown*, `5 Vet. App. 458, 460–61 (1993)`), an examiner who disregards reported continuity renders a fatally inadequate opinion (*Dalton*, `21 Vet. App. at 39`; *McKinney*, `28 Vet. App. at 30–31`).
5. ***Barr v. Nicholson*, `21 Vet. App. 303, 311 (2007)` — The Undertaken-Exam Adequacy Mandate:**
   * **Once VA undertakes to provide a medical examination or nexus opinion—even if VA was *not* legally obligated to provide one under `§ 5103A(d)`—VA MUST ensure that the examination and opinion are adequate!** VA cannot defend a defective C&P opinion at the Board or CAVC by arguing that *McLendon* or `§ 1168` did not require ordering the exam in the first place (`38 C.F.R. § 4.2` also independently requires returning inadequate evaluation reports!).

### B. CRITICAL WAIVER TRAP: *Francway v. Wilkie* (`2019` *En Banc*) Competency vs. Adequacy

> [!WARNING]
> ***Francway v. Wilkie*, `940 F.3d 1304, 1307–09 (Fed. Cir. 2019) (en banc)` — Challenge Examiner Competency BEFORE the Board or Lose It Forever:**
> Under the presumption of regularity, VA C&P examiners (including general-practice contract nurse practitioners and physician assistants opined on complex neurological, oncological, or orthopedic causation!) are **presumed competent** to render medical opinions **unless the claimant raises a specific objection to the examiner's competence/qualifications before the Board (or AOJ)**.
> 1. **Fatal CAVC Mistake:** Raising an examiner's lack of board certification or specialty training **for the first time at the CAVC** automatically fails as waived under *Francway*!
> 2. **Mandatory Board Pleading Protocol:** In every Board brief (`VA Form 10182` submission) where an unqualified generalist rendered an adverse opinion on a complex specialty question:
>    * Explicitly state: *"Claimant challenges the clinical competence and specialist qualifications of the [Date] C&P examiner under Francway v. Wilkie, 940 F.3d 1304 (Fed. Cir. 2019), and demands production of the examiner's curriculum vitae and specialty credentials,"* explaining **why** the medical issue (e.g., `AL` amyloidosis etiology, complex `TBI` vs. dementia differentiation, synergistic `TERA` toxicology) requires specialist expertise (`38 C.F.R. § 3.159(a)(1)`).
>    * **Always Pair With Adequacy (*Nieves-Rodriguez* / *Stefl* / *Dalton*):** Even where *Francway* was not raised below, **opinion adequacy (`inaccurate facts` / `missing rationale` / `ignoring lay history`) is NEVER presumed under regularity** and remains 100% reviewable at the CAVC!

## 5. Lay Evidence Competence, Credibility & The STR-Silence Shield (`38 C.F.R. § 3.159(a)(2)`)

Under `38 U.S.C. § 1154(a)` and `38 C.F.R. § 3.159(a)(2)`, **lay evidence** is any evidence not requiring specialized education, training, or experience, and is **competent** whenever offered by a person who has knowledge of facts or circumstances and conveys matters **observable and within the realm of common human experience**. Always separate **lay competence** (*a legal question of admissibility/capability*) from **lay credibility and probative weight** (*a factual assessment constrained by strict legal guardrails*):

### A. Three Pathways to Lay Competence (*Jandreau* & *Davidson*)

1. ***Jandreau v. Nicholson*, `492 F.3d 1372, 1376–77 & n.4 (Fed. Cir. 2007)`:** Swept aside early CAVC cases that categorically barred lay witnesses from establishing diagnoses or continuity, holding lay evidence is **competent** in three distinct scenarios:
   * **Pathway 1 (Observable Symptoms Supporting Later Medical Diagnosis):** Lay witness describes contemporaneous observable symptoms (`chronic joint pain`, `sciatic shooting pain`, `ringing in the ears`, `panic attacks`, `sleep apnea snoring/apneas` witnessed by a spouse, `syncopal spells`) that a physician later connects to a clinical diagnosis;
   * **Pathway 2 (Relaying a Contemporaneous Medical Diagnosis):** Lay witness testifies that a military medic or private physician diagnosed them with a specific condition at the time (`"the battalion surgeon told me in 1971 I had a torn meniscus"`); **OR**
   * **Pathway 3 (Simple, Lay-Identifiable Conditions):** The condition itself is non-complex and readily identifiable by lay observation (e.g., `varicose veins`, `flat feet` / *Falzone v. Brown*, `8 Vet. App. 398 (1995)`, `subjective tinnitus` / *Charles v. Principi*, `16 Vet. App. 370 (2002)`).
2. ***Davidson v. Shinseki*, `581 F.3d 1313, 1316 (Fed. Cir. 2009)`:** Confirmed that *Jandreau* applies with equal force to **etiological nexus and `§ 3.303(b)` continuity of symptomatology**—reversing the CAVC for categorically assuming a surviving spouse was incompetent to provide probative lay evidence linking her husband's wartime anxiety disorder to his death.

### B. Prohibiting Impeachment by STR Silence & Invented Medical Standards (*Buchanan*, *AZ*, *Kahana*, *Maxson*)

| Precedent | Core Doctrinal Holding & Litigation Scalpel |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***Buchanan v. Nicholson*, `451 F.3d 1331, 1336–37 (Fed. Cir. 2006)`** | **The Foundational STR-Silence Rule:** The Board **cannot** determine that lay evidence lacks credibility **merely because it is unaccompanied by contemporaneous medical or service records**! Treating the absence of medical records as a standalone basis to reject lay statements legally nullifies `38 U.S.C. § 1154(a)` and `§ 3.159(a)(2)`. |
| ***AZ v. Shinseki*, `731 F.3d 1303, 1315–18 (Fed. Cir. 2013)`** | **Evidentiary Foundation Requirement for Record Silence:** Applying common-law evidence rules (`Fed. R. Evid. 803(7)`), absence of an entry in military or medical records is probative of non-occurrence **ONLY IF** an affirmative foundation establishes that the event *would regularly and reliably have been recorded* had it occurred. Where institutional/cultural barriers discourage reporting (such as unreported Military Sexual Trauma in *AZ*, or stoic combat infantry culture discouraging sick-call visits), **record silence has ZERO probative value against occurrence** ([Playbook 16](../playbooks/16-ptsd-stressors-and-mst-corroboration.md))! |
| ***Kahana v. Shinseki*, `24 Vet. App. 428, 434–35 (2011)`** & ***Colvin v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 171, 175 (1991)`** | **Bar on Board-Invented Medical Assumptions:** In *Kahana*, the Board discredited a veteran's account of an in-service right-knee `ACL` snap by asserting that a true `ACL` tear causes such severe acute pain that it *necessarily* requires immediate medical attention and would appear in `STRs`. The CAVC vacated: **without independent medical evidence in the record** establishing that every `ACL` injury forces immediate clinical care, the Board impermissibly substituted its own unsubstantiated medical judgment (*Colvin* error) to fabricate a *Buchanan* exception! |
| ***Maxson v. Gober*, `230 F.3d 1330, 1333 (Fed. Cir. 2000)`** & ***Pond v. West*, `12 Vet. App. 341, 346 (1999)`** | **Treatment Gaps & Permissible Credibility Factors:** While the Board may consider a prolonged unexplained gap in medical treatment *alongside other affirmative evidence*—such as separation physicals where the veteran affirmatively checked `"No"` to specific symptom questions, internal contradictions, facial implausibility, demeanor, or specific bias/interest (*Pond*; note that generic financial interest shared by *every* VA claimant cannot alone impeach testimony!)—the Board **must** also weigh **reasons for non-treatment** (`lack of health insurance`, `rural access`, `self-treatment with OTC analgesics/alcohol`, `avoidance symptoms of PTSD`). |

## 6. Benefit of the Doubt (`38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)`; `38 C.F.R. § 3.102`) After *Lynch* & *Bufkin v. Collins* (`2025`)

### A. Substantive Standard at the Agency & Board (*Gilbert*, *Ortiz*, *Lynch*)

Codified by the VJRA (`Pub. L. 100-687`, `1988`) from longstanding VA regulation (`38 C.F.R. § 3.102`), **`38 U.S.C. § 5107(b)`** commands: *"When there is an approximate balance of positive and negative evidence regarding any issue material to the determination of a matter, the Secretary shall give the benefit of the doubt to the claimant."*

1. ***Gilbert v. Derwinski*, `1 Vet. App. 49, 53–56 (1990)` ("Tie Goes to the Runner"):** Unlike civil litigation (where a `50/50` tie loses for failure to carry a preponderance burden), in Veterans Law **equipoise wins**—and applies independently to **every material factual or medical issue** (`COD/insanity` per *Newman v. McDonough*, `35 Vet. App. 310 (2022)`, `in-service injury`, `current diagnosis`, `nexus`, `degree of disability`, and `staged effective dates`).
2. ***Ortiz v. Principi*, `274 F.3d 1361, 1364–65 (Fed. Cir. 2001)`:** Benefit of the doubt does **not** apply if the Board permissibly determines that the **preponderance of the evidence weighs against** the claim.
3. ***Lynch v. McDonough*, `21 F.4th 776, 781–82 (Fed. Cir. 2021) (en banc)`:** Clarified *Ortiz* and rejected any requirement that positive evidence reach a heightened `"persuasive"` threshold on its own: **equipoise is NOT restricted to exact mathematical `50.0%–50.0%` parity**. Whenever positive and negative evidence are `"nearly equal"` or `"too close to call"`—i.e., **unless a *fair preponderance* of the evidence weighs *against* the claim**—`§ 5107(b)` mandates resolving the issue in the veteran's favor!

### B. Judicial Review After *Bufkin v. Collins*, `604 U.S. 369 (2025)` & Winning Appellate Strategy

In ***Bufkin v. Collins*, `604 U.S. 369 (2025)`** (`7–2`), the U.S. Supreme Court construed `38 U.S.C. § 7261(b)(1)` (directing the CAVC to *"take due account of the Secretary's application of section 5107(b)"*), holding that:
* `§ 7261(b)(1)` does **not** create a standalone *de novo* standard of judicial review over whether the evidence stood in `"approximate balance"`;
* Rather, the CAVC reviews the Board's underlying factual findings and **weighing of positive vs. negative evidence for clear error (`§ 7261(a)(4)`)**, while reviewing pure legal interpretations *de novo* (`§ 7261(a)(1)`).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Post-*Bufkin* Appellate Playbook — Attack the Scale's Inputs, Never Ask CAVC to Re-Weigh Valid Pan Contents:**
> After *Bufkin*, arguing in a CAVC brief that *"Dr. Smith's positive IME and the VA examiner's negative opinion were both adequate, so the Board clearly erred in finding Dr. Smith outweighed"* almost always fails under deferential clear-error review. Instead, **every winning `§ 5107(b)` appellate brief attacks the legal and procedural predicates that corrupted the Board's scale *before* weighing occurred**:
> 1. **Predicate Scalpel 1 — Demolish the Negative Pan (*Nieves-Rodriguez* / *Stefl* / *Dalton* / *Barr*):** Prove the negative VA C&P opinion rested on an inaccurate factual premise, ignored lay continuity, or lacked medical rationale. An **inadequate** C&P opinion carries **zero probative weight** as a matter of law—leaving the veteran's reasoned private IME or lay continuity unrefuted (compelling remand or, where record development is complete and only one permissible view of the evidence remains, *Deloach* reversal)!
> 2. **Predicate Scalpel 2 — Restore Illegally Excluded Positive Weight (*Buchanan* / *AZ* / *Kahana* / *Colvin*):** Prove the Board applied an **erroneous legal rule** (*reviewed de novo even after Bufkin!*) when it discounted competent lay testimony due to STR silence or lay-invented medical assumptions.
> 3. **Predicate Scalpel 3 — Enforce `38 U.S.C. § 7104(d)(1)` Reasons or Bases (*Gilbert* / *Caluza*):** Prove the Board completely omitted or mischaracterized favorable VAMC notes, buddy statements, or *Lynch*'s `"fair preponderance against"` standard—preventing the CAVC from exercising informed judicial review and mandating vacatur!
